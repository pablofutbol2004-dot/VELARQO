Kitchens are planned for months and put off for longer. The homeowner is waiting on a mortgage, a builder, or a partner who isn't sure. Your design and your price are still on their fridge.

Ringing round a year of old quotes rarely happens between fits. I do that part, in your name, and only pass you people who want you back out to measure up again.

What happens, start to finish.

  1. You send me your old quotes.

    Quotes from 3 months to 2 years old that never became a job. A spreadsheet or a job-system export is fine, messy too. Within 2 working days I tell you honestly whether they're worth chasing.

  2. You approve every word.

    You see the exact texts and the one email below, with your firm's name on them. Nothing goes out until you say yes.

  3. Surveys go in your diary.

    Homeowners who want to go ahead get a time from the slots you gave me. You go back out, measure up and requote as normal.

What your customers receive.

Short, plain, and from your firm. The words in brackets are filled in from your quote records. You can change any of it.

  • Three texts and one email at most, over about two weeks.
  • Sent 9am to 7pm, Monday to Saturday. Never on a Sunday.
  • It stops the moment someone replies, books or says STOP. Anyone who says no is never contacted again.
  • No discounts or made-up deadlines, unless you have a real offer you want to run.

The questions installers ask first.

"They're my leads. Why would I pay you for them?"

You wouldn't be paying for leads. I only touch quotes that are 3 months to 2 years old and weren't won: the ones nobody is chasing. I also keep a slice of your list back, uncontacted, so you can see what the follow-up added compared with doing nothing.

"Won't it annoy my customers?"

They asked you for a quote, and the messages are short and about that quote. One STOP and they never hear from you again. You approve every word first.

"How do I know it works?"

You don't yet, and neither do I on your quotes. Velarqo is new. That's why it starts small and you only pay for surveys that get booked.

"What does it cost?"

A fee for each booked survey, agreed on a call before anything starts and written into the terms. Nothing upfront.
