# Carousel QC Rubric and Failure Modes

## Scoring model

Score each category 0–5.

- **0** = broken / absent
- **1** = poor
- **2** = weak
- **3** = acceptable
- **4** = strong
- **5** = exceptional

Recommended shipping threshold:
- no category below **3**;
- overall average at least **3.7**;
- cover, sequence, hierarchy, and truth/evidence at least **4** when relevant.

---

# 1. Strategic fit

### Questions
- Is the target viewer obvious?
- Is the viewer change clear?
- Is the chosen archetype appropriate to the idea?
- Does the carousel have a reason to exist beyond “we need a post”?

### Reject when
- content could target anyone;
- format appears selected before the idea;
- value proposition is generic;
- CTA is unrelated to the content.

---

# 2. Cover

### Questions
- Can the category/topic be identified quickly?
- Is there a specific reason to swipe?
- Does one focal point dominate?
- Is the premise legible at feed size?
- Does the cover accurately represent the payoff?

### Reject when
- title needs a paragraph to make sense;
- visual is attractive but irrelevant;
- too many focal points;
- bait exceeds payoff;
- tiny decorative text is carrying essential meaning.

---

# 3. Slide 2 validation

### Questions
- Does slide 2 reward the first swipe?
- Does it establish stakes, proof, specificity, map, or story momentum?
- Would a viewer feel the cover was honest?

### Reject when
- slide 2 repeats the cover;
- slide 2 contains filler;
- slide 2 delays the actual point again.

---

# 4. Sequence progression

### Questions
- Does the carousel change state over time?
- Is there a clear logic connecting slides?
- Does each slide add something non-redundant?
- Is the payoff placed appropriately?

### Delete test
Remove each slide mentally.

If meaning, rhythm, proof, or payoff do not suffer, the slide is probably redundant.

### Reject when
- slides are merely different phrasings of the same idea;
- list ordering is arbitrary when sequence matters;
- the strongest point is buried after weak setup;
- conclusion feels abrupt.

---

# 5. Swipe motivation

### Questions
- Why should the viewer continue from each slide?
- Is continuation based on genuine value rather than cheap cliffhangers?
- Does the sequence use structural completion, contrast, proof, story, escalation, or visual novelty?

### Reject when
- every slide says “but wait…”;
- no slide creates unresolved value;
- the sequence can be consumed in random order when it is supposed to be narrative.

---

# 6. Local completeness

### Questions
- Is each slide understandable on its own enough to be useful?
- Does it contain one dominant idea?
- Is required context present?

### Reject when
- slide relies on unexplained pronouns or missing setup;
- headline is meaningless outside the immediately prior slide;
- slide is just connective tissue with no value.

### Exception
A deliberately cinematic/photo narrative may use transitional frames with lower standalone value. Judge against intent.

---

# 7. Information density

### Questions
- Is the density appropriate to the role?
- Could the slide be simplified without losing meaning?
- Is a dense slide genuinely reference-worthy?
- Is a sparse slide genuinely meaningful?

### Reject when
- text wall is caused by failure to edit;
- tiny type is needed to fit content;
- sparse slide is just empty motivational filler;
- multiple independent ideas are crushed into one panel.

---

# 8. Hierarchy and reading order

### Questions
- What is read first?
- What is read second?
- Is the relationship between elements obvious?
- Can the slide be scanned without searching?

### Reject when
- headline, image, labels, and icons all compete;
- important text is visually weaker than decoration;
- reading order depends on guessing;
- alignment creates awkward eye movement.

---

# 9. Typography

### Questions
- Is text readable at mobile size?
- Are line lengths comfortable?
- Are font roles consistent?
- Is emphasis purposeful?
- Is bold used selectively?

### Reject when
- too many type styles fight;
- decorative display font is used for body copy;
- all text is bold, eliminating hierarchy;
- line breaks create accidental ambiguity;
- microcopy carries the central idea.

---

# 10. Spacing and grid

### Questions
- Do related elements align?
- Are margins consistent?
- Is whitespace intentional?
- Is spacing based on a small repeated scale?

### Reject when
- elements look “almost aligned”;
- slide feels crowded despite modest content;
- random gaps create visual noise;
- edge margins change accidentally slide-to-slide.

---

# 11. Visual function

For each non-text element ask:

> What job does this element perform?

Valid jobs:
- evidence;
- demonstration;
- context;
- mood;
- symbol;
- navigation;
- grouping;
- hierarchy;
- continuity.

### Reject when
- icons are added only to fill space;
- stock imagery has no relation to the point;
- diagram adds complexity without explaining relationships;
- decorative effects make the slide harder to read.

---

# 12. Visual coherence

### Questions
- Are 3–6 visual invariants visible across the sequence?
- Does variation stay inside a recognizable system?
- Does the carousel feel like one artifact?

### Reject when
- every slide looks like a different template pack;
- brand colors/fonts change without reason;
- image treatment is inconsistent;
- layout changes feel random.

---

# 13. Visual rhythm

### Questions
- Does attention mode change when useful?
- Is there a healthy alternation of density/scale/mode?
- Are repeated layouts intentional?

### Reject when
- 10 slides use the exact same composition for non-reference content;
- variation is so aggressive that cohesion disappears;
- every slide is equally loud;
- there is no pacing relief.

---

# 14. Image curation

### Questions
- Are images sequenced intentionally?
- Is there scale/color/subject/energy rhythm?
- Is the strongest image placed strategically?
- Are adjacent crops sufficiently different?

### Reject when
- photo order appears random;
- 5 consecutive images have identical framing;
- weaker images dilute a strong visual set;
- text overlays destroy better compositions.

---

# 15. Evidence and truth

### Questions
- Are numbers/source claims actually supported?
- Are charts honest representations?
- Is correlation presented as causation?
- Is a creator heuristic being presented as established fact?

### Hard reject
- fabricated study;
- invented testimonial;
- invented performance result;
- manipulated screenshot presented as proof;
- unsupported causal claim stated as fact.

---

# 16. Specificity

### Questions
- Could this post have been made for 100 unrelated niches?
- Does it contain concrete examples?
- Does it use real audience language, situations, objects, or constraints?

### Reject when
- “provide value,” “be consistent,” “tell stories,” “use a strong hook” are presented without mechanism or example.

---

# 17. Save/share value

Not every carousel needs this, but if intended:

### Save value can come from
- checklist;
- reference;
- framework;
- template;
- decision rule;
- example library;
- implementation steps.

### Share value can come from
- identity resonance;
- clear articulation of a belief;
- surprising insight;
- humor;
- visual beauty;
- social usefulness.

### Reject when
The CTA says “save this” but there is nothing worth returning to.

---

# 18. CTA fit

### Questions
- Is the CTA the logical next step?
- Does it match viewer intent created by the post?
- Is the conversion ask proportional to trust?

### Reject when
- cold viewer receives a high-friction ask with no bridge;
- lead magnet keyword is unrelated;
- CTA appears on multiple slides and overwhelms the value;
- CTA is included only because a template has a CTA slot.

---

# 19. Originality / anti-template test

### Questions
- Does this output feel like a reskin of a recent carousel?
- Are sequence, hook, layout, and evidence choices appropriate to this idea specifically?
- Is there at least one content-specific visual idea?

### Reject when
- replacing title and image would turn it into any other post;
- same cover composition appears repeatedly without reason;
- AI aesthetic effects dominate topic-specific thinking.

---

# 20. Thumbnail/contact-sheet test

Generate a contact sheet and inspect the entire sequence at once.

Look for:
- flat rhythm;
- accidental density clusters;
- repeated slide silhouettes;
- color imbalance;
- weak closing slide;
- cover not sufficiently distinct;
- mid-carousel visual collapse.

A sequence can pass individual-slide QC and still fail this test.

# Common failure modes from the corpus + likely AI failure modes

## Failure 1 — Pretty slideshow
Beautiful independent slides with no progression.

**Fix:** define slide roles and sequence engine before rendering.

## Failure 2 — Template tyranny
Every idea forced into the same 8-slide structure.

**Fix:** archetype selection based on content objective.

## Failure 3 — Generic AI wisdom
Strong design wraps vague advice.

**Fix:** require mechanism, example, or evidence for every nontrivial teaching claim.

## Failure 4 — Fake framework
Obvious advice is renamed into a system to manufacture authority.

**Fix:** framework components must improve explanation or decision-making.

## Failure 5 — Hook/payoff mismatch
Cover is stronger than the actual content.

**Fix:** slide 2 validation + promise ledger.

## Failure 6 — Overdesigned educational slide
Decorative graphics reduce comprehension.

**Fix:** every visual must have a stated function.

## Failure 7 — Random creativity
Each slide uses different fonts, effects, colors, and imagery.

**Fix:** choose visual invariants first.

## Failure 8 — Dead repetition
Every slide uses the same layout and density.

**Fix:** rhythm plan.

## Failure 9 — Forced education
Image-first portfolio is ruined by unnecessary copy.

**Fix:** classify content mode before writing.

## Failure 10 — Dense because “saveable”
Unreadable slides justified as reference content.

**Fix:** chunk information across multiple cards or convert to diagram/table.

## Failure 11 — Sparse because “premium”
Minimal slide says almost nothing.

**Fix:** judge semantic value, not whitespace.

## Failure 12 — Screenshot isolation absolutism
Every slide is made fully self-contained, destroying narrative flow.

**Fix:** local completeness + global incompleteness.

## Failure 13 — Cliffhanger spam
Every slide withholds the answer.

**Fix:** deliver value continuously while preserving larger progression.

## Failure 14 — Fake scientific authority
Charts, brain imagery, percentages, and research language make claims feel more certain than sources allow.

**Fix:** claim ledger and evidence gate.

## Failure 15 — CTA infection
Every slide funnels toward a lead magnet.

**Fix:** CTA only at natural bridge; allow no-CTA outputs.

## Failure 16 — Visual mismatch
Image is semantically unrelated to copy.

**Fix:** assign image role and justify relevance.

## Failure 17 — Cover microcopy dependence
Essential message is hidden in tiny subtitle.

**Fix:** feed-size test.

## Failure 18 — “Minimal” center stack on every slide
Common AI pattern: centered heading + tiny body + icon.

**Fix:** vary layout according to information geometry.

## Failure 19 — Moodboard dumping
Example roundup shows references but no takeaway.

**Fix:** every example needs mechanism + lesson.

## Failure 20 — Weak ending
Carousel simply stops after the last list item.

**Fix:** add synthesis, callback, conclusion, application, or natural CTA.

# Fast automated gate

Before a carousel reaches human review, require:

```yaml
structural_gate:
  clear_viewer_change: true
  archetype_selected: true
  slide_roles_complete: true
  slide_2_validates_cover: true
  redundant_slide_count: 0
  sequence_has_payoff: true
visual_gate:
  visual_invariants_defined: true
  each_slide_has_focal_point: true
  each_visual_has_function: true
  feed_size_cover_pass: true
  contact_sheet_rhythm_pass: true
truth_gate:
  unsupported_claims: 0
  fabricated_proof: 0
originality_gate:
  recent_template_similarity: "below threshold"
```

Human review should then focus on taste, nuance, and whether the carousel genuinely feels worth consuming.
