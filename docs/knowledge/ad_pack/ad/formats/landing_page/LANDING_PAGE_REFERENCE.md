# LANDING_PAGE_REFERENCE.md

## 1. Purpose

`landing_page` owns page-specific conversion structure: traffic/message match, first-view orientation, page sequencing, form/action design, page-specific proof placement, mobile usability, and landing-page measurement. General copy, proof construction, offers, funnel state logic, and qualification rules remain canonical elsewhere.

## 2. Core model

**traffic context → first-view orientation → information/proof sequence → clear primary action → confirmed next state → measurement and iteration**

## 4. Canonical rules

### Page Role And Scope

- **Rule:** Define the landing page around one primary audience, offer context, and intended conversion action before choosing sections or layout. **Why:** A page cannot prioritize information coherently when it is simultaneously trying to serve unrelated visitors, offers, and next actions. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:11:48] [C12K:hcPxMuxh5Tc@00:03:08]
- **Rule:** Treat a landing page as a focused decision environment rather than a general-purpose website page: every major element should help the intended visitor understand, trust, or take the primary next step. **Why:** General navigation and unrelated content compete with the page-specific decision the visitor arrived to make. **Source:** [C12K:PN2dcxR8pJg@00:04:39] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:10:27]
- **Rule:** Give a cold or unfamiliar visitor enough information to make the requested commitment instead of assuming they will convert from a headline and button alone. **Why:** Visitors who lack prior context usually need benefits, mechanism, evidence, and uncertainty reduction before a meaningful commitment is reasonable. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:09:20] [C12K:hcPxMuxh5Tc@00:03:59]
- **Rule:** Do not choose page length from a universal short-page or long-page rule; include the information required by offer complexity, visitor awareness, and decision risk, then remove what does not help the decision. **Why:** High-intent visitors may need little education while unfamiliar or high-risk offers can require much more explanation and proof. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:10:56] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:53:55]
- **Rule:** Build the page from actual customer problems, desired outcomes, objections, and decision criteria rather than filling a standard template with generic copy. **Why:** A structurally correct page still fails when its sections answer questions the intended visitor does not have. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:07:20] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:10:27]

### Traffic And Message Match

- **Rule:** Preserve message continuity from the acquisition source into the landing page: the promise, audience, offer, and expected next step should feel like the same conversation. **Why:** A visitor who clicks one promise and lands on a materially different message must re-orient and may infer bait-and-switch or irrelevance. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:10:27] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:10:56]
- **Rule:** Adapt page directness and education depth to the visitor’s awareness and intent instead of using the same page sequence for every traffic source. **Why:** Someone actively searching for a solution can often act earlier than someone interrupted by an ad who is only becoming aware of the problem. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:10:56] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:49:58]
- **Rule:** When materially different traffic sources imply different intent or problems, use distinct page variants or sections rather than forcing all traffic into one blended message. **Why:** Source-level differences can change what must be explained, proved, or requested before conversion. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:10:56] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:11:48]
- **Rule:** Do not send purpose-specific campaign traffic to a generic destination when a dedicated landing page can preserve the campaign’s context. **Why:** A generic homepage makes the visitor rediscover the relevant offer and weakens message match. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:10:27] [C12K:PN2dcxR8pJg@00:03:00]

### Above Fold And Orientation

- **Rule:** Use the first visible screen to answer the visitor’s immediate orientation questions: what this is, why it matters to them, and what they can do next. **Why:** The first view is the only page region every visitor encounters before deciding whether to continue. **Source:** [C12K:CNRiNz0YYlM@00:06:19] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:06:35]
- **Rule:** Make the primary headline communicate the visitor-relevant outcome or differentiator, not merely the company category or a vague slogan. **Why:** A category label explains what the business is but leaves the visitor to infer why they should care. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:06:08] [C12K:CNRiNz0YYlM@00:07:01]
- **Rule:** Use the subheadline to clarify, qualify, or explain the mechanism behind the headline when the headline alone cannot carry the needed context. **Why:** The headline should stay easy to grasp while the subheadline can answer the most important immediate follow-up question. **Source:** [C12K:zA0B-VwOPn4@00:09:50] [C12K:zA0B-VwOPn4@00:10:04]
- **Rule:** Choose a hero visual that demonstrates, previews, or reinforces the promised result or mechanism rather than functioning as unrelated decoration. **Why:** The first visual can strengthen comprehension and proof, or it can consume prime space without helping the decision. **Source:** [C12K:zA0B-VwOPn4@00:05:49] [C12K:hcPxMuxh5Tc@00:02:05]
- **Rule:** Place the primary action above the fold when the visitor is sufficiently informed and ready to act; do not force an early form when the traffic still needs education. **Why:** Action visibility helps high-intent visitors, but an immediate commitment can be premature for low-awareness traffic. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:11:26] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:49:58]
- **Rule:** Put the most relevant trust or risk-reduction cue near an early high-stakes action when that cue resolves a likely immediate doubt. **Why:** Evidence is more useful when it appears where the uncertainty becomes consequential rather than in a distant generic proof block. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:50:27] [C12K:rfdkMStaBW0@01:14:07]

### Page Sequence And Information

- **Rule:** Order sections according to the questions the visitor must resolve next, not according to a fixed universal landing-page template. **Why:** Problem-aware and solution-aware visitors need different sequencing even when the underlying offer is identical. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:52:56] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:53:55]
- **Rule:** Let section headlines carry meaningful benefits or conclusions so a visitor can understand the page by scanning the headings alone. **Why:** Visitors often scan before deciding what to read closely; generic labels such as “Our Services” waste that scan path. **Source:** [C12K:PcwuZJNJny4@00:09:17] [C12K:PcwuZJNJny4@00:10:03]
- **Rule:** Break dense explanation into visually digestible sections, bullets, comparisons, steps, or other structures that expose information hierarchy. **Why:** Scannable structure lets visitors find the evidence or explanation they need without processing one undifferentiated wall of copy. **Source:** [C12K:PcwuZJNJny4@00:09:38] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@01:01:01]
- **Rule:** Use a concise “how it works” sequence when process uncertainty is material, and end the sequence on the visitor’s desired result rather than on internal operational steps. **Why:** A simple process model lowers ambiguity while keeping attention on the outcome the process is meant to produce. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@01:01:46] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@01:02:15]
- **Rule:** Do not assemble unrelated value propositions, images, and generic sections merely to make the page look complete. **Why:** Random completeness increases length without building a coherent argument or helping the visitor progress toward a decision. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:10:27]
- **Rule:** Use the final conversion section to restate the desired outcome, clarify what happens after action, and present the primary next step instead of ending with a generic “Contact us.” **Why:** The bottom of the page is the last decision point for visitors who consumed the full argument; generic labels waste the accumulated context. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@01:03:57] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@01:04:26]

### Action And Form Design

- **Rule:** Keep one dominant call-to-action meaning across the page even when the same action is repeated in several locations. **Why:** Repeated access to one next step can reduce effort; competing primary actions fragment intent and make the page harder to interpret. **Source:** [C12K:hcPxMuxh5Tc@00:03:08] [C12K:hcPxMuxh5Tc@00:03:34]
- **Rule:** Judge CTA placement by completed downstream actions rather than by button clicks alone. **Why:** An earlier button can increase clicks while sending under-informed visitors into a form or checkout they do not complete. **Source:** [C12K:hcPxMuxh5Tc@00:03:59] [C12K:hcPxMuxh5Tc@00:04:26]
- **Rule:** Ask only for form fields that materially support fulfillment, routing, qualification, compliance, or follow-up; remove fields that create effort without changing what happens next. **Why:** Every unnecessary input adds abandonment risk, while some additional fields can be justified when they improve a real downstream decision. **Source:** [C12K:rfdkMStaBW0@00:09:08] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@01:04:54]
- **Rule:** Explain what the visitor will receive or what will happen after submitting a form when that next step is not obvious. **Why:** Uncertainty about the consequence of submission increases perceived risk and makes a generic form feel like an invitation to be sold to. **Source:** [C12K:rfdkMStaBW0@00:34:50] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:50:27]
- **Rule:** Use persistent, readable form labels—especially on mobile—instead of relying only on placeholder text that disappears during entry. **Why:** Visitors should not have to remember what a field meant after they begin typing. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@01:04:54]
- **Rule:** After conversion, confirm the completed action and make the next expected state obvious; the funnel pack owns what that next state should be. **Why:** A clear confirmation prevents uncertainty, duplicate submissions, and a broken handoff immediately after the page succeeds. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:12:41] [C12K:01cCzRY3iJM@00:08:01]

### Proof And Objection Placement

- **Rule:** Select proof using the `proof` pack, then place each proof element close to the claim, fear, or action it is meant to support. **Why:** Page-specific proof placement should resolve local uncertainty without recreating a generic testimonial dump. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:54:24] [C12K:rfdkMStaBW0@01:14:07]
- **Rule:** Move proof earlier for high-intent visitors when trust is the remaining barrier; move education earlier when understanding the problem or mechanism is the barrier. **Why:** The same sections can perform differently depending on what the visitor already believes before arriving. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:53:17] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:54:24]
- **Rule:** On mobile, expose high-value proof directly when it is important to the decision instead of hiding it behind low-discovery carousels or accordions. **Why:** Critical evidence that is technically present but rarely opened behaves as if it were absent. **Source:** [C12K:PcwuZJNJny4@00:08:48] [C12K:PcwuZJNJny4@00:09:05]
- **Rule:** Build FAQ content from recurring customer questions, objections, and observed hesitation rather than padding the page with generic questions. **Why:** A useful FAQ resolves real uncertainty at the point where visitors who reached the bottom may still hesitate. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@01:03:30] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:12:41]
- **Rule:** If the offer has a legitimate guarantee or risk reversal, decide its page prominence based on the doubt it resolves; do not invent one for page conversion. **Why:** Guarantee design belongs to `offer_framing`; the landing page should only communicate a real term where it is decision-relevant. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@01:03:10] [C12K:hcPxMuxh5Tc@00:05:12]
- **Rule:** For service businesses where the buyer is evaluating the people delivering the work, use team or founder identity as trust context when it materially reduces uncertainty. **Why:** Human identity can matter to service credibility in a way that is less relevant to many self-serve products. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@01:02:44]

### Visual Usability And Mobile

- **Rule:** Use visual hierarchy to make the intended reading order obvious: primary promise first, supporting context second, action and evidence where they become relevant. **Why:** A page can contain the right information and still fail if typography, spacing, or emphasis makes everything compete equally. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:51:46] [C12K:PcwuZJNJny4@00:09:38]
- **Rule:** Optimize the mobile page as its own layout rather than assuming the desktop hierarchy survives automatic stacking. **Why:** Reordering, hidden content, oversized spacing, and interaction patterns can materially change what mobile visitors notice and can access. **Source:** [C12K:PcwuZJNJny4@00:08:48] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@01:04:54]
- **Rule:** Keep the page visually credible and readable without adding animation, decoration, or brand effects that compete with comprehension and action. **Why:** Visual quality can support trust, but visual spectacle that obscures hierarchy adds cognitive work without decision value. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:08:00] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:08:29]
- **Rule:** Avoid excessive empty space or oversized decorative regions when they push decision-relevant information below the first view without adding clarity. **Why:** Prime screen space is scarce, especially on mobile; spacing should separate information, not bury it. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:50:36] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:51:05]
- **Rule:** Reduce technical and interaction delays that materially interrupt the conversion action, such as unnecessary page refreshes or laborious selection flows. **Why:** A visitor who has decided to act can still be lost when the interface inserts avoidable waiting or state changes. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@01:06:19] [C12K:rfdkMStaBW0@00:09:08]

### Measurement And Iteration

- **Rule:** Establish the page’s baseline conversion and downstream-quality metrics before interpreting a redesign or test. **Why:** Without a baseline and a defined outcome, “better landing page” becomes an aesthetic judgment rather than a measurable hypothesis. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:11:48] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@01:13:57]
- **Rule:** Inspect behavior from both converters and non-converters to identify hesitation, missed information, dead clicks, scroll patterns, and abandonment points. **Why:** Aggregate conversion rate shows that a problem exists; behavioral evidence helps localize why. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:12:16] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:12:41]
- **Rule:** Segment landing-page performance by traffic source and device before trusting a blended average. **Why:** A page can look healthy overall while failing for a high-value source or for mobile visitors. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@00:10:56] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@01:13:28]
- **Rule:** Prioritize high-leverage hypotheses—often the promise, first-view message, hero, proof, or action path—before polishing low-impact visual details. **Why:** Large decision variables usually have more room to move behavior than tiny cosmetic adjustments, especially early in optimization. **Source:** [C12K:CNRiNz0YYlM@00:05:44] [C12K:CNRiNz0YYlM@00:06:03]
- **Rule:** Use controlled A/B tests when traffic is sufficient for useful inference; with low traffic, prefer qualitative evidence and larger hypothesis-driven changes over significance theater. **Why:** Tiny-sample split tests can produce unstable winners and delay learning. **Source:** [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@01:12:00] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@01:12:29]
- **Rule:** Track qualified leads, purchases, revenue, or other downstream economics alongside landing-page conversion rate. **Why:** A local conversion lift can be harmful if it attracts lower-fit actions or reduces economic value. **Source:** [C12K:hcPxMuxh5Tc@00:03:59] [C12K:1AAcCET2Hiw@01:16:33]

## 5. Important tensions / resolutions

- **Short vs long pages:** length follows awareness, complexity, and risk; remove irrelevant material rather than pursuing a word-count ideology.
- **CTA visibility vs premature action:** high-intent visitors benefit from immediate access; low-awareness visitors may need education before a form.
- **Friction reduction vs qualification:** remove useless effort, but keep fields or steps that materially improve a downstream decision.
- **Design quality vs decoration:** credibility and hierarchy matter; visual spectacle is not a conversion mechanism by itself.
- **Local conversion vs business outcome:** a higher form rate is not automatically better if downstream lead quality or economics deteriorate.

## 7. Boundaries and cross-references

- **`copywriting` / `hooks`:** own headline and sentence craft. This pack owns what the first page view must accomplish and where the copy lives.
- **`visual_communication`:** owns hierarchy/readability principles. This pack owns their landing-page application.
- **`proof`:** owns claim → doubt → evidence selection. This pack owns page-specific placement and visibility.
- **`offer_framing`:** owns the promise, price, guarantees, and commercial terms. The page communicates the already-defined offer.
- **`funnel_mechanics`:** owns state transitions, handoffs, recovery, and downstream path design. This pack owns the landing-page step itself.
- **`qualification`:** owns what information determines fit. This pack owns form UX, not the criteria.
