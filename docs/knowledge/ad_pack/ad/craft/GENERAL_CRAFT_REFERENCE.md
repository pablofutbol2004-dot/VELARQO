## Canonical rules

### `attention` — Attention Craft

### Relevance And Orientation

- **Rule:** Make the audience understand the subject and why it matters before asking them to invest more attention. **Why:** Attention is easier to sustain when relevance is legible rather than implied. **Source:** [B:kallaway/02_hooks-and-initial-retention] [B:round_2/02_retention-hooks-pacing-and-story]
- **Rule:** Keep each segment centered on one active idea or question instead of making the audience decode several competing threads. **Why:** Lower cognitive load preserves comprehension and reduces attention leakage. **Source:** [B:round_2/01_editing-philosophy-attention-emotion-and-clarity] [B:round_2/03_visual-layout-typography-motion-and-b-roll]
- **Rule:** Make the audience the reference point for relevance by connecting information to a recognizable problem, desire, consequence, or identity. **Why:** People allocate more attention to information that maps onto their own situation. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:round_4/01_hooks-attention-and-open-loops]

### Question Management

- **Rule:** Maintain a clear unanswered question when continued attention is needed, but keep the subject and stakes explicit. **Why:** A specific knowledge gap creates forward pull without becoming vague withholding. **Source:** [B:kallaway/02_hooks-and-initial-retention] [B:round_4/01_hooks-attention-and-open-loops]
- **Rule:** After resolving one meaningful question, either end or open the next relevant question instead of letting interest decay into filler. **Why:** Attention often drops after payoff unless the audience has a legitimate new reason to continue. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:round_2/02_retention-hooks-pacing-and-story]
- **Rule:** Give partial progress while an important question remains open; do not withhold all useful information until the end. **Why:** Progress proves the promise is real and prevents curiosity from feeling manipulative. **Source:** [B:round_2/02_retention-hooks-pacing-and-story] [B:round_4/01_hooks-attention-and-open-loops]
- **Rule:** Close every major curiosity debt with a real payoff. **Why:** Unpaid loops may win temporary attention but erode trust and future willingness to continue. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:round_4/09_round-4-action-playbooks-and-tensions]

### Pattern And Change

- **Rule:** Use change when attention needs renewal, but make the change carry meaning rather than stimulation alone. **Why:** Motivated novelty can refresh attention while gratuitous novelty competes with comprehension. **Source:** [B:round_2/01_editing-philosophy-attention-emotion-and-clarity] [B:round_2/03_visual-layout-typography-motion-and-b-roll]
- **Rule:** Vary intensity instead of sustaining maximum stimulation continuously. **Why:** Contrast between tension, release, motion, stillness, sound, and silence makes peaks perceptible. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting] [B:round_2/04_sound-design-music-and-silence]

### Cognitive Load

- **Rule:** Reduce search cost by keeping the audience’s eye and mind oriented to the next important element. **Why:** Attention is lost when people must hunt for the focal point or reconstruct the argument. **Source:** [B:round_2/03_visual-layout-typography-motion-and-b-roll] [B:round_2/01_editing-philosophy-attention-emotion-and-clarity]
- **Rule:** Use visible structure when a sequence contains multiple parts. **Why:** Lists, stages, and frameworks reduce uncertainty and help the audience track progress. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior]
- **Rule:** Simplify before adding stimulation when attention is weak. **Why:** Confusion cannot reliably be repaired by more motion, effects, or rhetorical intensity. **Source:** [B:round_2/01_editing-philosophy-attention-emotion-and-clarity] [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior]

### `content_measurement` — Content Measurement Craft

### Objective Metrics

- **Rule:** Define the business or communication objective before choosing success metrics. **Why:** A metric is only meaningful relative to the behavior the content was designed to produce. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:kallaway/06_content-strategy-growth-algorithms-and-monetization]
- **Rule:** Separate attention outcomes from follower or relationship outcomes and from conversion outcomes. **Why:** Views, follows, leads, and revenue measure different stages and can move independently. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:round_4/07_growth-testing-publishing-and-distribution]
- **Rule:** Judge reach-oriented content with attention and sharing signals, and judge commercial content with qualified actions and economic outcomes. **Why:** A high-view asset can be a poor commercial result and a low-view asset can be commercially strong. **Source:** [B:kallaway/06_content-strategy-growth-algorithms-and-monetization] [B:round_3/05_creator-ugc-performance-and-scaling]
- **Rule:** Track conversion quality per unit of exposure when the goal is business value, not only total conversions. **Why:** Rates such as qualified leads or revenue per view help compare differently sized pieces. **Source:** [B:kallaway/06_content-strategy-growth-algorithms-and-monetization] [B:round_4/04_expert-brand-positioning-and-monetization]

### Testing

- **Rule:** Change one major variable at a time when the purpose of a test is causal learning. **Why:** If topic, hook, format, proof, length, and CTA all change, the result is hard to diagnose. **Source:** [B:round_4/07_growth-testing-publishing-and-distribution]
- **Rule:** Test variables such as topic, hook, visual framing, format, length, proof, and CTA as separate hypotheses. **Why:** A structured variable map turns output volume into learnable experiments. **Source:** [B:round_4/07_growth-testing-publishing-and-distribution] [B:round_3/05_creator-ugc-performance-and-scaling]
- **Rule:** Produce small controlled batches before scaling a creative combination. **Why:** Small batches create enough evidence to compare without committing excessive resources. **Source:** [B:round_3/05_creator-ugc-performance-and-scaling] [B:kallaway/06_content-strategy-growth-algorithms-and-monetization]
- **Rule:** Keep an exploration stream for new ideas while continuing to exploit repeatable winners. **Why:** A factory without experimentation decays, while experimentation without reuse wastes proven learning. **Source:** [B:kallaway/06_content-strategy-growth-algorithms-and-monetization]

### Diagnosis

- **Rule:** Compare a piece with the creator or account’s own baseline rather than relying only on raw view counts across unequal accounts. **Why:** Relative outliers expose unusually strong ideas more reliably than absolute scale alone. **Source:** [B:round_3/03_content-research-reverse-engineering-and-validation] [B:kallaway/05_ideas-research-validation-and-substance]
- **Rule:** Inspect performance at exact drop points or weak stages and map them back to the corresponding line, shot, claim, or transition. **Why:** Diagnosis improves when failure is localized to a concrete creative decision. **Source:** [B:round_2/02_retention-hooks-pacing-and-story]
- **Rule:** Tag each asset with the variables needed to explain performance before publishing. **Why:** Volume without metadata becomes noise because winners cannot be decomposed. **Source:** [B:round_3/05_creator-ugc-performance-and-scaling]
- **Rule:** Record audience questions, comments, objections, and repeated wording as qualitative evidence alongside quantitative metrics. **Why:** Behavioral data explains what happened; audience language can help explain why. **Source:** [B:round_3/03_content-research-reverse-engineering-and-validation] [B:round_4/04_expert-brand-positioning-and-monetization]

### Validation

- **Rule:** Use a validation ladder: external evidence, internal attention, audience response, business response, then repeatability. **Why:** One successful reference or one successful post is weaker evidence than repeated performance through the full objective chain. **Source:** [B:round_3/03_content-research-reverse-engineering-and-validation]
- **Rule:** Promote a format or concept into a major template only after variations continue to work. **Why:** Repeatability separates a durable mechanism from a one-off outlier. **Source:** [B:round_3/03_content-research-reverse-engineering-and-validation] [B:round_3/05_creator-ugc-performance-and-scaling]
- **Rule:** Distinguish content strength, distribution strength, and conversion strength when reviewing a result. **Why:** A weak outcome can come from a good piece with poor reach, strong reach with weak conversion, or weak content itself. **Source:** [B:round_3/07_distribution-networks-clipping-and-audience-growth]
- **Rule:** Study patterns across several adjacent sources or creators before treating one isolated hit as a general rule. **Why:** Cross-source recurrence is stronger evidence of demand than a single exceptional post. **Source:** [B:round_3/03_content-research-reverse-engineering-and-validation]
- **Rule:** Treat historic winners as time-sensitive evidence and keep research current enough to detect saturation or decay. **Why:** Topics, formats, audience taste, and platform conditions change over time. **Source:** [B:round_3/03_content-research-reverse-engineering-and-validation]
- **Rule:** Verify attribution before scaling a supposedly high-performing format or creator. **Why:** Correlation with sales, comments, or professional appearance does not prove causal contribution. **Source:** [B:round_3/05_creator-ugc-performance-and-scaling]

### Platform Caution

- **Rule:** Treat exact algorithm mechanics, sample sizes, benchmark thresholds, posting-time rules, hashtag weights, and feature-specific tactics as re-check items. **Why:** Platform systems are dynamic, partially opaque, and represented in the corpus largely through creator claims. **Source:** [B:01_KNOWLEDGE_CORE/CLAIMS_CAVEATS_LIMITS_AND_TENSIONS] [B:round_4/07_growth-testing-publishing-and-distribution]
- **Rule:** Treat watch time and retention as important signals rather than the complete explanation of recommendation performance. **Why:** The corpus explicitly flags “algorithms only care about hook rate and watch time” as an oversimplification. **Source:** [B:round_4/07_growth-testing-publishing-and-distribution]

### `content_quality` — Content Quality Craft

### Substance And Distinction

- **Rule:** Make the substance work before relying on editing, aesthetics, or production polish. **Why:** Presentation can amplify quality but cannot replace a weak idea or empty claim. **Source:** [B:round_2/01_editing-philosophy-attention-emotion-and-clarity] [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]
- **Rule:** Reject or research further any piece whose core points are generic, obvious, or unsupported. **Why:** Polishing undifferentiated substance produces forgettable content more efficiently, not better content. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting] [B:kallaway/05_ideas-research-validation-and-substance]
- **Rule:** Do not present copied wording, scripts, coined frameworks without credit, or distinctive visual concepts frame-for-frame as original work. **Why:** Learning from references is legitimate; disguising another creator’s distinctive expression as your own is not. **Source:** [B:round_3/03_content-research-reverse-engineering-and-validation]
- **Rule:** Use AI for categorization, variation, synthesis, and repetitive work while retaining human responsibility for selection, facts, taste, experience, and originality. **Why:** Automation is strongest on repeatable tasks and weakest where judgment and authentic evidence matter. **Source:** [B:round_3/03_content-research-reverse-engineering-and-validation] [B:kallaway/07_ai-enabled-content-system-and-operating-workflow]

### Trust And Integrity

- **Rule:** Do not fabricate facts, personal stories, science, or results to make the content more dramatic. **Why:** False substance destroys the trust that content is supposed to compound. **Source:** [B:round_4/01_hooks-attention-and-open-loops] [B:round_4/05_scriptwriting-compression-personalization-and-power-words]
- **Rule:** Label uncertain claims as opinion, hypothesis, or source-derived belief instead of presenting confidence as proof. **Why:** Strong delivery should not blur the difference between evidence and assertion. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting] [B:01_KNOWLEDGE_CORE/CLAIMS_CAVEATS_LIMITS_AND_TENSIONS]
- **Rule:** Verify scientific, legal, medical, financial, and current platform claims before teaching them as settled facts. **Why:** The corpus contains creator interpretations and date-sensitive claims that may be incomplete or outdated. **Source:** [B:01_KNOWLEDGE_CORE/CLAIMS_CAVEATS_LIMITS_AND_TENSIONS]
- **Rule:** Attack the error, method, or rejected default rather than degrading the audience. **Why:** Negative framing can clarify contrast without sacrificing authority or respect. **Source:** [B:round_4/01_hooks-attention-and-open-loops] [B:round_4/09_round-4-action-playbooks-and-tensions]

### Audience Value

- **Rule:** Ensure self-referential content still gives the audience instruction, proof, access, identification, entertainment, or a realistic example. **Why:** Status display alone is weak audience value. **Source:** [B:round_3/02_founder-led-content-and-personal-brand]
- **Rule:** Make the content useful to the intended audience even if that reduces total relevance to everyone else. **Why:** On-target value can outperform broad virality for trust and commercial outcomes. **Source:** [B:kallaway/06_content-strategy-growth-algorithms-and-monetization]
- **Rule:** Use examples, metaphors, visuals, and explanations that make the audience feel capable of understanding rather than inferior for not knowing. **Why:** Comprehension supports likability, trust, and application. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior]

### Production Standard

- **Rule:** Choose the lowest production level that preserves accuracy, clarity, trust, and the intended emotional effect. **Why:** More production is not inherently more valuable and can reduce speed or native fit. **Source:** [B:round_2/08_systems-presets-learning-and-production-efficiency] [B:round_3/04_brand-storytelling-formats-and-creative-systems]
- **Rule:** Use higher production when the asset is evergreen, proof-heavy, visually dependent, emotionally complex, or strategically high-stakes. **Why:** Some messages benefit from control and durability more than speed. **Source:** [B:round_3/04_brand-storytelling-formats-and-creative-systems] [B:round_4/06_recording-delivery-and-visual-trust]
- **Rule:** Choose the highest sustainable cadence that still permits deliberate learning and reliable quality. **Why:** The sources disagree on posting frequency, but agree that blind volume without improvement is weak. **Source:** [B:round_2/08_systems-presets-learning-and-production-efficiency] [B:round_4/09_round-4-action-playbooks-and-tensions]
- **Rule:** Do not scale a format because of appearance, comments, one anecdotal result, or competitor usage alone. **Why:** Repeatable evidence matters more than social proof about the production style. **Source:** [B:round_3/05_creator-ugc-performance-and-scaling]

### `copywriting` — Copywriting Craft

### Clarity And Directness

- **Rule:** Write to one reader and use direct second-person language when it makes the application clearer. **Why:** Direct address increases perceived relevance and reduces abstract distance. **Source:** [B:round_4/05_scriptwriting-compression-personalization-and-power-words] [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior]
- **Rule:** Prefer short active sentences and concrete nouns and verbs over abstract, passive phrasing. **Why:** Concrete active language is faster to parse and easier to visualize. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting] [B:round_4/05_scriptwriting-compression-personalization-and-power-words]
- **Rule:** Explain necessary jargon in plain language or restate it with a simple metaphor. **Why:** The reader should not need specialist decoding to follow the argument. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]
- **Rule:** Give each sentence one primary job whenever possible. **Why:** Atomic messaging reduces comprehension failures caused by overloaded sentences. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]

### Compression

- **Rule:** Audit drafts by keeping tight high-value lines, rewriting useful but verbose lines, and deleting filler. **Why:** Compression raises information density without requiring the removal of necessary context. **Source:** [B:round_4/03_educational-value-and-the-trinity-of-value] [B:round_4/05_scriptwriting-compression-personalization-and-power-words]
- **Rule:** Remove transition phrases, throat-clearing, and repeated setup when the relationship between ideas remains clear without them. **Why:** Unnecessary connective language delays the useful claim. **Source:** [B:kallaway/02_hooks-and-initial-retention] [B:round_4/05_scriptwriting-compression-personalization-and-power-words]

### Specificity And Language

- **Rule:** Use truthful specificity in numbers, timeframes, settings, actions, and quoted instructions when specificity changes credibility or usefulness. **Why:** Specific language makes claims easier to test, picture, and apply. **Source:** [B:round_4/05_scriptwriting-compression-personalization-and-power-words]
- **Rule:** Prefer vivid, domain-native words that sharpen outcome, pain, mechanism, or identity over generic hype words. **Why:** Precise language carries more meaning than interchangeable superlatives. **Source:** [B:round_4/05_scriptwriting-compression-personalization-and-power-words]
- **Rule:** Use contrast words to mark real changes in logic, not as decorative drama. **Why:** Words such as “but” and “therefore” help the reader track causal movement when the underlying relationship is genuine. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting] [B:round_4/05_scriptwriting-compression-personalization-and-power-words]
- **Rule:** Name recurring concepts or frameworks only when the label improves memory or compression. **Why:** A useful term lets a repeated idea travel; unnecessary branding adds jargon. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]

### Persuasion And Objections

- **Rule:** Anticipate the strongest believable objection and answer it inside the flow rather than adding a defensive detour. **Why:** Objection handling works best when it feels like part of the reasoning. **Source:** [B:round_4/05_scriptwriting-compression-personalization-and-power-words]
- **Rule:** Anchor a new claim to an accepted truth or familiar experience when that bridge is accurate. **Why:** Shared premises reduce the inferential leap required to consider a new conclusion. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting] [B:kallaway/02_hooks-and-initial-retention]
- **Rule:** Use pain, loss, or comparison to clarify stakes without exaggerating harm or manufacturing panic. **Why:** Aggressive fear language can trigger skepticism and damage trust. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:round_4/01_hooks-attention-and-open-loops]
- **Rule:** Do not use unsupported percentages, invented autobiographical details, or false scientific certainty. **Why:** Persuasive force that depends on fabricated evidence or authority creates ethical and reputational risk. **Source:** [B:round_4/05_scriptwriting-compression-personalization-and-power-words] [B:round_4/01_hooks-attention-and-open-loops]

### Audience Language

- **Rule:** Collect recurring audience wording and reuse the underlying language patterns when they accurately describe the problem. **Why:** Audience-native phrasing can improve clarity and relevance without inventing terminology. **Source:** [B:round_3/03_content-research-reverse-engineering-and-validation] [B:round_2/07_creativity-research-references-and-idea-systems]

### `cta_conversion` — CTA and Conversion Craft

### Objective And Action

- **Rule:** Choose the intended audience action before designing the content. **Why:** The desired action determines the depth, timing, friction, and CTA the content needs. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:round_4/02_idea-generation-problem-narrowing-and-content-research]
- **Rule:** Use one primary next action that matches the audience’s current level of intent. **Why:** Competing CTAs increase friction and make the content’s purpose ambiguous. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:round_4/04_expert-brand-positioning-and-monetization]
- **Rule:** Make the CTA a continuation of the value just delivered rather than an unrelated interruption. **Why:** Contextual continuity makes the ask feel useful instead of extractive. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting] [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior]
- **Rule:** Place the ask after the audience has enough value and relevance to understand why the next step helps. **Why:** Premature asks spend trust before it has been earned. **Source:** [B:round_4/04_expert-brand-positioning-and-monetization] [B:round_4/07_growth-testing-publishing-and-distribution]

### Pain And Solution

- **Rule:** Make a real problem emotionally present enough to matter, then signal a credible path to relief before the experience becomes merely negative. **Why:** Pain without hope can create avoidance instead of action. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior]
- **Rule:** Do not rely on fear as the universal motive; consider aspiration, convenience, identity, pleasure, curiosity, and habit when they better fit the decision. **Why:** The corpus itself flags fear-dominance as a creator model rather than a universal law. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior]

### Friction And Offer

- **Rule:** After teaching the core principle, sell implementation speed, reduced effort, personalization, feedback, tools, integration, or accountability rather than withholding the useful idea. **Why:** The paid step can monetize execution without making free content intentionally incomplete. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:round_4/03_educational-value-and-the-trinity-of-value]
- **Rule:** Reduce unnecessary steps between the content and the requested action. **Why:** Every additional decision, form field, platform jump, or ambiguous instruction can lower conversion. **Source:** [B:round_3/08_luxury-premium-positioning-and-brand-value] [B:round_3/07_distribution-networks-clipping-and-audience-growth]
- **Rule:** Give attention surges a deliberate destination such as another useful asset, an owned contact channel, or the relevant offer. **Why:** Without a destination, attention resets and the relationship may not deepen. **Source:** [B:round_3/07_distribution-networks-clipping-and-audience-growth] [B:round_4/04_expert-brand-positioning-and-monetization]
- **Rule:** Use a next-related-problem CTA when continued education is more appropriate than an immediate commercial ask. **Why:** Session continuation can build trust and intent before conversion. **Source:** [B:round_4/04_expert-brand-positioning-and-monetization] [B:round_4/07_growth-testing-publishing-and-distribution]

### Ethics

- **Rule:** Do not fabricate fake leaks, false social proof, or autobiographical stakes to increase action. **Why:** Conversion pressure that depends on fabricated evidence or events damages trust and makes the action illegitimate. **Source:** [B:round_3/07_distribution-networks-clipping-and-audience-growth] [B:round_4/01_hooks-attention-and-open-loops]

### `editing` — Editing Craft

### Editing Purpose

- **Rule:** Edit to control comprehension, attention, emotion, and continuity before considering decorative polish. **Why:** Effects are only useful when they serve the audience’s experience of the message. **Source:** [B:round_2/01_editing-philosophy-attention-emotion-and-clarity] [B:round_4/08_content-operations-editing-and-outsourcing]
- **Rule:** Build a coherent primary narrative or A-roll foundation before layering effects, B-roll, motion graphics, or sound design. **Why:** A weak core cannot be reliably rescued by post-production density. **Source:** [B:round_2/01_editing-philosophy-attention-emotion-and-clarity]
- **Rule:** Before each edit choice, identify what the audience should understand or feel next. **Why:** A clear intention makes cuts, holds, visuals, and sound easier to justify. **Source:** [B:round_2/01_editing-philosophy-attention-emotion-and-clarity]

### Cuts And Pacing

- **Rule:** Cut when thought, emphasis, perspective, or useful visual information changes rather than following a fixed cadence. **Why:** The corpus contains no universal cut interval; pacing must follow content and emotion. **Source:** [B:round_2/01_editing-philosophy-attention-emotion-and-clarity] [B:round_2/02_retention-hooks-pacing-and-story]
- **Rule:** Use holds and silence when weight, anticipation, comprehension, or emotional contrast benefits from less editing. **Why:** Removal can be more powerful than constant stimulation. **Source:** [B:round_2/01_editing-philosophy-attention-emotion-and-clarity] [B:round_2/04_sound-design-music-and-silence]
- **Rule:** Use J-cuts, L-cuts, or other continuity devices when they make transitions feel like thought flow rather than hard mechanical breaks. **Why:** Audio continuity can hide edit seams and preserve momentum. **Source:** [B:round_2/05_software-workflows-premiere-pro-davinci-resolve-and-capcut]
- **Rule:** Increase edit density only when the information or emotion justifies it. **Why:** More edits do not automatically create more retention and can reduce clarity. **Source:** [B:round_2/01_editing-philosophy-attention-emotion-and-clarity] [B:round_2/02_retention-hooks-pacing-and-story]

### Sound

- **Rule:** Keep voice intelligibility above music and sound effects whenever speech carries the core information. **Why:** Masking the message defeats the purpose of supporting audio. **Source:** [B:round_2/04_sound-design-music-and-silence]
- **Rule:** Use motion sounds for movement, texture sounds for material or interface actions, and emotional sounds for tension or release. **Why:** Functional sound layers are easier to control than indiscriminate sound-effect stacking. **Source:** [B:round_2/04_sound-design-music-and-silence]
- **Rule:** Align the perceptual peak of a hit or whoosh with the peak of the corresponding visual movement or emphasis. **Why:** Synchronization makes sound feel motivated rather than pasted on. **Source:** [B:round_2/04_sound-design-music-and-silence]
- **Rule:** Choose music for the emotional context it creates and let its structure inform rhythm only when that supports the message. **Why:** Music can guide pacing but should not force inappropriate cuts. **Source:** [B:round_2/04_sound-design-music-and-silence]
- **Rule:** Use silence as an intentional sound choice instead of treating every empty moment as a defect. **Why:** Silence can create gravity, contrast, and release. **Source:** [B:round_2/04_sound-design-music-and-silence]

### Effects And Motion

- **Rule:** Add an effect only when it clarifies, intensifies, explains, or renews; remove it when it competes, delays, or advertises the editor. **Why:** Motivated effects feel integrated while performative effects pull attention away from the idea. **Source:** [B:round_2/01_editing-philosophy-attention-emotion-and-clarity]
- **Rule:** Reserve the strongest animation or full-screen treatment for the most important information. **Why:** Visual intensity should correspond to narrative importance. **Source:** [B:round_2/03_visual-layout-typography-motion-and-b-roll]
- **Rule:** Ease and shape motion so it communicates intentional acceleration and deceleration rather than robotic linear movement. **Why:** Natural motion improves perceived polish without requiring more elements. **Source:** [B:round_2/03_visual-layout-typography-motion-and-b-roll]

### Workflow And Review

- **Rule:** Prototype expensive or complex sequences with rough stand-ins before final production when reshoots would be costly. **Why:** A cheap mockup validates timing, coverage, and shot requirements early. **Source:** [B:round_2/08_systems-presets-learning-and-production-efficiency]
- **Rule:** Define a minimum viable polish standard and a stopping rule. **Why:** Editing can expand indefinitely without a threshold for “good enough to publish.” **Source:** [B:round_2/08_systems-presets-learning-and-production-efficiency]
- **Rule:** Save reusable editing components after they prove useful, but keep the underlying communicative decision editable. **Why:** Templates compound speed while rigid presets can fossilize bad choices. **Source:** [B:round_2/08_systems-presets-learning-and-production-efficiency] [B:round_4/08_content-operations-editing-and-outsourcing]

### `hooks` — Hook Craft

### Hook Objective

- **Rule:** Make a hook communicate the subject, the reason to care, and the reason this treatment is worth continuing. **Why:** A dramatic line without orientation can attract the wrong attention or create confusion. **Source:** [B:kallaway/02_hooks-and-initial-retention]
- **Rule:** Let irrelevant people self-select out rather than hiding the topic to maximize raw curiosity. **Why:** On-target attention is more useful than undifferentiated pauses. **Source:** [B:kallaway/02_hooks-and-initial-retention] [B:kallaway/06_content-strategy-growth-algorithms-and-monetization]

### Context Contrast

- **Rule:** Start with a clear topic plus an interesting known fact, pain, benefit, or common ground, then introduce a connected reversal. **Why:** Clarity creates orientation and contrast creates a reason to continue. **Source:** [B:kallaway/02_hooks-and-initial-retention]
- **Rule:** Use a contrast turn such as a contradiction, limitation, or hidden cause only when the turn remains connected to the original subject. **Why:** Random surprise breaks continuity; meaningful reversal deepens curiosity. **Source:** [B:kallaway/02_hooks-and-initial-retention] [B:round_4/01_hooks-attention-and-open-loops]
- **Rule:** Make the non-obvious claim genuinely useful or interesting enough to repay the setup. **Why:** Contrarian framing without substance damages trust. **Source:** [B:kallaway/02_hooks-and-initial-retention]
- **Rule:** Challenge the common approach rather than the audience’s intelligence or worth. **Why:** Mistake framing can create stakes without humiliating the viewer. **Source:** [B:round_4/01_hooks-attention-and-open-loops] [B:round_4/09_round-4-action-playbooks-and-tensions]

### Promise And Stakes

- **Rule:** Lead with a familiar desired outcome, pain, or consequence before naming an unfamiliar mechanism when the mechanism itself is not yet meaningful. **Why:** People can opt into a result they understand before learning the technical label behind it. **Source:** [B:kallaway/02_hooks-and-initial-retention]
- **Rule:** State the problem or outcome clearly while withholding the method, causal explanation, or resolution when curiosity is needed. **Why:** This balances clarity with an honest information gap. **Source:** [B:round_4/01_hooks-attention-and-open-loops]
- **Rule:** Use specific stakes, constraints, or consequences instead of generic intensity words. **Why:** Concrete stakes make the promise easier to evaluate and believe. **Source:** [B:round_4/01_hooks-attention-and-open-loops] [B:round_4/05_scriptwriting-compression-personalization-and-power-words]
- **Rule:** Do not insert precise numbers, percentages, deadlines, or scientific language unless they are supported. **Why:** False precision may increase initial interest while weakening credibility. **Source:** [B:round_4/01_hooks-attention-and-open-loops] [B:round_4/05_scriptwriting-compression-personalization-and-power-words]

### Familiarity And Novelty

- **Rule:** Bridge an unfamiliar idea through a genuinely relevant familiar reference when it improves comprehension. **Why:** Familiar anchors can reduce orientation cost without requiring the idea itself to be familiar. **Source:** [B:kallaway/02_hooks-and-initial-retention]

### Hook Sequence

- **Rule:** Treat the opening as a sequence rather than a single sentence: hook, confirmation, credibility or specificity, then progression. **Why:** A strong first line can still fail if the following lines do not validate it. **Source:** [B:round_4/01_hooks-attention-and-open-loops] [B:round_4/09_round-4-action-playbooks-and-tensions]
- **Rule:** Use the line immediately after the hook to confirm relevance, add proof or specificity, or begin the promised explanation. **Why:** Unrelated biography or setup in the lock-in zone wastes the attention just earned. **Source:** [B:round_4/01_hooks-attention-and-open-loops] [B:round_4/05_scriptwriting-compression-personalization-and-power-words]
- **Rule:** When useful, establish a common belief before presenting the different mechanism or interpretation. **Why:** The contrast is easier to perceive when the audience first recognizes the baseline model. **Source:** [B:kallaway/02_hooks-and-initial-retention]

### Multichannel Alignment

- **Rule:** Make the headline text, spoken opening, and first visual imply the same subject and promise when several channels are present. **Why:** Conflicting signals increase confusion at the point where orientation matters most. **Source:** [B:kallaway/02_hooks-and-initial-retention]
- **Rule:** Use the first visual to confirm, demonstrate, or add information to the hook rather than merely decorate it. **Why:** A meaningful visual can establish context faster than words alone. **Source:** [B:kallaway/02_hooks-and-initial-retention]

### `ideation_angles` — Ideation & Angles Craft

### Definitions And Units

- **Rule:** Separate the parent topic, content idea, angle, and format instead of treating them as one thing. **Why:** A topic is the subject area; an idea is the specific proposition, problem, question, or transformation; an angle is the treatment of that idea; format is how it is delivered. Keeping the units separate makes iteration deliberate. **Source:** [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:01:24] [C12K:tJAmLKU9DEA@00:09:16]
- **Rule:** Do not stop at a topic; turn it into a specific audience-relevant idea before drafting. **Why:** “Branding,” “fitness,” or “content marketing” are categories, not yet useful content premises. A usable idea tells the audience what specific problem, question, claim, transformation, or tension will be addressed. **Source:** [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:00:55] [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:50:14]
- **Rule:** Treat an angle as a meaningful change in premise, take, context, causal explanation, or point of attack—not merely a new hook or synonymous wording. **Why:** Two pieces can share a topic while becoming different content because the underlying treatment changes; cosmetic phrasing changes do not create a new idea. **Source:** [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:01:27] [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:50:43]

### Input Mining And Capture

- **Rule:** Mine recurring audience questions, requests, comments, objections, beliefs, disagreements, and frustrations as raw idea inputs. **Why:** These signals reveal uncertainty and demand in the audience’s own environment instead of forcing the creator to guess what matters. **Source:** [C12K:5N1aLgU8E00@00:00:35] [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:05:13] [C12K:_5RQAjOyG10@00:11:35]
- **Rule:** Mine sales, support, delivery, customer, and client conversations for the problems and experiences people actually describe. **Why:** Real interactions expose specific language, edge cases, objections, and operational pain that generic market summaries often miss. **Source:** [C12K:O9zXC_kNtw8@00:03:39] [C12K:avm5jc2AWYI@00:13:54]
- **Rule:** Capture spontaneous observations and ideas immediately in one trusted idea bank instead of relying on memory. **Why:** Useful ideas often appear during work, conversations, or everyday observation; a consistent capture point prevents those insights from disappearing before an ideation session. **Source:** [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:02:19]
- **Rule:** Mine your own strongest past content before external references and look for components worth iterating. **Why:** Your own winners are the closest evidence of what your specific audience already responds to and what you can execute credibly. **Source:** [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:01:25] [C12K:reisEL_D7xc@01:22:14]
- **Rule:** Use lived experience, real decisions, experiments, mistakes, results, and work-in-progress as idea material when they create a useful lesson for the audience. **Why:** First-hand material gives the creator specific substance and a viewpoint that cannot be reproduced by generic research alone. **Source:** [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:48:47] [C12K:N34zz1-RSGw@00:15:28]
- **Rule:** Use competitor, adjacent-niche, and cross-domain creators as research inputs, not as scripts to imitate. **Why:** External references can reveal demand and mechanisms that work while still leaving the creator responsible for relevance, reasoning, and original expression. **Source:** [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:03:27] [C12K:v_-9YiG1kO0@00:12:33]
- **Rule:** Separate idea references from execution references when useful. **Why:** A creator may be valuable because their topics show demand while another is valuable because of structure, visuals, or delivery; mixing those roles encourages unnecessary cloning. **Source:** [C12K:v_-9YiG1kO0@00:12:06] [C12K:v_-9YiG1kO0@00:13:01]
- **Rule:** Treat fresh events, new cases, recent work, and changes in your own environment as ways to refresh durable ideas without becoming dependent on trend-chasing. **Why:** Evergreen principles can gain relevance from current context while the underlying idea remains useful after the moment passes. **Source:** [C12K:reisEL_D7xc@01:22:14] [C12K:O9zXC_kNtw8@00:03:39]

### Demand And Relevance

- **Rule:** Prefer ideas connected to demonstrated audience demand: a problem, question, desire, uncertainty, or decision people already care about. **Why:** An original treatment cannot rescue a subject that the intended audience has no reason to consume. **Source:** [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:00:44] [C12K:5N1aLgU8E00@00:00:42]
- **Rule:** Prefer specific, observable problems over vague abstractions unless the content can first make the hidden problem visible. **Why:** People respond more readily to a condition they can recognize in their own experience than to an abstract issue they do not perceive themselves as having. **Source:** [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:04:34]
- **Rule:** Keep the intended audience and desired audience fit visible when selecting ideas; raw reach is not the only definition of a good idea. **Why:** A broadly popular topic can attract the wrong people, while a narrower idea can be more useful when it concentrates the exact audience the creator wants. **Source:** [C12K:O9zXC_kNtw8@00:00:41] [C12K:N34zz1-RSGw@00:16:56]
- **Rule:** Use research and prior performance to form better hypotheses, not as commands that eliminate judgment or testing. **Why:** What worked elsewhere reduces guessing but does not prove that the same idea or treatment will work for a different creator, audience, offer, or context. **Source:** [C12K:tJAmLKU9DEA@00:10:47] [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:05:27]
- **Rule:** Look for recurring components across several strong references instead of generalizing from one isolated hit. **Why:** Repeated patterns give a stronger signal about demand or mechanism than a single winner whose success may depend on factors you have not identified. **Source:** [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:04:16] [B:content_measurement/validation]
- **Rule:** Pair a costly or consequential audience problem with a solution, explanation, or experience you can genuinely contribute. **Why:** This intersection combines relevance with credible distinction and reduces the temptation to teach generic solutions you have never applied. **Source:** [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:46:00] [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:48:47]

### Angle Development

- **Rule:** Expand a strong core idea into multiple angles before assuming you need a completely new topic. **Why:** A useful idea usually contains several relevant questions, contexts, objections, mechanisms, and consequences that can each become distinct content. **Source:** [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:50:43] [C12K:reisEL_D7xc@01:22:14]
- **Rule:** Generate angles by changing a meaningful dimension such as audience or segment, context or use case, mechanism or cause, consequence or stakes, constraint, comparison, objection, belief, or point of view. **Why:** Changing a real dimension of the problem creates a new reason to consume the idea instead of merely repainting the same piece. **Source:** [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:52:06] [C12K:_5RQAjOyG10@00:11:40] [C12K:dW7lF22BlKU@00:08:41]
- **Rule:** Use beliefs, misconceptions, objections, and disagreement as angle sources only when they expose a real audience tension worth resolving. **Why:** Contrarian framing is useful when it clarifies a consequential misunderstanding; disagreement manufactured only for attention produces empty angles. **Source:** [C12K:_5RQAjOyG10@00:11:35] [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:05:19]
- **Rule:** Create distinction through first-hand experience, evidence, argument, examples, worldview, or mechanism rather than pretending the underlying topic has never existed. **Why:** Most useful subjects are not new; the creator’s contribution becomes distinctive through what they can add to the treatment. **Source:** [B:round_3/03_content-research-reverse-engineering-and-validation] [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:49:44]
- **Rule:** Use a distinctive lens on familiar material when the lens changes what the audience notices, understands, or can do. **Why:** A familiar subject can feel new when viewed through a relevant transformation, diagnostic, economic, historical, human, technical, or other meaningful lens. **Source:** [C12K:ol26gYDKJ1E@00:03:42] [B:storytelling/story_lenses]
- **Rule:** Borrow a familiar demand signal, then solve or explain the underlying problem in your own way. **Why:** Demand can be borrowed without borrowing the original creator’s wording, reasoning, examples, or conclusions. **Source:** [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:04:03] [C12K:v_-9YiG1kO0@00:13:50]
- **Rule:** Generate angle candidates expansively before filtering them. **Why:** Simultaneously creating and judging candidates suppresses variation; separating divergence from selection makes it easier to discover stronger treatments. **Source:** [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:55:33]
- **Rule:** Transfer a format, lens, or creative mechanism from another niche only when the underlying audience behavior or narrative mechanism maps to your subject. **Why:** Cross-pollination creates useful novelty when the structure transfers; copying a surface pattern without the underlying fit creates gimmicks. **Source:** [C12K:ol26gYDKJ1E@00:03:42] [C12K:v_-9YiG1kO0@00:12:33]

### Reference Mining And Remix

- **Rule:** Prioritize references with evidence of disproportionate performance or unusually strong audience response over random references chosen only because they look good. **Why:** Reference mining is more useful when the source has some signal that the idea or execution resonated beyond normal baseline. **Source:** [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:03:05] [C12K:tJAmLKU9DEA@00:07:27]
- **Rule:** Decompose a reference into components instead of treating the whole piece as one indivisible template. **Why:** Separating topic, angle, hook, structure, format, visuals, audio, or equivalent components lets the creator learn from mechanisms without cloning the entire artifact. **Source:** [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:01:02]
- **Rule:** For each strong reference, identify what likely worked, what was weak, and what gap your version can improve. **Why:** Reverse engineering should create a better hypothesis, not a duplicate; weaknesses and missing information are often where the creator can add value. **Source:** [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:03:52] [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:20:18]
- **Rule:** Hold only the components you have reason to preserve and deliberately remix the rest. **Why:** Keeping every component recreates the original; changing everything discards the evidence you researched. Selective remixing balances validation with originality. **Source:** [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:06:10]
- **Rule:** Combine mechanisms and inspiration from several references rather than tracing one source too closely. **Why:** Synthesis gives more room for judgment and distinct expression while reducing derivation risk. **Source:** [B:round_4/02_idea-generation-problem-narrowing-and-content-research] [C12K:v_-9YiG1kO0@00:01:54]
- **Rule:** Borrow the underlying question or problem when useful, then rebuild the reasoning, examples, evidence, and expression from your own material. **Why:** The market does not grant ownership over a broad problem, but distinctive expression and original reasoning should not be copied. **Source:** [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:04:03] [B:content_quality/substance_and_distinction]
- **Rule:** Treat any one winning reference as a seed or hypothesis, not proof that every visible component caused the result. **Why:** Performance is multicausal; pattern comparison and testing are needed before assuming what should be preserved. **Source:** [C12K:tJAmLKU9DEA@00:10:50] [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:04:21]

### Selection Banking And Reuse

- **Rule:** Select ideas using a combination of audience relevance, evidence of demand, distinct contribution, credibility or proof available, strategic fit, and ability to execute well. **Why:** Optimizing only for virality, novelty, or ease can produce content that grows the wrong audience, says nothing new, or cannot be delivered credibly. **Source:** [C12K:O9zXC_kNtw8@00:01:11] [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:48:47] [C12K:N34zz1-RSGw@00:16:56]
- **Rule:** Generate more candidates than you publish and filter after generation. **Why:** A larger candidate pool makes selection meaningful and reduces the pressure to force the first plausible idea into production. **Source:** [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:55:33] [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:04:58]
- **Rule:** Store source or provenance and a short note about why an idea or reference mattered, not just a bare link or title. **Why:** An annotated bank preserves the reasoning that made the reference useful and makes later selection and remixing much faster. **Source:** [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:18:42] [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:02:28]
- **Rule:** Promote a recurring problem or topic into a durable pillar only when repeated signals show it can support many useful ideas. **Why:** One successful post is an idea-level signal; a pillar needs enough breadth and recurrence to sustain a body of work. **Source:** [C12K:tJAmLKU9DEA@00:08:35]
- **Rule:** Revisit evergreen ideas and past winners with new stories, contexts, segments, evidence, or angles instead of assuming repetition is automatically bad. **Why:** Creators become tired of their own ideas earlier than audiences do, and new audience members may never have seen the earlier version. **Source:** [C12K:reisEL_D7xc@01:20:26] [C12K:reisEL_D7xc@01:22:14]
- **Rule:** When your own idea wins, treat it as a seed for deliberate iteration rather than immediately abandoning it for novelty. **Why:** A validated idea can support follow-ups, adjacent questions, deeper explanations, new contexts, and new angles while preserving what the audience already demonstrated interest in. **Source:** [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:01:38] [C12K:reisEL_D7xc@01:22:14]
- **Rule:** Use AI to cluster, expand, summarize, and generate variants from grounded inputs, while keeping human responsibility for relevance, selection, facts, taste, and original contribution. **Why:** AI is effective at combinatorial search and organization but can generate plausible generic ideas disconnected from the audience or creator’s real experience. **Source:** [C12K:5N1aLgU8E00@00:01:00] [C12K:N34zz1-RSGw@00:24:11] [B:content_quality/substance_and_distinction]

### `packaging` — Packaging Craft

### Promise Definition

- **Rule:** Define the core idea and audience promise before drafting the body. **Why:** Packaging and substance drift when the content does not know what it is promising. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]
- **Rule:** Make the title, thumbnail or first-frame concept create one clear expectation rather than several competing promises. **Why:** A single legible promise improves self-selection and reduces mismatch. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]
- **Rule:** Frame the idea around the audience’s recognizable problem, desired state, or consequential question when the underlying mechanism is unfamiliar. **Why:** People can evaluate relevance faster through an outcome they understand. **Source:** [B:kallaway/02_hooks-and-initial-retention]
- **Rule:** Use broad, instantly understandable wording instead of a technical label when the label would create avoidable confusion, then introduce precision inside the content. **Why:** Packaging should win correct comprehension before specialist terminology is required. **Source:** [B:kallaway/02_hooks-and-initial-retention]

### Promise Integrity

- **Rule:** Make the opening content immediately confirm that the packaged promise is the content actually being delivered. **Why:** Fast confirmation reduces the feeling of bait-and-switch. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]
- **Rule:** Do not make the packaging more extraordinary than the available evidence and payoff can support. **Why:** Expectation that exceeds reality damages attention and trust. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]
- **Rule:** Aim for the delivered reality to meet or exceed the expectation created before consumption. **Why:** Positive surprise strengthens satisfaction, memory, and willingness to consume again. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior]

### Validated Frames

- **Rule:** Study proven title, thumbnail, and first-frame structures as packaging references, then adapt them to the actual idea and promise. **Why:** Validated packaging grammar can reduce presentation risk without deciding the substance or angle for you. **Source:** [B:round_3/03_content-research-reverse-engineering-and-validation] [B:round_4/02_idea-generation-problem-narrowing-and-content-research]

### Recognizability

- **Rule:** Use consistent visual or verbal packaging codes when recognizability is strategically useful, but keep individual ideas distinct. **Why:** Recognition can compound across content without forcing every piece to look identical. **Source:** [B:round_3/01_brand-strategy-positioning-and-world-building] [B:kallaway/06_content-strategy-growth-algorithms-and-monetization]
- **Rule:** Adapt packaging to the consumption context rather than assuming one title, caption, or cover treatment is optimal everywhere. **Why:** The same substance can require different framing across surfaces and audience states. **Source:** [B:round_3/07_distribution-networks-clipping-and-audience-growth]

### `proof` — Proof Craft

### Claim To Evidence

- **Rule:** Bind each proof item to a specific claim or doubt instead of using generic trust decoration. **Why:** Evidence persuades by resolving uncertainty about something concrete; disconnected testimonials or badges can add noise without increasing belief. **Source:** [C12K:5--HmBapYgA@00:12:07] [C12K:vUbLw80KTpo@01:40:55]
- **Rule:** Require stronger proof as a claim becomes larger, less familiar, higher-risk, or asks for greater commitment. **Why:** Bigger or costlier claims create more skepticism, so the evidence burden should rise with the consequence of being wrong. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:round_4/01_hooks-attention-and-open-loops] [C12K:JDR-R--4HhM@00:24:40]
- **Rule:** Make proof specific and verifiable: identify the relevant subject, starting point, result, timeframe, and conditions when they materially affect the claim. **Why:** Specific evidence gives the audience something they can inspect and prevents vague words such as proven, successful, or fast from doing the work of proof. **Source:** [C12K:9c5BhTXqzN0@00:04:59] [C12K:0HGJlL6fnAc@00:06:22]
- **Rule:** Verify the outcome at the boundary that matters to the audience; internal activity or process completion is not proof of an external result. **Why:** A system can report success while the customer, user, or business never receives the promised outcome. **Source:** [C12K:BInpv7lGp1o@00:13:41]

### Proof Selection

- **Rule:** Match the type of proof to the doubt: use outcomes for result claims, demonstrations for observable performance, similar cases for applicability, and independent evidence for external validation. **Why:** Different doubts require different evidence; there is no universal proof hierarchy that is strongest for every claim. **Source:** [B:round_4/03_educational-value-and-the-trinity-of-value] [C12K:vUbLw80KTpo@01:40:55] [C12K:eAg9NsxffMI@00:08:29]
- **Rule:** Prefer audience or situation similarity over prestige when the main doubt is whether the result will transfer to someone like them. **Why:** A highly relevant peer case often answers applicability better than a famous but dissimilar endorsement. **Source:** [C12K:HsQeQM1jUeg@00:03:03] [C12K:Hz_2XmS1Q78@00:04:36]
- **Rule:** Prefer direct observation or demonstration when the claim can be shown rather than merely asserted. **Why:** Visible behavior, output, or change reduces dependence on the creator asking the audience to take the claim on trust. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:round_3/04_brand-storytelling-formats-and-creative-systems] [C12K:eAg9NsxffMI@00:08:29]
- **Rule:** Stack complementary proof types only when they resolve different doubts; do not confuse repeated redundant evidence with stronger proof. **Why:** A result, demonstration, and independent source can reinforce different uncertainties, while ten near-identical testimonials may add little new information. **Source:** [C12K:eAg9NsxffMI@00:08:29] [C12K:wr0mxKOrJuQ@00:05:02]

### Demonstrations And Artifacts

- **Rule:** Show the product, service, process, or output in use when its mechanism or quality is observable. **Why:** A relevant demonstration lets the audience evaluate performance directly instead of relying only on descriptive claims. **Source:** [C12K:vUbLw80KTpo@01:40:55] [C12K:eAg9NsxffMI@00:08:29]
- **Rule:** Use screenshots, recordings, receipts, dashboards, logs, or other artifacts as proof only when their provenance and meaning are clear. **Why:** Raw artifacts can increase verifiability, but an unexplained screenshot can be ambiguous, cherry-picked, or unrelated to the claimed outcome. **Source:** [C12K:W7_p0arAMF4@00:00:00] [C12K:BInpv7lGp1o@00:13:41]
- **Rule:** For before-and-after or comparison proof, show enough of the starting state, intervention or context, and result to make the change interpretable. **Why:** An endpoint without a believable baseline or pathway can look dramatic while proving very little about what caused the change. **Source:** [C12K:jV_bKvKqgHY@00:32:18] [C12K:oRMG_HpOAN4@00:18:04]
- **Rule:** Prefer user or business outcome artifacts over vanity metrics or internal proxies when the claim is about an outcome. **Why:** Evidence should measure the thing promised; process completion, impressions, or internal status are weaker when the audience cares about revenue, time saved, delivery, or another external result. **Source:** [C12K:BInpv7lGp1o@00:13:41] [C12K:Fw8NGGnEchs@00:15:34]

### Testimonials

- **Rule:** Prefer testimonials that describe a concrete result, change, or experience over generic praise. **Why:** Specific testimony gives the audience evidence about what happened, while compliments such as great service mainly communicate sentiment. **Source:** [C12K:oIX1cPYm-qw@00:11:16] [C12K:GBgReYa9hX8@00:10:43]
- **Rule:** Match testimonials to the exact product, problem, objection, segment, or use case when possible. **Why:** The closer the testimonial is to the prospect’s situation, the more directly it answers whether the result is relevant to them. **Source:** [C12K:h8cMdMI2fGE@00:09:46] [C12K:Hz_2XmS1Q78@00:04:36]
- **Rule:** Ask for a testimonial soon after a meaningful win or peak-satisfaction moment, while details and emotion are fresh. **Why:** Clients can recall the change more vividly and are more willing to describe it when the value has just become salient. **Source:** [C12K:xgerR4GgZQc@00:17:18] [C12K:oRMG_HpOAN4@00:18:04] [C12K:3D-AyDCAZHo@00:08:18]
- **Rule:** Use focused prompts or a natural conversation to surface the starting problem, observed change, perceived cause, and practical benefit; preserve the customer’s own language. **Why:** Good prompts produce useful specificity without turning the testimonial into a scripted advertisement. **Source:** [C12K:GBgReYa9hX8@00:10:43] [C12K:HNn9j3sD6S0@01:15:56]

### Case Studies

- **Rule:** Structure a case study around context and baseline, problem, intervention, process, and result, including timeframe or ROI when they are important to interpretation. **Why:** A case becomes credible and useful when the audience can see what changed, what was done, and what outcome followed. **Source:** [C12K:mugDs0XaQ1A@00:35:01] [C12K:jV_bKvKqgHY@00:32:18]
- **Rule:** Explain the pathway and key decisions, not only the before-and-after result. **Why:** Showing how the transformation happened makes the case educational and helps the audience judge whether the mechanism is plausible. **Source:** [C12K:mugDs0XaQ1A@00:35:01] [C12K:jV_bKvKqgHY@00:32:18]
- **Rule:** Choose case studies from comparable clients, industries, starting states, or constraints when applicability is the main concern. **Why:** Similarity helps the audience infer that the result may transfer to their own situation. **Source:** [C12K:HsQeQM1jUeg@00:03:03] [C12K:Hz_2XmS1Q78@00:04:36]
- **Rule:** Repurpose strong case evidence into educational assets so the same material can teach while demonstrating capability. **Why:** A useful case can create value for the audience and prove competence without becoming a detached self-promotional testimonial block. **Source:** [C12K:HNn9j3sD6S0@00:55:28] [C12K:KjwVPlM5gjc@00:13:42]

### Social And Authority

- **Rule:** Use review volume, adoption, repeat use, or broad customer participation as legitimacy signals when popularity, reliability, or market acceptance is relevant. **Why:** A large body of independent behavior can reduce the chance that a positive signal is an isolated exception. **Source:** [C12K:JDR-R--4HhM@00:25:20]
- **Rule:** Treat credentials, awards, longevity, notable clients, and professional presentation as authority cues, not direct proof of every outcome claim. **Why:** These cues can establish competence or legitimacy, but they do not automatically demonstrate that a specific promise will be achieved. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [C12K:JDR-R--4HhM@00:23:31]
- **Rule:** Use personal or living proof to show that the creator has achieved or performs the claimed behavior, but add client or peer evidence when repeatability for others is the key doubt. **Why:** Doing it yourself demonstrates capability; transferability to customers is a separate claim. **Source:** [C12K:eAg9NsxffMI@00:08:29] [C12K:0HGJlL6fnAc@00:06:22]
- **Rule:** Use peer-to-peer endorsements in a trusted industry or community context when the audience relies heavily on people like themselves. **Why:** A recommendation from a relevant peer can carry contextual trust that a distant authority or generic testimonial does not. **Source:** [C12K:gEU8cIlJB34@00:08:20]

### Placement And Capture

- **Rule:** Place proof close to the claim, objection, teaching point, or decision it is meant to resolve instead of confining all proof to a detached testimonial section. **Why:** The audience can connect the evidence to the exact uncertainty while that uncertainty is active. **Source:** [C12K:5--HmBapYgA@00:12:07] [C12K:pr5ZPbCDyK0@00:25:43]
- **Rule:** Give enough relatable starting context before an extreme result so the transformation remains believable and relevant. **Why:** Jumping straight to an exceptional endpoint can feel disconnected when the audience cannot see how the subject started or why the change applies. **Source:** [C12K:oRMG_HpOAN4@00:18:04] [C12K:jV_bKvKqgHY@00:32:18]
- **Rule:** Build proof capture into delivery closeout so meaningful wins trigger collection of testimonials, case data, and supporting artifacts with permission. **Why:** Systematic capture prevents real results from disappearing and creates a compounding evidence library for future communication. **Source:** [C12K:3D-AyDCAZHo@00:08:18] [C12K:UYtUCg9D7K8@00:02:48]
- **Rule:** When proof is missing, create it through genuine delivery such as a beta, pilot, pro-bono engagement, or first-client project and document the result honestly. **Why:** The durable solution to a proof deficit is real experience and outcomes, not stronger wording. **Source:** [C12K:yjjTH3G44yY@00:02:47] [C12K:6FRzfcU17W0@00:03:26] [C12K:vJAiJc4GeQE@00:05:04]

### `spoken_writing` — Spoken Writing Craft

### Write For Ears

- **Rule:** Write as if speaking to one person in a voice note rather than addressing a crowd from a page. **Why:** Conversational syntax sounds more natural when heard aloud. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting] [B:kallaway/04_delivery-presence-audio-and-production]
- **Rule:** Read every script aloud and rewrite lines that are technically correct but awkward to say. **Why:** Written fluency does not guarantee spoken fluency. **Source:** [B:round_4/06_recording-delivery-and-visual-trust] [B:kallaway/04_delivery-presence-audio-and-production]
- **Rule:** Break long paragraphs into recordable thought units with one clear emphasis per unit. **Why:** Shorter performance units preserve clarity, energy, and editability. **Source:** [B:kallaway/04_delivery-presence-audio-and-production] [B:round_4/05_scriptwriting-compression-personalization-and-power-words]

### Rhythm And Cadence

- **Rule:** Vary sentence length and syllable pattern so the script does not produce a monotonous verbal rhythm. **Why:** Rhythmic variation helps emphasis and keeps speech from sounding machine-generated. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting] [B:kallaway/04_delivery-presence-audio-and-production]
- **Rule:** Use deliberate pauses, holds, and faster runs according to meaning rather than maintaining one continuous tempo. **Why:** Pace contrast can signal importance, tension, confidence, or release. **Source:** [B:kallaway/04_delivery-presence-audio-and-production] [B:round_2/04_sound-design-music-and-silence]
- **Rule:** Let important statements land with clean endings instead of trailing into filler words. **Why:** A decisive cadence makes the core thought easier to perceive and edit. **Source:** [B:kallaway/04_delivery-presence-audio-and-production]
- **Rule:** Use lists and repeated grammatical patterns selectively to create predictable rhythm, then break the pattern at the important turn. **Why:** Pattern and variation can make spoken structure easier to follow. **Source:** [B:kallaway/04_delivery-presence-audio-and-production]

### Delivery Support

- **Rule:** Write slightly more energy into performance than ordinary private conversation when the medium flattens presence, but do not turn the speaker into a caricature. **Why:** Recorded delivery can read lower-energy than it felt in the room. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:kallaway/04_delivery-presence-audio-and-production]
- **Rule:** Preserve natural personality and recognizable speech habits instead of imitating a generic “creator voice.” **Why:** Sustainable authenticity supports likability and differentiation. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:round_2/06_formats-yapping-and-creator-presence]
- **Rule:** Use a teleprompter as a memory aid rather than reading every word with fixed cadence. **Why:** Reading can flatten emphasis, eye behavior, and conversational timing. **Source:** [B:kallaway/04_delivery-presence-audio-and-production] [B:round_4/06_recording-delivery-and-visual-trust]
- **Rule:** Re-record unclear or low-energy lines instead of expecting editing to manufacture conviction. **Why:** Performance quality is cheaper to fix at capture than in post. **Source:** [B:round_4/06_recording-delivery-and-visual-trust] [B:kallaway/04_delivery-presence-audio-and-production]
- **Rule:** Prioritize intelligible enunciation and distilled wording before adding vocal style. **Why:** Style cannot compensate for speech the listener cannot reliably parse. **Source:** [B:kallaway/04_delivery-presence-audio-and-production]

### `storytelling` — Storytelling Craft

### Direction And Arc

- **Rule:** Decide the ending or final meaning before building the path to it. **Why:** A known destination helps every earlier beat earn its place. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]
- **Rule:** Build causal movement with “but,” “therefore,” consequences, and decisions instead of a flat chain of “and then” events. **Why:** Causality creates changing tension and makes events feel consequential. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting] [B:round_4/01_hooks-attention-and-open-loops]
- **Rule:** Alternate context and conflict rather than delivering long uninterrupted blocks of either. **Why:** Context makes conflict understandable and conflict gives context a reason to matter. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]
- **Rule:** Use waves of tension and release instead of constant escalation. **Why:** Release creates contrast and makes later peaks feel stronger. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]
- **Rule:** Place the promised answer, event, or transformation at the narrative climax and make the preceding progression visibly earn it. **Why:** A climax works when it resolves the central story promise rather than appearing arbitrarily. **Source:** [B:round_2/02_retention-hooks-pacing-and-story]
- **Rule:** When telling an internet-native story, lead with the consequential event and why it matters before lower-priority time and place details. **Why:** Relevance-first ordering reduces slow setup while preserving context later. **Source:** [B:kallaway/02_hooks-and-initial-retention] [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]

### Conflict And Stakes

- **Rule:** Give the protagonist or subject a clear objective, obstacle, risk, or rejected default. **Why:** Conflict defines what can be won or lost and gives the audience something to track. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]
- **Rule:** Use an antagonistic force that can be a method, system, norm, constraint, institution, or person rather than inventing personal drama. **Why:** A clear opposing force creates contrast without requiring manufactured conflict. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]
- **Rule:** Give the audience a reason to root for the protagonist by connecting the goal to recognizable values, identity, or a desired future. **Why:** Stakes become emotionally meaningful when the audience can project into them. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]

### Story Lenses

- **Rule:** Choose a distinctive lens on a familiar event instead of assuming the event itself must be novel. **Why:** A different causal, economic, human, cultural, or technical angle can create originality from shared material. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]
- **Rule:** Turn process into narrative by showing the failed version, constraint, trade-off, decision, and resulting change. **Why:** Transformation through decisions is more memorable than a static list of final features. **Source:** [B:round_3/04_brand-storytelling-formats-and-creative-systems] [B:round_3/02_founder-led-content-and-personal-brand]
- **Rule:** Use a public-goal narrative only when it has a measurable objective, deadline, stakes, recurring progress, and a real climax. **Why:** A quest needs structure beyond repeated updates to sustain meaning. **Source:** [B:round_3/04_brand-storytelling-formats-and-creative-systems]
- **Rule:** For event or process stories, capture context, action, reaction, and outcome so the final narrative contains both cause and consequence. **Why:** Coverage of only the activity often produces footage without a story. **Source:** [B:round_3/04_brand-storytelling-formats-and-creative-systems] [B:round_3/02_founder-led-content-and-personal-brand]

### Payoff And Meaning

- **Rule:** End with a clear resolution, lesson, or changed state rather than simply stopping when the chronology ends. **Why:** The audience needs to know what the events meant and what changed. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting] [B:round_4/05_scriptwriting-compression-personalization-and-power-words]
- **Rule:** Compress the central story idea until it can be repeated accurately after one consumption. **Why:** A portable takeaway improves memory and shareability. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]
- **Rule:** Choose a primary emotional outcome and make the story beats support it rather than mixing incompatible emotional signals accidentally. **Why:** A coherent emotional direction strengthens interpretation and recall. **Source:** [B:round_3/04_brand-storytelling-formats-and-creative-systems]

### `value_communication` — Value Communication Craft

### Problem To Solution

- **Rule:** Define the audience’s current state, desired state, and the exact bridge between them before explaining details. **Why:** Value is clearer when the audience can see what changes and how. **Source:** [B:round_4/03_educational-value-and-the-trinity-of-value] [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior]
- **Rule:** Narrow the problem until the available format can solve it completely enough to act on. **Why:** Broad topics often collapse into generic advice or missing steps. **Source:** [B:round_4/02_idea-generation-problem-narrowing-and-content-research] [B:round_4/03_educational-value-and-the-trinity-of-value]
- **Rule:** Include the exact action, enough mechanism to understand why it works, and any critical step needed for execution. **Why:** A list of principles is not automatically a usable solution. **Source:** [B:round_4/03_educational-value-and-the-trinity-of-value]
- **Rule:** Do not confuse breadth with value; prefer one complete bridge over many shallow tips when depth is required for action. **Why:** Completeness improves practical usefulness and trust. **Source:** [B:round_4/03_educational-value-and-the-trinity-of-value] [B:round_4/09_round-4-action-playbooks-and-tensions]

### Differentiation

- **Rule:** When teaching a familiar answer, improve its usefulness through a clearer mechanism, sharper explanation, stronger example, or more actionable application. **Why:** Value communication can make familiar knowledge more usable without pretending the underlying subject is novel. **Source:** [B:round_4/03_educational-value-and-the-trinity-of-value] [B:kallaway/05_ideas-research-validation-and-substance]
- **Rule:** Explain what is different about the proposed path and why that difference matters. **Why:** A point of difference without causal justification feels like opinion rather than useful guidance. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]

### Explanation Modes

- **Rule:** Use procedural structure when the audience needs steps, settings, scripts, templates, or checklists. **Why:** Procedural value reduces implementation ambiguity. **Source:** [B:round_4/03_educational-value-and-the-trinity-of-value]
- **Rule:** Use diagnostic structure when the audience needs to identify a symptom, cause, mistake, or decision rule. **Why:** Diagnosis helps people choose the right action before applying a solution. **Source:** [B:round_4/03_educational-value-and-the-trinity-of-value]
- **Rule:** Use comparative structure when the distinction between two approaches is itself the useful lesson. **Why:** Contrast makes trade-offs and decision criteria legible. **Source:** [B:round_4/03_educational-value-and-the-trinity-of-value]
- **Rule:** Use insight structure only when the reframe changes judgment, behavior, or a future decision. **Why:** A surprising statement is not useful merely because it is surprising. **Source:** [B:round_4/03_educational-value-and-the-trinity-of-value]

### Explanation Flow

- **Rule:** For each major point, cover what it is, why it matters, and how to use it when all three are needed. **Why:** Definition without mechanism or application often leaves the audience unable to act. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]
- **Rule:** Alternate high-level framing with concrete application instead of staying entirely abstract or entirely tactical. **Why:** The audience needs both the mental model and the executable detail. **Source:** [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]
- **Rule:** Minimize the distance between advice and first useful action when the content is intended to be practical. **Why:** Low-friction application increases the chance that perceived value becomes real value. **Source:** [B:kallaway/06_content-strategy-growth-algorithms-and-monetization] [B:round_4/03_educational-value-and-the-trinity-of-value]

### Examples And Comparison

- **Rule:** Choose examples that resemble the intended audience’s situation closely enough that they can imagine applying the lesson. **Why:** Viewer-depictable examples reduce the gap between understanding and action. **Source:** [B:round_4/03_educational-value-and-the-trinity-of-value]
- **Rule:** Use visual or concrete comparison when it can simultaneously show the problem, mechanism, and outcome. **Why:** Demonstration compresses explanation and makes abstract claims easier to verify. **Source:** [B:round_3/04_brand-storytelling-formats-and-creative-systems] [B:round_2/03_visual-layout-typography-motion-and-b-roll]

### `visual_communication` — Visual Communication Craft

### Hierarchy And Focus

- **Rule:** Give every visual moment one obvious primary focal point. **Why:** Competing face, text, graphics, and motion force the audience to choose what to ignore. **Source:** [B:round_2/03_visual-layout-typography-motion-and-b-roll] [B:round_4/06_recording-delivery-and-visual-trust]
- **Rule:** Keep successive focal elements in a coherent viewing region when possible. **Why:** Stable eye travel reduces search cost after cuts or graphic changes. **Source:** [B:round_2/03_visual-layout-typography-motion-and-b-roll]
- **Rule:** Use size, contrast, placement, and whitespace to establish information priority rather than styling every element equally. **Why:** Hierarchy tells the audience what to read or inspect first. **Source:** [B:round_2/03_visual-layout-typography-motion-and-b-roll] [B:round_4/06_recording-delivery-and-visual-trust]
- **Rule:** Remove or subordinate visual elements that ask for attention without helping the current idea. **Why:** Decorative competition weakens comprehension and perceived control. **Source:** [B:round_2/01_editing-philosophy-attention-emotion-and-clarity] [B:round_4/06_recording-delivery-and-visual-trust]

### Visual Semantics

- **Rule:** Match supporting imagery to the specific noun, action, mechanism, or consequence being discussed. **Why:** Relevant imagery reduces verbal explanation and confirms meaning. **Source:** [B:round_2/03_visual-layout-typography-motion-and-b-roll] [B:kallaway/03_storytelling-and-scriptwriting]
- **Rule:** Select B-roll for the story statement it proves or illustrates, not for beauty alone. **Why:** A beautiful but irrelevant shot consumes attention without advancing understanding. **Source:** [B:round_2/03_visual-layout-typography-motion-and-b-roll]
- **Rule:** Trim supporting footage to the exact action or moment that carries the intended meaning. **Why:** Precise in/out points keep the visual aligned with the spoken or written claim. **Source:** [B:round_2/03_visual-layout-typography-motion-and-b-roll]
- **Rule:** Use a relevant environment, object, or demonstration as a credibility cue only when it genuinely relates to the subject. **Why:** Context can communicate competence quickly, but staged symbolism cannot replace evidence. **Source:** [B:round_4/06_recording-delivery-and-visual-trust] [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior]

### Text And Image

- **Rule:** Keep typography readable, restrained, and hierarchically consistent; reserve stronger styling for genuinely important words or moments. **Why:** Uniform emphasis destroys hierarchy while excessive styling increases decoding effort. **Source:** [B:round_2/03_visual-layout-typography-motion-and-b-roll]
- **Rule:** Keep text placement stable enough that the audience does not need to hunt for every new line. **Why:** Predictable placement lowers eye movement and supports continuity. **Source:** [B:round_2/03_visual-layout-typography-motion-and-b-roll]
- **Rule:** Let text and image complement rather than redundantly repeat each other when one channel can add useful information. **Why:** Complementary channels increase information density without adding extra concepts. **Source:** [B:kallaway/02_hooks-and-initial-retention] [B:round_2/03_visual-layout-typography-motion-and-b-roll]

### Motion And Review

- **Rule:** Place stronger movement at meaningful thought or state changes rather than scattering it uniformly. **Why:** Motion gains communicative value when it marks a transition the audience should notice. **Source:** [B:round_2/03_visual-layout-typography-motion-and-b-roll]
- **Rule:** Review the finished visual on the actual consumption device or size before approval. **Why:** Hierarchy that works on a large editing monitor can fail at real viewing scale. **Source:** [B:round_2/03_visual-layout-typography-motion-and-b-roll]

### Comparison And Demonstration

- **Rule:** Use a contrast demonstration when one frame can make the difference between current and desired states obvious. **Why:** A single comparison can function as explanation and proof at once. **Source:** [B:round_3/04_brand-storytelling-formats-and-creative-systems]
