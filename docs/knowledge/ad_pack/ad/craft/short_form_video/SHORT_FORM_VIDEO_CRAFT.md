### Gaps, contradictions, and date-sensitive/platform-specific items to re-check

- **No universal numeric retention law is supported.** The corpus contains heuristics such as “first 1–3 seconds,” 3–4-second motion refreshes, ~2-second visual swaps, 2–3-second shot lengths, and a 5–10-second lock-in zone, but the sources themselves disagree or caveat them. Treat them as starting hypotheses, not fixed platform laws. [B:03_SOURCE_NOTES/kallaway/18_the-psychology-of-killer-hooks.md] [B:03_SOURCE_NOTES/round_2/02_3-hacks-to-get-perfect-retention-on-your-short-form-content.md] [B:03_SOURCE_NOTES/round_2/07_my-step-by-step-process-for-editing-short-form-content.md] [B:03_SOURCE_NOTES/round_2/15_the-art-of-yapping-full-guide-to-talking-on-video.md] [B:03_SOURCE_NOTES/round_4/11_the-science-behind-viral-content-that-can-blow-up-any-brand.md]
- **No universal ideal length is established.** Some creators advocate very compressed ~30-second pieces, others work around ~60 seconds, and several frameworks scale by information density and story. Length should be tested against the promise and retention, not copied from a single example. ## 4. Hooks

### What a hook must do

- **Prioritize clarity before cleverness.** A hook fails through delay, confusion, irrelevance, or disinterest; templates cannot rescue an unclear subject or weak stakes. [B:03_SOURCE_NOTES/kallaway/09_give-me-15-mins-and-i-ll-make-your-hooks-impossible-to-skip.md]
- **Write subject + stakes first, then compress.** The viewer should have one obvious interpretation and one reason to care. [B:03_SOURCE_NOTES/kallaway/18_the-psychology-of-killer-hooks.md]
- **Use simple grammar, active voice, and easy processing.** “Sixth-grade language” in the sources means low decoding friction, not intellectually shallow ideas. [B:03_SOURCE_NOTES/kallaway/09_give-me-15-mins-and-i-ll-make-your-hooks-impossible-to-skip.md]
- **Use “you/your” when direct ownership genuinely increases relevance**, but do not repeat it until the tone becomes accusatory or artificial. ## 8. Quality: strong vs weak

### Strong short-form patterns

- **Strong:** the first seconds make subject, stakes, and expected payoff obvious while leaving a meaningful question open. [B:03_SOURCE_NOTES/kallaway/18_the-psychology-of-killer-hooks.md] [B:03_SOURCE_NOTES/round_4/01_10-000-hours-of-studying-the-best-viral-hooks-in-51-minutes.md]

- **Strong:** one narrow problem is solved completely enough that the viewer can act without doing additional research for the missing core step. [B:03_SOURCE_NOTES/round_4/04_how-to-actually-make-viral-video-ideas.md] [B:03_SOURCE_NOTES/round_4/12_how-to-give-value-the-trinity-of-value.md]
- **Strong:** the viewer can tell what is important because the eye path, captions, visuals, and audio hierarchy are controlled. [B:03_SOURCE_NOTES/round_2/02_3-hacks-to-get-perfect-retention-on-your-short-form-content.md] [B:03_SOURCE_NOTES/round_2/07_my-step-by-step-process-for-editing-short-form-content.md]
