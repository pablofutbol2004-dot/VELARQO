# Proof craft — reference map

## 0. Scope and governing model

- `proof` contains **format-neutral principles for making claims credible**: demonstrations, artifacts, testimonials, case studies, social proof, authority cues, evidence placement, and proof capture.
- The core model is **claim → doubt → evidence**. There is no universal “best proof”; the evidence should answer the uncertainty that blocks belief or action.
- This pack distinguishes **persuasive proof** from `content_measurement`: measurement asks whether content/strategy worked; proof asks what evidence makes a communicated claim believable.
- It also distinguishes proof from `content_quality`: truthfulness, verification, uncertainty labels, and non-fabrication remain quality/integrity rules. This pack covers how genuine evidence is selected, structured, shown, and captured.
- Format-specific testimonial placement, case-study layout, landing-page sections, VSL sequencing, ad-policy constraints, etc. belong in their format/channel packs and should reference these principles rather than duplicate them.

## 1. Corpus and extraction method

- Source corpus: **11,917 transcripts** (~422 MB uncompressed), plus legacy sources already represented in the original 12 general-craft packs.
- A broad proof-related routing scan produced roughly **3,925 candidate transcripts**. That count is only retrieval routing: repeated mentions, irrelevant meanings of “proof/review/results,” and low-quality material were not treated as independent evidence.
- High-signal candidates were inspected around the relevant passages, then reduced into atomic rules. Repetition across creators was used to find recurring concepts, not to create duplicate rules or imply scientific validation.
- New transcript provenance uses `C12K:<video_id>@<timestamp>`. Legacy source tags are preserved on migrated principles.
- Creator revenue claims, conversion lifts, psychological explanations, and universal-sounding formulas are not generalized unless the principle survives without relying on the unverifiable number or causal claim.

## 3. Claim To Evidence

- **Rule:** Bind each proof item to a specific claim or doubt instead of using generic trust decoration. **Why:** Evidence persuades by resolving uncertainty about something concrete; disconnected testimonials or badges can add noise without increasing belief. **Source:** [C12K:5--HmBapYgA@00:12:07] [C12K:vUbLw80KTpo@01:40:55]
- **Rule:** Require stronger proof as a claim becomes larger, less familiar, higher-risk, or asks for greater commitment. **Why:** Bigger or costlier claims create more skepticism, so the evidence burden should rise with the consequence of being wrong. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:round_4/01_hooks-attention-and-open-loops] [C12K:JDR-R--4HhM@00:24:40]
- **Rule:** Make proof specific and verifiable: identify the relevant subject, starting point, result, timeframe, and conditions when they materially affect the claim. **Why:** Specific evidence gives the audience something they can inspect and prevents vague words such as proven, successful, or fast from doing the work of proof. **Source:** [C12K:9c5BhTXqzN0@00:04:59] [C12K:0HGJlL6fnAc@00:06:22]
- **Rule:** Verify the outcome at the boundary that matters to the audience; internal activity or process completion is not proof of an external result. **Why:** A system can report success while the customer, user, or business never receives the promised outcome. **Source:** [C12K:BInpv7lGp1o@00:13:41]

## 4. Proof Selection

- **Rule:** Match the type of proof to the doubt: use outcomes for result claims, demonstrations for observable performance, similar cases for applicability, and independent evidence for external validation. **Why:** Different doubts require different evidence; there is no universal proof hierarchy that is strongest for every claim. **Source:** [B:round_4/03_educational-value-and-the-trinity-of-value] [C12K:vUbLw80KTpo@01:40:55] [C12K:eAg9NsxffMI@00:08:29]
- **Rule:** Prefer audience or situation similarity over prestige when the main doubt is whether the result will transfer to someone like them. **Why:** A highly relevant peer case often answers applicability better than a famous but dissimilar endorsement. **Source:** [C12K:HsQeQM1jUeg@00:03:03] [C12K:Hz_2XmS1Q78@00:04:36]
- **Rule:** Prefer direct observation or demonstration when the claim can be shown rather than merely asserted. **Why:** Visible behavior, output, or change reduces dependence on the creator asking the audience to take the claim on trust. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [B:round_3/04_brand-storytelling-formats-and-creative-systems] [C12K:eAg9NsxffMI@00:08:29]
- **Rule:** Stack complementary proof types only when they resolve different doubts; do not confuse repeated redundant evidence with stronger proof. **Why:** A result, demonstration, and independent source can reinforce different uncertainties, while ten near-identical testimonials may add little new information. **Source:** [C12K:eAg9NsxffMI@00:08:29] [C12K:wr0mxKOrJuQ@00:05:02]

## 5. Demonstrations And Artifacts

- **Rule:** Show the product, service, process, or output in use when its mechanism or quality is observable. **Why:** A relevant demonstration lets the audience evaluate performance directly instead of relying only on descriptive claims. **Source:** [C12K:vUbLw80KTpo@01:40:55] [C12K:eAg9NsxffMI@00:08:29]
- **Rule:** Use screenshots, recordings, receipts, dashboards, logs, or other artifacts as proof only when their provenance and meaning are clear. **Why:** Raw artifacts can increase verifiability, but an unexplained screenshot can be ambiguous, cherry-picked, or unrelated to the claimed outcome. **Source:** [C12K:W7_p0arAMF4@00:00:00] [C12K:BInpv7lGp1o@00:13:41]
- **Rule:** For before-and-after or comparison proof, show enough of the starting state, intervention or context, and result to make the change interpretable. **Why:** An endpoint without a believable baseline or pathway can look dramatic while proving very little about what caused the change. **Source:** [C12K:jV_bKvKqgHY@00:32:18] [C12K:oRMG_HpOAN4@00:18:04]
- **Rule:** Prefer user or business outcome artifacts over vanity metrics or internal proxies when the claim is about an outcome. **Why:** Evidence should measure the thing promised; process completion, impressions, or internal status are weaker when the audience cares about revenue, time saved, delivery, or another external result. **Source:** [C12K:BInpv7lGp1o@00:13:41] [C12K:Fw8NGGnEchs@00:15:34]

## 6. Testimonials

- **Rule:** Prefer testimonials that describe a concrete result, change, or experience over generic praise. **Why:** Specific testimony gives the audience evidence about what happened, while compliments such as great service mainly communicate sentiment. **Source:** [C12K:oIX1cPYm-qw@00:11:16] [C12K:GBgReYa9hX8@00:10:43]
- **Rule:** Match testimonials to the exact product, problem, objection, segment, or use case when possible. **Why:** The closer the testimonial is to the prospect’s situation, the more directly it answers whether the result is relevant to them. **Source:** [C12K:h8cMdMI2fGE@00:09:46] [C12K:Hz_2XmS1Q78@00:04:36]
- **Rule:** Ask for a testimonial soon after a meaningful win or peak-satisfaction moment, while details and emotion are fresh. **Why:** Clients can recall the change more vividly and are more willing to describe it when the value has just become salient. **Source:** [C12K:xgerR4GgZQc@00:17:18] [C12K:oRMG_HpOAN4@00:18:04] [C12K:3D-AyDCAZHo@00:08:18]
- **Rule:** Use focused prompts or a natural conversation to surface the starting problem, observed change, perceived cause, and practical benefit; preserve the customer’s own language. **Why:** Good prompts produce useful specificity without turning the testimonial into a scripted advertisement. **Source:** [C12K:GBgReYa9hX8@00:10:43] [C12K:HNn9j3sD6S0@01:15:56]

## 7. Case Studies

- **Rule:** Structure a case study around context and baseline, problem, intervention, process, and result, including timeframe or ROI when they are important to interpretation. **Why:** A case becomes credible and useful when the audience can see what changed, what was done, and what outcome followed. **Source:** [C12K:mugDs0XaQ1A@00:35:01] [C12K:jV_bKvKqgHY@00:32:18]
- **Rule:** Explain the pathway and key decisions, not only the before-and-after result. **Why:** Showing how the transformation happened makes the case educational and helps the audience judge whether the mechanism is plausible. **Source:** [C12K:mugDs0XaQ1A@00:35:01] [C12K:jV_bKvKqgHY@00:32:18]
- **Rule:** Choose case studies from comparable clients, industries, starting states, or constraints when applicability is the main concern. **Why:** Similarity helps the audience infer that the result may transfer to their own situation. **Source:** [C12K:HsQeQM1jUeg@00:03:03] [C12K:Hz_2XmS1Q78@00:04:36]
- **Rule:** Repurpose strong case evidence into educational assets so the same material can teach while demonstrating capability. **Why:** A useful case can create value for the audience and prove competence without becoming a detached self-promotional testimonial block. **Source:** [C12K:HNn9j3sD6S0@00:55:28] [C12K:KjwVPlM5gjc@00:13:42]

## 8. Social And Authority

- **Rule:** Use review volume, adoption, repeat use, or broad customer participation as legitimacy signals when popularity, reliability, or market acceptance is relevant. **Why:** A large body of independent behavior can reduce the chance that a positive signal is an isolated exception. **Source:** [C12K:JDR-R--4HhM@00:25:20]
- **Rule:** Treat credentials, awards, longevity, notable clients, and professional presentation as authority cues, not direct proof of every outcome claim. **Why:** These cues can establish competence or legitimacy, but they do not automatically demonstrate that a specific promise will be achieved. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [C12K:JDR-R--4HhM@00:23:31]
- **Rule:** Use personal or living proof to show that the creator has achieved or performs the claimed behavior, but add client or peer evidence when repeatability for others is the key doubt. **Why:** Doing it yourself demonstrates capability; transferability to customers is a separate claim. **Source:** [C12K:eAg9NsxffMI@00:08:29] [C12K:0HGJlL6fnAc@00:06:22]
- **Rule:** Use peer-to-peer endorsements in a trusted industry or community context when the audience relies heavily on people like themselves. **Why:** A recommendation from a relevant peer can carry contextual trust that a distant authority or generic testimonial does not. **Source:** [C12K:gEU8cIlJB34@00:08:20]

## 9. Placement And Capture

- **Rule:** Place proof close to the claim, objection, teaching point, or decision it is meant to resolve instead of confining all proof to a detached testimonial section. **Why:** The audience can connect the evidence to the exact uncertainty while that uncertainty is active. **Source:** [C12K:5--HmBapYgA@00:12:07] [C12K:pr5ZPbCDyK0@00:25:43]
- **Rule:** Give enough relatable starting context before an extreme result so the transformation remains believable and relevant. **Why:** Jumping straight to an exceptional endpoint can feel disconnected when the audience cannot see how the subject started or why the change applies. **Source:** [C12K:oRMG_HpOAN4@00:18:04] [C12K:jV_bKvKqgHY@00:32:18]
- **Rule:** Build proof capture into delivery closeout so meaningful wins trigger collection of testimonials, case data, and supporting artifacts with permission. **Why:** Systematic capture prevents real results from disappearing and creates a compounding evidence library for future communication. **Source:** [C12K:3D-AyDCAZHo@00:08:18] [C12K:UYtUCg9D7K8@00:02:48]
- **Rule:** When proof is missing, create it through genuine delivery such as a beta, pilot, pro-bono engagement, or first-client project and document the result honestly. **Why:** The durable solution to a proof deficit is real experience and outcomes, not stronger wording. **Source:** [C12K:yjjTH3G44yY@00:02:47] [C12K:6FRzfcU17W0@00:03:26] [C12K:vJAiJc4GeQE@00:05:04]

## 10. Important tensions and resolutions

- **Proof volume vs proof relevance:** Some corpus sources advocate overwhelming prospects with proof; others stress a single specific/verifiable example. Resolution: maximize relevance, directness, and verifiability first; add additional proof when it answers a different doubt.
- **Prestige vs similarity:** Famous clients, awards, and authorities can establish legitimacy, while similar-peer cases better answer “will this work for me?” Resolution: choose by the uncertainty being resolved rather than treating prestige as universally stronger.
- **Promise vs proof:** “Proof beats promise” appears as a creator slogan, but proof cannot create demand for an outcome nobody wants. Resolution: the promise establishes relevance/desire; proof establishes believability.
- **Raw artifacts vs polished presentation:** Screenshots, receipts, logs, and native recordings can feel inspectable; polished presentation can improve comprehension. Resolution: provenance and interpretability matter more than a raw-vs-polished ideology.
- **Dedicated case-study content vs woven proof:** Both occur in the corpus. Resolution: proof should live close to the claim/doubt it resolves; whether that becomes a standalone case study or an embedded example is a format/strategy choice.
- **Exceptional results vs believable transfer:** Extreme outcomes can attract attention but may reduce identification if the starting context is missing. Resolution: include baseline, pathway, and relevant conditions when they materially affect interpretation.

## 12. Boundaries and cross-references

- **`content_quality`:** owns fabrication, factual verification, uncertainty labels, caveats, and truth/integrity. `proof` assumes evidence is genuine and asks how to make it relevant and legible.
- **`content_measurement`:** owns experiment design, attribution, metrics, and evidence used to decide what content works. Do not move analytical validation into persuasive proof.
- **`value_communication`:** owns teaching, explanation, examples, comparisons, and practical bridges. An example becomes proof only when it is used as evidence for a claim.
- **`visual_communication`:** owns composition and execution of visual demonstrations/comparisons. The decision to prefer a demonstration because it directly substantiates a claim lives here in `proof`.
- **`cta_conversion`:** owns next-action design and friction. Proof burden may increase with commitment, but CTA mechanics should reference that rule rather than restate it.
- **Future `offer_framing`:** guarantees, pricing, risk reversal, value framing, and package terms belong there; proof may support them but does not define the offer.
- **Future `sales_conversations`:** interactive objection discovery/handling belongs there; the evidence used to resolve an objection should inherit this pack.
- **Future format packs:** landing pages, VSLs, email, ads, etc. should contain only format-specific proof execution and placement.

## 13. Date-sensitive / compliance-sensitive items — re-check

- Platform policies around testimonials, endorsements, before/after imagery, health/financial claims, and paid-ad proof formats.
- Legal/disclosure requirements for incentivized reviews, gifted products, affiliate relationships, client confidentiality, and testimonial permissions in the relevant jurisdiction.
- Current behavior and trust signals of review platforms (verification labels, moderation, review import/export, display rules).
- Any numeric claim such as “X% lift from adding testimonials” or benchmark about review counts, placement, conversion, or credibility. Treat corpus numbers as creator-reported unless independently verified.

## 14. Deliberately excluded from this pack

- Pricing, guarantees, bonuses, risk reversal, and offer architecture → `offer_framing`.
- Funnel stages, handoffs, and leakage → `funnel_mechanics`.
- Discovery calls, conversational objection handling, negotiation, and closes → `sales_conversations`.
- Qualification criteria and fit questions → `qualification`.
- Landing-page sections, VSL order, ad layouts, email sequencing, carousel slides, and other format-specific proof execution → respective format packs.
- Platform-specific review/UGC mechanics and current algorithm/distribution behavior → `channel_ops`.
- Strategic performance evidence used to decide what content to make → `content_measurement`.

## Final classification decision
