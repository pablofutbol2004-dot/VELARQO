# Buyer Awareness Craft — Reference

## 1. Purpose
Owns durable principles for diagnosing what a prospect currently understands and matching content to that knowledge state.

## 2. Core model
Awareness is the buyer’s present understanding of the problem, solution category, specific offer, and decision. The useful unit is the question active in the buyer’s mind, not the platform or a rigid funnel label.

## 3. Pack size

- **Rules:** 15
- **Categories:** 4

## 4. Canonical rules

### Awareness Model

- **Rule:** Define buyer awareness by what the prospect currently understands about the problem, solution category, specific offer, and decision—not by the platform or content format they are consuming. **Why:** Awareness is a knowledge and decision state; the same channel can contain people at very different stages. **Source:** [C12K:Sxn1ji7vYzU@00:04:39] [C12K:Sxn1ji7vYzU@00:07:09]
- **Rule:** Treat an unaware prospect as someone who does not yet recognize the relevant problem or improved state strongly enough to seek a solution. **Why:** Messaging that assumes an active problem can fail when the audience has not yet named or valued the issue. **Source:** [C12K:Sxn1ji7vYzU@00:04:39] [C12K:zrpWNzEoaLU@00:03:23]
- **Rule:** Treat a problem-aware prospect as someone who recognizes the problem but has not yet formed a clear view of the available solution paths. **Why:** At this stage the buyer needs diagnosis, causes, consequences, and orientation more than detailed comparison of specific offers. **Source:** [C12K:Sxn1ji7vYzU@00:05:47]
- **Rule:** Treat a solution-aware prospect as someone who knows that solution categories or approaches exist and now needs help evaluating mechanisms and alternatives. **Why:** The central question has shifted from whether a solution exists to which way of solving the problem makes sense. **Source:** [C12K:Sxn1ji7vYzU@00:06:33] [C12K:zrpWNzEoaLU@00:04:40]
- **Rule:** Treat a product-aware prospect as someone who knows the specific offer and is evaluating relevance, fit, proof, tradeoffs, or terms. **Why:** More category education may be redundant once the buyer is comparing a known option against alternatives or inaction. **Source:** [C12K:Sxn1ji7vYzU@00:06:50]
- **Rule:** Treat the most-aware prospect as someone who already understands the problem, solution, and offer well enough that the remaining task is usually a direct decision or action. **Why:** High-awareness prospects often need clarity on the final decision rather than another long educational detour. **Source:** [C12K:Sxn1ji7vYzU@00:07:09]

### Diagnosing State

- **Rule:** Diagnose awareness from the question currently active in the buyer's mind rather than from a generic funnel label. **Why:** A useful message answers the uncertainty the prospect actually has; stage labels are only shorthand for that question. **Source:** [C12K:zrpWNzEoaLU@00:06:14] [C12K:zrpWNzEoaLU@00:06:49]
- **Rule:** Expect one market to contain multiple awareness cohorts at the same time and create content for the important cohorts rather than assuming a single market-wide stage. **Why:** Prospects can enter with different prior knowledge, experiences, and exposure even when they share the same underlying problem. **Source:** [C12K:vUbLw80KTpo@00:06:34] [C12K:vUbLw80KTpo@00:19:34]

### Message Stage Match

- **Rule:** Do not agitate a problem as though it is already felt when the audience has not yet recognized it; first make the diagnosis or consequence legible. **Why:** Problem-agitation language resonates mainly after the prospect accepts the problem, while unaware audiences need a reason to see it. **Source:** [C12K:vUbLw80KTpo@00:33:25] [C12K:vUbLw80KTpo@00:54:32]
- **Rule:** For problem-aware buyers, explain why the current state persists and why the current approach may be failing before jumping to a specific product. **Why:** Diagnosis answers the stage-appropriate question and creates a bridge from problem recognition to solution evaluation. **Source:** [C12K:zrpWNzEoaLU@00:06:49] [C12K:zrpWNzEoaLU@00:09:01]
- **Rule:** For solution-aware buyers, compare mechanisms, tradeoffs, and reasons to prefer one approach before relying on product-specific persuasion. **Why:** Once the buyer accepts that solutions exist, the next uncertainty is usually how the approaches differ and which causal model is credible. **Source:** [C12K:Sxn1ji7vYzU@00:06:33] [C12K:zrpWNzEoaLU@00:09:13]
- **Rule:** As awareness rises, reduce redundant education and make the message progressively more specific to the offer, proof, fit, and next decision. **Why:** Higher-awareness prospects already possess context that lower-awareness prospects still need. **Source:** [C12K:pFzSQX7fo6M@00:08:52] [C12K:pFzSQX7fo6M@00:09:16]

### Progression And Limits

- **Rule:** Use content to move prospects into a more informed adjacent state rather than assuming every piece must jump an unaware person directly to purchase readiness. **Why:** Large state jumps ask one asset to establish the problem, category, mechanism, product, proof, and decision simultaneously. **Source:** [C12K:zrpWNzEoaLU@00:05:53] [C12K:zrpWNzEoaLU@00:08:31]
- **Rule:** When much of the target market is unaware, budget for category education and demand development before expecting late-stage conversion messaging to scale. **Why:** A market that does not yet recognize the problem or solution needs context before direct product comparison can work broadly. **Source:** [C12K:Sxn1ji7vYzU@00:04:49] [C12K:pFzSQX7fo6M@00:09:42]
- **Rule:** Do not infer exact awareness from a single proxy such as source, watch time, follower status, or number of prior touches; use those signals as hints, not definitions. **Why:** Exposure can raise awareness, but different people can consume the same amount of content while retaining different beliefs and knowledge. **Source:** [C12K:pFzSQX7fo6M@00:07:17] [C12K:pFzSQX7fo6M@00:08:24]
## 5. Important tensions and resolutions

- Stage models are useful compression, not exact human states.
- Markets contain mixed-awareness cohorts; do not assign one stage to an entire audience.
- A single asset can move more than one stage when it supplies enough context, but large jumps increase the explanatory burden.

## 6. Corpus claims deliberately not promoted into universal rules

- No fixed number of hours, touches, posts, or days is required to reach a given awareness stage.
- Traffic source or follower status is not treated as a deterministic awareness label.
- Named funnel stages are not assumed to map identically across every market.
