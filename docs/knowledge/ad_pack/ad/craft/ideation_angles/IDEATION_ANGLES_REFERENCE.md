# IDEATION_ANGLES_REFERENCE.md

## 1. Purpose

`ideation_angles` owns the craft of finding, defining, selecting, developing, storing, and reusing content ideas and angles before format-specific execution begins. It treats the 12k transcript archive as evidence to distill, not as material to preserve verbatim.

## 2. Canonical unit model

- **Parent topic:** broad subject territory that can support many ideas.
- **Idea:** a specific audience-relevant proposition, problem, question, transformation, or tension worth making content about.
- **Angle:** the meaningful treatment of that idea: premise, take, context, mechanism, stakes, comparison, objection, audience lens, etc.
- **Format:** the delivery structure or medium. Format is downstream of this pack.

## 3. Definitions And Units

- **Rule:** Separate the parent topic, content idea, angle, and format instead of treating them as one thing. **Why:** A topic is the subject area; an idea is the specific proposition, problem, question, or transformation; an angle is the treatment of that idea; format is how it is delivered. Keeping the units separate makes iteration deliberate. **Source:** [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:01:24] [C12K:tJAmLKU9DEA@00:09:16]
- **Rule:** Do not stop at a topic; turn it into a specific audience-relevant idea before drafting. **Why:** “Branding,” “fitness,” or “content marketing” are categories, not yet useful content premises. A usable idea tells the audience what specific problem, question, claim, transformation, or tension will be addressed. **Source:** [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:00:55] [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:50:14]
- **Rule:** Treat an angle as a meaningful change in premise, take, context, causal explanation, or point of attack—not merely a new hook or synonymous wording. **Why:** Two pieces can share a topic while becoming different content because the underlying treatment changes; cosmetic phrasing changes do not create a new idea. **Source:** [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:01:27] [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:50:43]

## 4. Input Mining And Capture

- **Rule:** Mine recurring audience questions, requests, comments, objections, beliefs, disagreements, and frustrations as raw idea inputs. **Why:** These signals reveal uncertainty and demand in the audience’s own environment instead of forcing the creator to guess what matters. **Source:** [C12K:5N1aLgU8E00@00:00:35] [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:05:13] [C12K:_5RQAjOyG10@00:11:35]
- **Rule:** Mine sales, support, delivery, customer, and client conversations for the problems and experiences people actually describe. **Why:** Real interactions expose specific language, edge cases, objections, and operational pain that generic market summaries often miss. **Source:** [C12K:O9zXC_kNtw8@00:03:39] [C12K:avm5jc2AWYI@00:13:54]
- **Rule:** Capture spontaneous observations and ideas immediately in one trusted idea bank instead of relying on memory. **Why:** Useful ideas often appear during work, conversations, or everyday observation; a consistent capture point prevents those insights from disappearing before an ideation session. **Source:** [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:02:19]
- **Rule:** Mine your own strongest past content before external references and look for components worth iterating. **Why:** Your own winners are the closest evidence of what your specific audience already responds to and what you can execute credibly. **Source:** [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:01:25] [C12K:reisEL_D7xc@01:22:14]
- **Rule:** Use lived experience, real decisions, experiments, mistakes, results, and work-in-progress as idea material when they create a useful lesson for the audience. **Why:** First-hand material gives the creator specific substance and a viewpoint that cannot be reproduced by generic research alone. **Source:** [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:48:47] [C12K:N34zz1-RSGw@00:15:28]
- **Rule:** Use competitor, adjacent-niche, and cross-domain creators as research inputs, not as scripts to imitate. **Why:** External references can reveal demand and mechanisms that work while still leaving the creator responsible for relevance, reasoning, and original expression. **Source:** [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:03:27] [C12K:v_-9YiG1kO0@00:12:33]
- **Rule:** Separate idea references from execution references when useful. **Why:** A creator may be valuable because their topics show demand while another is valuable because of structure, visuals, or delivery; mixing those roles encourages unnecessary cloning. **Source:** [C12K:v_-9YiG1kO0@00:12:06] [C12K:v_-9YiG1kO0@00:13:01]
- **Rule:** Treat fresh events, new cases, recent work, and changes in your own environment as ways to refresh durable ideas without becoming dependent on trend-chasing. **Why:** Evergreen principles can gain relevance from current context while the underlying idea remains useful after the moment passes. **Source:** [C12K:reisEL_D7xc@01:22:14] [C12K:O9zXC_kNtw8@00:03:39]

## 5. Demand And Relevance

- **Rule:** Prefer ideas connected to demonstrated audience demand: a problem, question, desire, uncertainty, or decision people already care about. **Why:** An original treatment cannot rescue a subject that the intended audience has no reason to consume. **Source:** [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:00:44] [C12K:5N1aLgU8E00@00:00:42]
- **Rule:** Prefer specific, observable problems over vague abstractions unless the content can first make the hidden problem visible. **Why:** People respond more readily to a condition they can recognize in their own experience than to an abstract issue they do not perceive themselves as having. **Source:** [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:04:34]
- **Rule:** Keep the intended audience and desired audience fit visible when selecting ideas; raw reach is not the only definition of a good idea. **Why:** A broadly popular topic can attract the wrong people, while a narrower idea can be more useful when it concentrates the exact audience the creator wants. **Source:** [C12K:O9zXC_kNtw8@00:00:41] [C12K:N34zz1-RSGw@00:16:56]
- **Rule:** Use research and prior performance to form better hypotheses, not as commands that eliminate judgment or testing. **Why:** What worked elsewhere reduces guessing but does not prove that the same idea or treatment will work for a different creator, audience, offer, or context. **Source:** [C12K:tJAmLKU9DEA@00:10:47] [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:05:27]
- **Rule:** Look for recurring components across several strong references instead of generalizing from one isolated hit. **Why:** Repeated patterns give a stronger signal about demand or mechanism than a single winner whose success may depend on factors you have not identified. **Source:** [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:04:16] [B:content_measurement/validation]
- **Rule:** Pair a costly or consequential audience problem with a solution, explanation, or experience you can genuinely contribute. **Why:** This intersection combines relevance with credible distinction and reduces the temptation to teach generic solutions you have never applied. **Source:** [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:46:00] [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:48:47]

## 6. Angle Development

- **Rule:** Expand a strong core idea into multiple angles before assuming you need a completely new topic. **Why:** A useful idea usually contains several relevant questions, contexts, objections, mechanisms, and consequences that can each become distinct content. **Source:** [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:50:43] [C12K:reisEL_D7xc@01:22:14]
- **Rule:** Generate angles by changing a meaningful dimension such as audience or segment, context or use case, mechanism or cause, consequence or stakes, constraint, comparison, objection, belief, or point of view. **Why:** Changing a real dimension of the problem creates a new reason to consume the idea instead of merely repainting the same piece. **Source:** [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:52:06] [C12K:_5RQAjOyG10@00:11:40] [C12K:dW7lF22BlKU@00:08:41]
- **Rule:** Use beliefs, misconceptions, objections, and disagreement as angle sources only when they expose a real audience tension worth resolving. **Why:** Contrarian framing is useful when it clarifies a consequential misunderstanding; disagreement manufactured only for attention produces empty angles. **Source:** [C12K:_5RQAjOyG10@00:11:35] [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:05:19]
- **Rule:** Create distinction through first-hand experience, evidence, argument, examples, worldview, or mechanism rather than pretending the underlying topic has never existed. **Why:** Most useful subjects are not new; the creator’s contribution becomes distinctive through what they can add to the treatment. **Source:** [B:round_3/03_content-research-reverse-engineering-and-validation] [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:49:44]
- **Rule:** Use a distinctive lens on familiar material when the lens changes what the audience notices, understands, or can do. **Why:** A familiar subject can feel new when viewed through a relevant transformation, diagnostic, economic, historical, human, technical, or other meaningful lens. **Source:** [C12K:ol26gYDKJ1E@00:03:42] [B:storytelling/story_lenses]
- **Rule:** Borrow a familiar demand signal, then solve or explain the underlying problem in your own way. **Why:** Demand can be borrowed without borrowing the original creator’s wording, reasoning, examples, or conclusions. **Source:** [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:04:03] [C12K:v_-9YiG1kO0@00:13:50]
- **Rule:** Generate angle candidates expansively before filtering them. **Why:** Simultaneously creating and judging candidates suppresses variation; separating divergence from selection makes it easier to discover stronger treatments. **Source:** [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:55:33]
- **Rule:** Transfer a format, lens, or creative mechanism from another niche only when the underlying audience behavior or narrative mechanism maps to your subject. **Why:** Cross-pollination creates useful novelty when the structure transfers; copying a surface pattern without the underlying fit creates gimmicks. **Source:** [C12K:ol26gYDKJ1E@00:03:42] [C12K:v_-9YiG1kO0@00:12:33]

## 7. Reference Mining And Remix

- **Rule:** Prioritize references with evidence of disproportionate performance or unusually strong audience response over random references chosen only because they look good. **Why:** Reference mining is more useful when the source has some signal that the idea or execution resonated beyond normal baseline. **Source:** [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:03:05] [C12K:tJAmLKU9DEA@00:07:27]
- **Rule:** Decompose a reference into components instead of treating the whole piece as one indivisible template. **Why:** Separating topic, angle, hook, structure, format, visuals, audio, or equivalent components lets the creator learn from mechanisms without cloning the entire artifact. **Source:** [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:01:02]
- **Rule:** For each strong reference, identify what likely worked, what was weak, and what gap your version can improve. **Why:** Reverse engineering should create a better hypothesis, not a duplicate; weaknesses and missing information are often where the creator can add value. **Source:** [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:03:52] [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:20:18]
- **Rule:** Hold only the components you have reason to preserve and deliberately remix the rest. **Why:** Keeping every component recreates the original; changing everything discards the evidence you researched. Selective remixing balances validation with originality. **Source:** [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:06:10]
- **Rule:** Combine mechanisms and inspiration from several references rather than tracing one source too closely. **Why:** Synthesis gives more room for judgment and distinct expression while reducing derivation risk. **Source:** [B:round_4/02_idea-generation-problem-narrowing-and-content-research] [C12K:v_-9YiG1kO0@00:01:54]
- **Rule:** Borrow the underlying question or problem when useful, then rebuild the reasoning, examples, evidence, and expression from your own material. **Why:** The market does not grant ownership over a broad problem, but distinctive expression and original reasoning should not be copied. **Source:** [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:04:03] [B:content_quality/substance_and_distinction]
- **Rule:** Treat any one winning reference as a seed or hypothesis, not proof that every visible component caused the result. **Why:** Performance is multicausal; pattern comparison and testing are needed before assuming what should be preserved. **Source:** [C12K:tJAmLKU9DEA@00:10:50] [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:04:21]

## 8. Selection Banking And Reuse

- **Rule:** Select ideas using a combination of audience relevance, evidence of demand, distinct contribution, credibility or proof available, strategic fit, and ability to execute well. **Why:** Optimizing only for virality, novelty, or ease can produce content that grows the wrong audience, says nothing new, or cannot be delivered credibly. **Source:** [C12K:O9zXC_kNtw8@00:01:11] [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:48:47] [C12K:N34zz1-RSGw@00:16:56]
- **Rule:** Generate more candidates than you publish and filter after generation. **Why:** A larger candidate pool makes selection meaningful and reduces the pressure to force the first plausible idea into production. **Source:** [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@01:55:33] [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:04:58]
- **Rule:** Store source or provenance and a short note about why an idea or reference mattered, not just a bare link or title. **Why:** An annotated bank preserves the reasoning that made the reference useful and makes later selection and remixing much faster. **Source:** [C12K:pirnGDCZD3Y@00:18:42] [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:02:28]
- **Rule:** Promote a recurring problem or topic into a durable pillar only when repeated signals show it can support many useful ideas. **Why:** One successful post is an idea-level signal; a pillar needs enough breadth and recurrence to sustain a body of work. **Source:** [C12K:tJAmLKU9DEA@00:08:35]
- **Rule:** Revisit evergreen ideas and past winners with new stories, contexts, segments, evidence, or angles instead of assuming repetition is automatically bad. **Why:** Creators become tired of their own ideas earlier than audiences do, and new audience members may never have seen the earlier version. **Source:** [C12K:reisEL_D7xc@01:20:26] [C12K:reisEL_D7xc@01:22:14]
- **Rule:** When your own idea wins, treat it as a seed for deliberate iteration rather than immediately abandoning it for novelty. **Why:** A validated idea can support follow-ups, adjacent questions, deeper explanations, new contexts, and new angles while preserving what the audience already demonstrated interest in. **Source:** [C12K:epfdvJ1L39g@00:01:38] [C12K:reisEL_D7xc@01:22:14]
- **Rule:** Use AI to cluster, expand, summarize, and generate variants from grounded inputs, while keeping human responsibility for relevance, selection, facts, taste, and original contribution. **Why:** AI is effective at combinatorial search and organization but can generate plausible generic ideas disconnected from the audience or creator’s real experience. **Source:** [C12K:5N1aLgU8E00@00:01:00] [C12K:N34zz1-RSGw@00:24:11] [B:content_quality/substance_and_distinction]

## 9. Important tensions and resolutions

- **Novelty vs demand:** Pure novelty can be irrelevant; pure imitation is derivative. Resolution: borrow or verify demand, then add a meaningful angle, experience, evidence, or mechanism.
- **Own instincts vs research:** First-hand curiosity and experience can generate excellent ideas, while research reduces blind guessing. Resolution: preserve both; research informs selection and iteration rather than replacing creator judgment.
- **One winner vs general pattern:** A single outlier is useful as a seed, not proof of causality. Resolution: compare recurring components across multiple references and test in your own context.
- **Repetition vs saturation:** Reusing a core idea is not the same as reposting the same execution. Resolution: revisit durable ideas through genuinely new contexts, evidence, segments, stories, or angles, and let measurement detect actual decay.
- **AI speed vs generic output:** AI can multiply candidates but also collapses toward common patterns. Resolution: ground AI in real audience evidence, creator experience, and selected references; keep human judgment at the selection and originality boundary.

## 11. Boundaries and cross-references

- **`content_measurement`:** owns experimental design, causal diagnosis, baselines, validation ladders, and deciding what actually worked. `ideation_angles` may consume those signals as research inputs but does not redefine measurement.
- **`packaging`:** owns titles, thumbnails, covers, first frames, and pre-consumption promise expression. It receives an idea/angle from this pack.
- **`hooks`:** owns the opening execution that earns continued attention. A hook is not the same thing as an angle.
- **`storytelling`:** owns narrative arc and story lenses once a story treatment is chosen. A story lens that exists specifically to structure a story remains there.
- **`value_communication`:** owns how a useful idea is explained, taught, compared, and made actionable.
- **`copywriting`:** owns audience-native wording and sentence-level expression. This pack may mine audience language as an input but does not own final phrasing.
- **`content_quality`:** owns factual integrity, originality/integrity checks, and quality gates. This pack owns how references are mined/remixed before that gate.
- **`proof`:** owns claim → doubt → evidence matching. Evidence can also inspire ideas, but persuasive proof mechanics remain there.
- **Future format packs:** may define format-specific ideation constraints only; they should not restate general idea mining or angle-generation rules.
- **Future `channel_ops`:** owns current platform trends, algorithm-specific discovery, and date-sensitive research surfaces.

## 13. Deliberately excluded from this pack

- Title/thumbnail/cover writing → `packaging`.
- Opening-line and first-seconds tactics → `hooks` and relevant format packs.
- Narrative sequencing → `storytelling`.
- Detailed scripts and sentence-level language → `copywriting` / `spoken_writing`.
- Experiment design, metric interpretation, causal validation, and winner promotion thresholds → `content_measurement`.
- Offer design, funnel stages, sales conversations, and qualification → business-mechanics packs.
- Platform-specific trend mining and algorithm behavior → `channel_ops`.
- Automatic content generation as an execution pipeline → operating system / workflow layer, not craft.
