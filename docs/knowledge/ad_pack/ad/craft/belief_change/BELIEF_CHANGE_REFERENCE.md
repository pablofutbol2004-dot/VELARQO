# Belief Change Craft — Reference

## 1. Purpose
Owns the craft of helping a buyer replace a commercially relevant mistaken, incomplete, or unhelpful model with a better-supported one.

## 2. Core model
Belief change is not rhetorical force. Diagnose the current model, identify the belief required for the next decision, explain why the old model fails, present a better causal model, and support it with reasoning, evidence, or experience so the prospect can update voluntarily.

## 3. Pack size

- **Rules:** 12
- **Categories:** 6

## 4. Canonical rules

### Belief Map

- **Rule:** Map the beliefs a qualified buyer would need to hold for the desired commercial decision to make sense, then identify which of those beliefs are currently missing or opposed. **Why:** Persuasion becomes diagnosable when the gap is expressed as a specific belief rather than a vague need to be more convincing. **Source:** [C12K:_5RQAjOyG10@00:00:49] [C12K:_5RQAjOyG10@00:14:02]
- **Rule:** Separate beliefs about the problem, opportunity, mechanism, personal applicability, and source from one another instead of treating purchase intent as one undifferentiated attitude. **Why:** A prospect can accept the problem while doubting the mechanism, or trust the mechanism while doubting that it applies to them. **Source:** [C12K:_5RQAjOyG10@00:11:40] [C12K:_5RQAjOyG10@00:14:02]
- **Rule:** Identify the buyer's current explanatory model before trying to replace it. **Why:** A new claim is easier to evaluate when it directly addresses the model that currently explains why the buyer acts or does not act. **Source:** [C12K:zrpWNzEoaLU@00:09:01] [C12K:zrpWNzEoaLU@00:09:13]

### Diagnosis And Reframe

- **Rule:** When a current belief blocks progress, show why that model produces the observed problem before presenting the replacement model. **Why:** Explaining the failure of the old model creates a reason to update rather than asking the audience to accept a contradictory assertion. **Source:** [C12K:zrpWNzEoaLU@00:09:01] [C12K:zrpWNzEoaLU@00:09:13]
- **Rule:** Frame the replacement belief as a better causal explanation or decision rule, not merely as the opposite opinion. **Why:** A useful reframe gives the buyer a model they can apply and test instead of a slogan they must accept on authority. **Source:** [C12K:zrpWNzEoaLU@00:04:40] [C12K:zrpWNzEoaLU@00:09:13]
- **Rule:** When the market does not yet desire the solution category, first make the better state and the path to it believable enough to create solution interest. **Why:** Demand can emerge when prospects recognize both the cost of the current state and the plausibility of a better way. **Source:** [C12K:zrpWNzEoaLU@00:04:40] [C12K:pFzSQX7fo6M@00:09:42]

### Evidence And Self Conclusion

- **Rule:** Prefer reasoning, demonstration, and evidence that let the prospect reach the conclusion themselves over repeated commands to believe the seller. **Why:** Self-derived conclusions are easier to inspect and less dependent on rhetorical pressure. **Source:** [C12K:_5RQAjOyG10@00:01:16]

### Decision Oriented Education

- **Rule:** Let a sequence of content resolve different decision questions over time rather than cramming every required belief into every piece. **Why:** Belief change can accumulate across multiple exposures, allowing each asset to make one coherent update. **Source:** [C12K:cXBJSYL5_2E@00:35:59] [C12K:cXBJSYL5_2E@00:36:28]

### Repetition And Consistency

- **Rule:** Repeat the central commercial message across multiple relevant pieces when the audience needs repeated exposure to understand and remember it. **Why:** A consistent message can accumulate familiarity and comprehension across a content ecosystem rather than depending on one perfect asset. **Source:** [C12K:_5RQAjOyG10@00:03:29]
- **Rule:** Vary examples, proof, and angles while keeping the underlying belief direction consistent when the same core idea needs reinforcement. **Why:** Repetition of the conclusion with fresh support can deepen understanding without mechanically duplicating the same piece. **Source:** [C12K:_5RQAjOyG10@00:03:29] [C12K:cXBJSYL5_2E@00:43:47]

### Scope And Ethics

- **Rule:** Do not require every commercial content piece to perform a belief shift; some pieces can primarily build affinity, proof, awareness, or qualification. **Why:** A commercial content system needs multiple jobs, and forcing every asset into persuasion makes the portfolio narrow and repetitive. **Source:** [C12K:cXBJSYL5_2E@00:35:00]
- **Rule:** Do not treat a factual constraint, poor fit, or missing evidence as a limiting belief to be rhetorically overcome. **Why:** Persuasion should update mistaken or incomplete models, not deny real constraints or substitute confidence for evidence. **Source:** [C12K:_5RQAjOyG10@00:11:05] [C12K:7NXz-2GfWoI@00:18:04]
## 5. Important tensions and resolutions

- Education vs information volume: useful commercial education improves decisions rather than maximizing facts delivered.
- Repetition vs proof: repeated exposure can aid comprehension and memory, but repetition does not make an unsupported claim true.
- Belief work vs constraints: a real constraint or poor fit must not be relabeled as a mindset problem.

## 6. Corpus claims deliberately not promoted into universal rules

- The “big domino” was not promoted as a universal fixed formula.
- No fixed list of four, five, or any other number of purchase beliefs is treated as universal.
- No claim that every piece of content must shift a belief was accepted.

## 7. Migrations / narrowed ownership

- No pre-existing rule was migrated into this pack.

## 8. Boundaries and cross-references

- `buyer_awareness` identifies the current knowledge state; `belief_change` owns updating the model.
- `proof` owns evidence design and evidence-to-claim matching.
- `objection_handling` owns explicit decision blockers and reusable responses.
- `trust_authority` owns beliefs about the source across time.

## 9. Date-sensitive / re-check items

- None. The pack deliberately excludes platform-specific mechanics, benchmarks, and current channel rules.

## 10. Deliberately excluded

- Fixed conversion benchmarks, posting frequencies, platform algorithms, and channel-specific implementation tactics.
- Manipulative pressure tactics or unsupported universal claims.
