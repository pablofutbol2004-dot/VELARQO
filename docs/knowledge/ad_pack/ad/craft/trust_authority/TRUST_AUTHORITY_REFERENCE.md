# Trust & Authority Craft — Reference

## 1. Purpose
Owns the accumulated perception that a source is competent, credible, reliable, appropriately aligned, and worth listening to or buying from.

## 2. Core model
Authority answers “do they understand this?” Trust answers “can I rely on them?” Both accumulate through demonstrated competence, integrity, associations, consistency, and relevant human signal. Neither substitutes for claim-specific proof.

## 3. Pack size

- **Rules:** 18
- **Categories:** 6

## 4. Canonical rules

### Trust And Authority Model

- **Rule:** Treat authority as confidence that the source understands the relevant problem, and trust as confidence that the source is credible and acting in a way the audience can rely on. **Why:** Competence and trustworthiness are related but separable; a person can be seen as knowledgeable without being considered safe to follow or buy from. **Source:** [C12K:7NXz-2GfWoI@00:12:12] [C12K:7NXz-2GfWoI@00:12:56]
- **Rule:** Do not equate fame, follower count, or broad reach with authority in the buyer's problem. **Why:** Attention can exist without demonstrated competence or a relationship strong enough to influence a commercial decision. **Source:** [C12K:7NXz-2GfWoI@00:08:56] [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@00:54:32]
- **Rule:** Treat market trust as an accumulated prior that changes how readily future claims and offers are believed. **Why:** The same message can be interpreted differently when the audience has years of positive or negative experience with the source or category. **Source:** [C12K:hWRAPUgMdIM@00:03:31] [C12K:hWRAPUgMdIM@00:10:28]

### Competence Signals

- **Rule:** Demonstrate authority by showing diagnosis, reasoning, process, and how results are produced instead of relying only on self-descriptions of expertise. **Why:** Visible thinking lets the audience evaluate competence from the work itself. **Source:** [C12K:cXBJSYL5_2E@00:36:28] [C12K:cXBJSYL5_2E@00:40:12]
- **Rule:** Treat credentials, awards, longevity, notable clients, and professional presentation as authority cues, not direct proof of every outcome claim. **Why:** These cues can establish competence or legitimacy, but they do not automatically demonstrate that a specific promise will be achieved. **Source:** [B:kallaway/01_psychology-and-viewer-behavior] [C12K:JDR-R--4HhM@00:23:31]
- **Rule:** State the boundary of your expertise and qualify opinions or predictions when evidence is limited. **Why:** Accurately scoped confidence protects credibility better than performing certainty outside demonstrated competence. **Source:** [C12K:7NXz-2GfWoI@00:18:04]

### Integrity And Reputation

- **Rule:** Protect long-term reputation when a short-term sales tactic would require misleading pressure, exaggerated certainty, or behavior the audience is likely to resent later. **Why:** A single conversion tactic can impose a larger future cost by lowering willingness to believe or engage with subsequent messages. **Source:** [C12K:uTySd2EJAjs@00:03:19] [C12K:uTySd2EJAjs@00:07:34]
- **Rule:** Do not present only wins when that creates an implausibly polished picture; relevant failures and lessons can make the source easier to trust. **Why:** A flawless self-portrait can trigger skepticism, while bounded disclosure shows the audience how the creator handles reality when things go wrong. **Source:** [C12K:Ch4Sl0POBhU@00:18:53] [C12K:Ch4Sl0POBhU@00:19:20]
- **Rule:** Use transparency to reveal information that helps the audience evaluate the business, process, or claim rather than treating disclosure itself as a trust trick. **Why:** Transparency builds trust when the revealed information is material and interpretable, not when it is performative oversharing. **Source:** [C12K:eXFp3cWkce4@00:10:52] [C12K:Ch4Sl0POBhU@00:19:20]
- **Rule:** When audience pushback exposes missing context, add the missing context without abandoning a well-supported position merely to preserve approval. **Why:** Responsiveness can improve trust while opportunistic belief changes can make the source appear unprincipled. **Source:** [C12K:Ch4Sl0POBhU@00:25:44]

### Association And Position

- **Rule:** Treat repeated associations with people, brands, ideas, and behaviors as inputs to how the audience evaluates your trustworthiness and positioning. **Why:** Audiences infer meaning from repeated pairings, so association can transfer positive or negative expectations to the source. **Source:** [C12K:Ch4Sl0POBhU@00:08:52] [C12K:hWRAPUgMdIM@00:11:00]
- **Rule:** Choose collaborators and visible affiliations with awareness that their reputation can become part of your own trust signal. **Why:** Repeated proximity to an untrusted source can transfer the same negative association to you. **Source:** [C12K:Ch4Sl0POBhU@00:10:03]
- **Rule:** If using a common enemy or contrarian position, attack a harmful idea, behavior, or default rather than manufacturing conflict with individuals for attention. **Why:** Concept-level disagreement can clarify values without making outrage or personal call-outs the basis of the brand. **Source:** [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@00:54:03] [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@00:55:01]
- **Rule:** Reject attention sources that materially weaken the trust, audience fit, or associations the business needs. **Why:** Not all views create the same downstream audience; attention acquired through the wrong signal can work against commercial positioning. **Source:** [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@00:54:32] [C12K:F0-yC1gVElA@00:55:30]

### Humanity And Affinity

- **Rule:** Use specific stories and lived experiences to communicate perspective and values instead of relying on generic trait claims such as innovative, hardworking, or authentic. **Why:** Concrete stories give the audience evidence of how the source thinks and behaves while generic traits ask for belief without demonstration. **Source:** [C12K:Ch4Sl0POBhU@00:14:37] [C12K:Ch4Sl0POBhU@00:15:29]
- **Rule:** Use values and worldview content to attract people who are compatible with how the business works, but do not let affinity substitute for competence or proof. **Why:** Shared beliefs can strengthen relationship and fit while leaving outcome claims as a separate evidentiary question. **Source:** [C12K:cXBJSYL5_2E@00:40:36] [C12K:7NXz-2GfWoI@00:14:32]

### Accumulation And Market Context

- **Rule:** Build trust through repeated useful and consistent exposures rather than assuming one credibility asset can create the entire relationship. **Why:** Trust compounds across interactions and can make later commercial messages easier to accept when prior behavior has been reliable. **Source:** [C12K:uTySd2EJAjs@00:08:29] [C12K:uTySd2EJAjs@00:09:02] [C12K:hWRAPUgMdIM@00:10:28]
- **Rule:** Raise the evidence and clarity burden when the market or category has been damaged by repeated bad experiences. **Why:** Category-level distrust makes buyers more skeptical even when the current seller did not cause the original disappointment. **Source:** [C12K:hWRAPUgMdIM@00:05:30] [C12K:hWRAPUgMdIM@00:12:07]
## 5. Important tensions and resolutions

- Authority vs fame: reach is not the same as competence in the buyer’s problem.
- Transparency vs oversharing: disclose information that improves evaluation, not disclosure for its own sake.
- Affinity vs proof: shared values can improve fit and relationship without proving an outcome claim.
- Short-term conversion vs reputation: tactics that win one sale can damage the prior applied to every future message.

## 6. Corpus claims deliberately not promoted into universal rules

- No formula combining authority, trust, and intimacy was treated as a universal quantitative law.
- No claim that vulnerability always increases trust was accepted; disclosure must be relevant and credible.
- No follower-count threshold for authority was accepted.

## 7. Migrations / narrowed ownership

- Migrated from `proof`: **Treat credentials, awards, longevity, notable clients, and professional presentation as authority cues, not direct proof of every outcome claim.**

## 8. Boundaries and cross-references

- `proof` owns evidence for a specific claim; authority cues and accumulated credibility live here.
- `qualification` owns whether a prospect is a fit; affinity signals here may influence who is attracted but do not define formal criteria.
- `storytelling` owns story mechanics; this pack owns the trust/authority job a story can serve.

## 9. Date-sensitive / re-check items

- None. The pack deliberately excludes platform-specific mechanics, benchmarks, and current channel rules.

## 10. Deliberately excluded

- Fixed conversion benchmarks, posting frequencies, platform algorithms, and channel-specific implementation tactics.
- Manipulative pressure tactics or unsupported universal claims.
