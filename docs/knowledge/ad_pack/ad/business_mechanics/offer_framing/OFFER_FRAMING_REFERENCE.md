# OFFER_FRAMING_REFERENCE.md

## 1. Purpose

`offer_framing` owns the durable principles for turning a real capability into a legible commercial proposition: who it is for, what result is pursued, why the path is credible, what is included, how price and terms are framed, how risk is allocated, and how the offer is validated against delivery economics.

It does **not** contain the actual offers for any specific business. Those belong in that business's offer graph.

## 2. Core model

A stronger offer is **not** the offer with the largest promise, most bonuses, strongest guarantee, or highest nominal price.

The useful model is:

**specific buyer/problem → clear desired outcome → credible mechanism → scoped/repeatable delivery → viable price & terms → appropriate risk reversal → optional relevant additions → truthful urgency/scarcity → validation from real buying + fulfillment behavior**

Offer attractiveness and fulfillment economics are coupled. A framing change that raises conversion while destroying margin, capacity, customer results, or trust is not an improvement to the business.

## 4. Canonical rules

### Offer Definition And Focus

- **Rule:** Define the offer around a specific buyer, a specific problem or desired result, and a specific way of getting there instead of presenting a generic service label. **Why:** Specificity increases relevance and makes the commercial proposition easier to understand, compare, and believe. **Source:** [C12K:gFafnaLGNss@00:06:28] [C12K:WowtRQNbexY@00:20:36]
- **Rule:** Make the offer legible enough that a qualified buyer can tell what outcome is being pursued, what they receive, and what commitment is required. **Why:** Ambiguity about the result, scope, or commitment creates avoidable uncertainty before the buyer can judge value. **Source:** [C12K:Xu6kY9RMPkE@00:02:21] [C12K:8_XCilpdP7I@00:21:25]
- **Rule:** Narrow the offer until most qualified buyers can be served through a repeatable delivery path; broaden only when the variation creates real customer value. **Why:** Serving radically different buyers with radically different work under one vague promise creates scope creep, longer sales cycles, and operational drag. **Source:** [C12K:8_XCilpdP7I@00:21:43] [C12K:8_XCilpdP7I@00:24:09]
- **Rule:** Do not create a new offer for every minor buyer variation; split or tier only when the required process, scope, access, risk, or economics materially change. **Why:** Cosmetic offer proliferation divides attention and delivery resources without creating a genuinely different commercial proposition. **Source:** [C12K:WowtRQNbexY@00:15:28] [C12K:WowtRQNbexY@00:16:26]

### Outcome And Value

- **Rule:** Frame the offer around the customer outcome or changed state rather than leading with implementation details, tools, or hours. **Why:** Buyers usually value what the work changes for them more than the internal mechanics used to produce that change. **Source:** [C12K:i9ogic8dlX4@00:06:20] [C12K:QTWBgFLuNvY@00:00:36]
- **Rule:** Increase real offer value by improving the buyer’s likelihood of success, reducing time to value, or reducing unnecessary effort and sacrifice rather than merely enlarging the promise. **Why:** The same desired outcome can become more valuable when it is easier, faster, or more reliable, while claim inflation creates credibility and fulfillment problems. **Source:** [C12K:i9ogic8dlX4@00:06:36] [C12K:WowtRQNbexY@00:05:57]
- **Rule:** Make success criteria specific and observable, and use a timeframe only when the delivery process and evidence can support it. **Why:** A concrete result is easier to evaluate than vague improvement, but false precision turns clarity into overpromising. **Source:** [C12K:nGumOYISiv0@00:05:43] [C12K:LzGJ3MQParY@01:27:01]
- **Rule:** List the major failure scenarios that could prevent the buyer from reaching the result and design the offer to remove, reduce, or explicitly manage the important ones. **Why:** An offer becomes more credible and useful when it anticipates the obstacles that would otherwise break the path to the promised outcome. **Source:** [C12K:nGumOYISiv0@00:07:24] [C12K:nGumOYISiv0@00:07:32]
- **Rule:** Express value relative to the buyer’s relevant alternatives, economic consequence, or status quo rather than treating seller effort as the main pricing justification. **Why:** Hours spent do not necessarily track the money, time, risk, or operational burden changed for the buyer. **Source:** [C12K:QTWBgFLuNvY@00:09:19] [C12K:noE1MQPMvAc@00:29:07]

### Mechanism And Differentiation

- **Rule:** Use the mechanism to explain how the offer produces the result and why that path is relevant; do not rely on a novel-sounding name without a meaningful process difference. **Why:** A mechanism is useful when it increases understanding or credibility, not when it is merely decorative differentiation. **Source:** [C12K:WowtRQNbexY@00:09:46] [C12K:WowtRQNbexY@00:10:38]
- **Rule:** Seek meaningful differentiation in the buyer, problem, method, scope, evidence, access, or economics before trying to differentiate by adding more components. **Why:** Extra deliverables and bonuses can make two fundamentally identical offers noisier without making the buying reason stronger. **Source:** [C12K:gFafnaLGNss@00:06:28] [C12K:6AqF4iHcB14@00:00:21]
- **Rule:** Use “without X” or similar effort-removal language only when that constraint is genuinely achievable and important to the buyer. **Why:** Removing a real obstacle can increase value; pretending necessary work disappears turns the offer into an unbelievable or undeliverable promise. **Source:** [C12K:i9ogic8dlX4@00:06:36] [C12K:WowtRQNbexY@00:06:25]

### Scope Packaging And Delivery

- **Rule:** Define inclusions, exclusions, responsibilities, handoffs, and completion boundaries before selling a fixed scope. **Why:** Unclear boundaries invite scope creep and make both price and delivery expectations unstable. **Source:** [C12K:8_XCilpdP7I@00:21:17] [C12K:8_XCilpdP7I@00:21:22]
- **Rule:** Standardize repeatable components of delivery where possible and reserve custom work for variables that materially affect the result. **Why:** Repeatability shortens sales and delivery complexity while still allowing useful customization where it matters. **Source:** [C12K:8_XCilpdP7I@00:21:43] [C12K:8_XCilpdP7I@00:24:09]
- **Rule:** Package deliverables as supports for the core result instead of presenting a feature inventory as if quantity alone were value. **Why:** A longer list can increase cognitive load without increasing confidence that the desired outcome will happen. **Source:** [C12K:QTWBgFLuNvY@00:00:36] [C12K:i9ogic8dlX4@00:06:20]
- **Rule:** Add done-for-you work, implementation help, or support when it materially increases success probability or reduces meaningful buyer effort enough to justify the delivery cost. **Why:** More service can raise value, but indiscriminate done-for-you work creates operational drag that can make the offer worse for the business. **Source:** [C12K:nGumOYISiv0@00:08:35] [C12K:nGumOYISiv0@00:08:48]
- **Rule:** Use access or support tiers only when the additional access changes the experience, speed, certainty, or cost to serve. **Why:** A higher tier should represent a real difference rather than a cosmetic repackaging of the same delivery. **Source:** [C12K:WowtRQNbexY@00:16:55] [C12K:WowtRQNbexY@00:17:22]
- **Rule:** Do not scale acquisition ahead of fulfillment understanding; validate the delivery path and its bottlenecks before pushing volume aggressively. **Why:** A strong front-end offer can amplify complaints, refunds, and operational failure when fulfillment is not ready for the volume it creates. **Source:** [C12K:8_XCilpdP7I@00:21:35] [C12K:lIuk83y6q3U@01:40:38]

### Pricing And Terms

- **Rule:** Set price using buyer value, delivery economics, capacity, and market evidence rather than hours worked alone. **Why:** Price needs to support the value created and the economics required to deliver it sustainably. **Source:** [C12K:QTWBgFLuNvY@00:09:19] [C12K:noE1MQPMvAc@00:07:11]
- **Rule:** When the offer is unvalidated, use comparable market pricing as a starting reference rather than pretending to know the optimal price, then update from real buying behavior. **Why:** A provisional market anchor reduces arbitrary guessing while preserving room to learn from actual demand. **Source:** [C12K:nGumOYISiv0@00:21:44] [C12K:nGumOYISiv0@00:21:58]
- **Rule:** Treat unusually high close rates as a possible signal of underpricing, over-scoping, unusually warm demand, or weak qualification—not as proof of a universal target price. **Why:** Close rate depends on traffic quality, sales skill, qualification, scope, and price, so a single threshold cannot determine the right price by itself. **Source:** [C12K:noE1MQPMvAc@00:07:11] [C12K:nGumOYISiv0@00:22:37]
- **Rule:** Couple pricing to capacity: if demand exceeds what can be delivered well, consider raising price, tightening qualification, or narrowing scope before simply accepting more buyers. **Why:** Maximizing units sold can reduce profit and result quality when fulfillment capacity is the constraint. **Source:** [C12K:nGumOYISiv0@00:23:13] [C12K:noE1MQPMvAc@00:27:21]
- **Rule:** Offer payment structures that solve real cash-flow friction while keeping the total commitment and tradeoffs easy to understand. **Why:** Payment timing can change affordability and risk perception without changing the underlying value of the offer. **Source:** [C12K:MPhvqxVGzkw@01:08:38] [C12K:MPhvqxVGzkw@01:10:39]
- **Rule:** Limit payment choices enough to avoid decision fatigue; use a small set of materially different options rather than presenting every possible financing configuration at once. **Why:** More ways to pay can reduce friction until the choice itself becomes another problem the buyer must solve. **Source:** [C12K:MPhvqxVGzkw@01:11:26] [C12K:MPhvqxVGzkw@01:11:33]
- **Rule:** When discounting, change something economically real such as payment timing, scope, term, volume, or reciprocal value instead of reducing price with no corresponding change. **Why:** A discount that buys nothing for the business teaches the buyer that the original price was negotiable without protecting margin or delivery. **Source:** [C12K:unshZobTt6Q@00:26:03] [C12K:unshZobTt6Q@00:27:04]
- **Rule:** Use price anchors only against a real reference price, alternative, or set of terms; do not invent a “usual” price or deadline solely to make the current price look cheaper. **Why:** Anchoring can clarify relative value, but fabricated reference points convert framing into deception. **Source:** [C12K:sc1Rz1P0X18@01:02:56] [C12K:c20277fDlIs@00:22:48]

### Risk Reversal And Guarantees

- **Rule:** Use risk reversal to reduce a specific buyer uncertainty instead of adding a generic guarantee badge to every offer. **Why:** Risk reversal is strongest when it addresses the concrete reason a qualified buyer fears being wrong. **Source:** [C12K:i9ogic8dlX4@00:09:09] [C12K:i9ogic8dlX4@00:09:33]
- **Rule:** Guarantee only outcomes, milestones, service standards, or remedies that the business can meaningfully influence and verify. **Why:** A guarantee becomes unstable when success depends mainly on variables outside the seller’s control. **Source:** [C12K:unshZobTt6Q@00:14:25] [C12K:OsktcdNDATU@00:01:19]
- **Rule:** Use conditional guarantees when buyer actions materially affect success, but make the required actions relevant, understandable, and realistically achievable. **Why:** Conditions can align responsibility without turning the guarantee into a technicality designed to disqualify almost everyone. **Source:** [C12K:OsktcdNDATU@00:01:19] [C12K:OsktcdNDATU@00:02:04]
- **Rule:** Put meaningful stakes behind the remedy; extended access or extra service is weak risk reversal when it costs little and does not address what the buyer feared losing. **Why:** The strength of a guarantee comes from what happens if the promise fails, not from the word “guarantee” itself. **Source:** [C12K:unshZobTt6Q@00:14:02] [C12K:unshZobTt6Q@00:14:25]
- **Rule:** Model expected refunds, service extensions, misuse, and adverse selection before strengthening a guarantee. **Why:** A guarantee should improve expected economics and buyer confidence without attracting behavior that makes fulfillment unsustainable. **Source:** [C12K:OsktcdNDATU@00:01:38] [C12K:nGumOYISiv0@00:18:10]
- **Rule:** State the guarantee period, eligibility conditions, required buyer actions, and exact remedy in writing before purchase. **Why:** Clear terms let both sides understand the risk transfer and reduce disputes about what was actually promised. **Source:** [C12K:OsktcdNDATU@00:01:19] [C12K:OsktcdNDATU@00:01:24]
- **Rule:** Do not use a stronger guarantee to compensate for an unbelievable or impossible core promise. **Why:** When the outcome itself lacks credibility, extreme guarantees can make the offer look more suspicious rather than safer. **Source:** [C12K:WowtRQNbexY@00:05:57] [C12K:WowtRQNbexY@00:06:25]

### Bonuses And Additions

- **Rule:** Add a bonus only when it removes a real obstacle, saves meaningful time or effort, raises success probability, or solves a predictable next problem. **Why:** Relevant additions can strengthen the path to the result; unrelated extras merely inflate the stack. **Source:** [C12K:nGumOYISiv0@00:13:46] [C12K:nGumOYISiv0@00:14:40]
- **Rule:** Prefer tools, templates, checklists, access, or implementation aids over more training when the buyer’s bottleneck is execution rather than missing information. **Why:** An actionable resource can reduce time and effort more directly than another module the buyer must consume. **Source:** [C12K:nGumOYISiv0@00:14:04] [C12K:nGumOYISiv0@00:14:21]
- **Rule:** Use obstacle-specific bonuses instead of generic sweeteners when a buyer segment repeatedly encounters the same implementation barrier. **Why:** A bonus is most persuasive when it closes a concrete gap between purchase and successful use. **Source:** [C12K:unshZobTt6Q@00:27:27] [C12K:nGumOYISiv0@00:14:40]
- **Rule:** Do not let stacked bonus values or a long list of extras overshadow the core offer; explain the practical function of each addition instead of relying on inflated nominal valuations. **Why:** Buyers can discount the entire stack when the extra items look arbitrary or implausibly priced. **Source:** [C12K:zRIRk9wVKPo@00:39:04] [C12K:zRIRk9wVKPo@00:39:29]

### Scarcity And Urgency

- **Rule:** Use scarcity or urgency only when it corresponds to a real constraint such as capacity, inventory, cohort timing, an actual deadline, or a genuine change in terms. **Why:** A real constraint helps the buyer understand why delaying changes the decision; a fabricated one spends trust for short-term pressure. **Source:** [C12K:c20277fDlIs@00:22:42] [C12K:noE1MQPMvAc@00:20:51]
- **Rule:** When capacity is genuinely limited, let scarcity protect delivery quality instead of inventing arbitrary spot counts unrelated to fulfillment. **Why:** Capacity-based limits can align demand with the number of customers the business can actually serve well. **Source:** [C12K:noE1MQPMvAc@00:20:51] [C12K:OuE71HfrWwo@00:22:02]
- **Rule:** Avoid running the same promotion or deadline so frequently that buyers learn to wait for it. **Why:** Repeated discounts and perpetual urgency reset the reference price downward and make future deadlines less credible. **Source:** [C12K:Grk2ayGFjX4@00:43:12] [C12K:Grk2ayGFjX4@00:43:20]

### Validation And Iteration

- **Rule:** Validate an offer with real buying behavior and qualified sales conversations, not compliments, survey enthusiasm, or internal confidence alone. **Why:** A commercial proposition is only partially validated until people who fit the target actually commit money or equivalent meaningful action. **Source:** [C12K:nGumOYISiv0@00:21:58] [C12K:lIuk83y6q3U@01:39:39]
- **Rule:** Evaluate offer changes using the whole economic and delivery picture: conversion, price, margin, refunds, retention, fulfillment load, and customer results. **Why:** An offer can close more often while making the business less profitable or creating worse outcomes. **Source:** [C12K:noE1MQPMvAc@00:17:19] [C12K:8_XCilpdP7I@00:21:35]
- **Rule:** When performance is weak, diagnose market, targeting, message, proof, sales process, price, and fulfillment separately before assuming the core offer must change. **Why:** The offer is one part of the acquisition system, so rebuilding it reflexively can hide the real constraint. **Source:** [C12K:6AqF4iHcB14@00:02:08] [C12K:py9cbnJ3z8U@00:11:12]
- **Rule:** Prefer iterations that remove confusion, unnecessary scope, or delivery friction before reflexively adding more deliverables, bonuses, or promises. **Why:** Complexity often makes the offer harder to sell and fulfill even when the stack looks larger on paper. **Source:** [C12K:8_XCilpdP7I@00:21:25] [C12K:8_XCilpdP7I@00:24:03]

## 5. Important tensions and resolutions

- **Specificity vs market size:** Narrowing usually increases relevance and repeatability, but arbitrary micro-niching can remove useful demand. Resolution: narrow where buyer, problem, process, scope, risk, or economics materially differ.
- **Bigger promise vs believability:** More ambitious outcomes can increase desire while reducing trust and deliverability. Resolution: increase value through probability, speed, effort reduction, and obstacle removal before inflating the claim.
- **Customization vs repeatability:** Custom work can raise fit while exploding sales and fulfillment complexity. Resolution: standardize what does not materially affect the result and customize only the consequential variables.
- **Higher price vs higher value:** Price can signal positioning and protect capacity, but a higher number does not create value by itself. Resolution: price from buyer value, delivery economics, capacity, and observed demand; do not use universal close-rate thresholds.
- **Risk reversal vs adverse selection:** Guarantees can reduce legitimate buyer uncertainty while attracting misuse or transferring uncontrollable risk. Resolution: match the remedy to a specific fear, model expected economics, and use relevant/achievable conditions when buyer actions matter.
- **Bonuses vs complexity:** Additions can remove obstacles, but arbitrary stacks increase cognitive and delivery load. Resolution: every addition should strengthen the path to the core outcome.
- **Urgency vs trust:** Deadlines can help action when a real constraint exists. Resolution: expose the real constraint; never invent one.
- **Conversion vs fulfillment:** A more persuasive front end can make the business worse if delivery fails under demand. Resolution: judge offer changes on conversion **and** margin, refunds, retention, load, and customer outcomes.

## 6. Corpus claims deliberately *not* promoted into universal rules

- “Every offer should have a guarantee.”
- “A specific close-rate threshold proves the price is too low.”
- “Higher price automatically creates higher perceived value.”
- “More bonuses make the offer more irresistible.”
- “Bonus stack value should exceed the core price by a fixed multiple.”
- “Always use scarcity or a countdown.”
- “Always use done-for-you delivery.”
- “One particular guarantee type is universally strongest.”

These appear in parts of the corpus, but they depend on market, traffic quality, sales process, fulfillment control, economics, and buyer behavior. The pack preserves the mechanism without encoding the guru-specific absolute.

## 8. Boundaries and cross-references

- **Business offer graph:** owns the actual offer(s), ICP, service configuration, pricing, and current commercial choices for a specific venture. This pack supplies reusable principles only.
- **`funnel_mechanics`:** owns stages, handoffs, leakage, sequencing, and how prospects move toward/away from an offer.
- **`qualification`:** owns who should advance, disqualifying conditions, and the evidence/questions used to determine fit.
- **`sales_conversations`:** owns interactive discovery, objection handling, negotiation, and decision-making in calls/DM/WhatsApp.
- **`proof`:** owns claim → doubt → evidence matching. Offer framing can require credibility but does not restate proof mechanics.
- **`cta_conversion`:** owns how content or a page asks for an action; it does not own the offer's pricing, guarantee, scope, or scarcity.
- **`value_communication`:** owns explanation of usefulness/value inside communication. This pack owns the underlying commercial exchange.
- **`ideation_angles`:** owns content ideas and angles. A commercial mechanism is the path by which the offer creates the customer result, not a content angle.
- **Format packs:** may define how an offer is expressed on a landing page, VSL, email, ad, etc.; they should not restate these general offer principles.

## 12. Final classification decision

`offer_framing` is a **business-mechanics pack**, not a general content-craft pack. Its primitives govern the commercial object being sold across landing pages, VSLs, sales conversations, emails, ads, referrals, and other channels. Format packs consume the offer; they do not own it.


## Pricing boundary

Underlying price-setting, pricing-model, pricing-metric, discount economics, testing, and migration principles live in `pricing`. `offer_framing` retains only the communication of the chosen price and terms.
