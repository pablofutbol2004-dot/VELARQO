# PRICING_REFERENCE.md

## Purpose

`pricing` owns the economic design of what buyers pay and how the price behaves over time: pricing basis, willingness to pay, pricing models and metrics, tiers, discounts, payment economics, experiments, elasticity, migrations, and unit-economics governance.

## Canonical boundary

- `pricing` decides **what to charge, what to charge against, how the commercial model works, and how to test/change it**.
- `offer_framing` owns **how the already-chosen price and terms are communicated inside the offer**, including legibility and truthful reference framing.
- `sales_conversations` owns interactive discussion of price, objections, negotiation behavior, and decision support.
- `qualification` owns whether the prospect has sufficient financial capacity for the actual commitment.
- `funnel_mechanics` owns where pricing exposure or payment occurs in the journey.

## Durable model

**customer/segment → value + alternatives → cost floor → pricing model → pricing metric → tiers/terms → test → elasticity + unit economics → migration/governance**

## Important distinctions

- **Price** = amount paid.
- **Pricing model** = recurring, one-time, fixed fee, usage, performance, hybrid, etc.
- **Pricing metric** = the unit that scales the bill: seat, usage, transaction, outcome, project, month, etc.
- **Tier** = packaged boundary around scope/value/usage/access.
- **Payment terms** = when cash is due and under what commitment.
- **Discount** = a change from the reference/base price, ideally in exchange for an economic concession.
- **Elasticity** = how demand volume changes as price changes.

## Explicit non-rules

- always use value-based pricing
- always raise price until customers complain
- X% close rate means raise price
- charge exactly Y% of the value created
- lifetime deals are good for every bootstrap business
- subscriptions are always better than one-time pricing
- performance pricing is always best
- cost-plus is always wrong
- three tiers are always optimal
- annual plans should always be discounted by two months
- more payment options are always better
- discounting is always bad
- higher price automatically creates higher perceived value
- grandfather every existing customer forever
