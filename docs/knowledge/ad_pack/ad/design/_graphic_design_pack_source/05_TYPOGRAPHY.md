# Typography

Typography is composition, hierarchy, and tone—not just font choice.

## Type Roles

- Display
- Heading
- Subheading
- Body
- Label
- Caption
- Numeric / Data
- CTA

## Font Pairing

Usually:
- one family is enough
- two families can create useful contrast
- three or more require strong justification

Strong systems often use:
- expressive display + neutral body
- serif + grotesk sans
- one variable family with major weight and size contrast

## Line Length

For body copy, aim roughly for 45–75 characters per line.

## Leading

- Large headlines: tighter
- Body copy: more generous

## Tracking

- Uppercase labels often benefit from slight positive tracking.
- Huge headlines can sometimes benefit from tighter tracking.
- Body copy usually works best near its natural tracking.

## Line Breaks

Optimize line breaks deliberately. Avoid awkward orphaned words and visually weak rag.

## Rules

- Hierarchy should be obvious without reading every word.
- Avoid too many type styles.
- Large type can be used as a compositional object.
- Giant numbers work well for steps, statistics, and sections.
