## His current model
**Free diagnosis → paid diagnosis → recurring advisory/implementation → optional builds.**
1. **Free 15-minute mini assessment:** find one bottleneck, prescribe a fix, build nothing. Ask: DIY, with you, or done for you?
2. **$999 AI assessment:** ~45-minute discovery to find 3–7 bottlenecks. The transcript goes into Claude, which produces a report (summary, impact/effort matrix, tools, quick wins, upsells, ROI). Then a 30-minute walkthrough.
3. **$1–2k/month "AI Concierge":** two 45-minute screen-share working sessions a month, async access, and a Notion hub showing the accumulated value.
4. **Upsells:** process work $3–5k, small automations $1–5k, knowledge systems ~$3k, revenue agents (speed-to-lead), fractional AI officer.

The channel has pivoted: "AI concierge" shows up in 21 of the newest 30 videos and none of the oldest. 26 of the newest 30 push AI Operator Academy (YouTube → templates → community → success stories).

## Best insights
- **Paid discovery:** turn the diagnosis itself into a product. You get paid for discovery, it qualifies budget, exposes future projects, gives the client something tangible, and makes the next sale much less speculative. AI makes the deliverable almost free to produce.
- **"AI Automation Sucks, Actually":** as the cost of building approaches zero, selling the build commoditizes. Value moves upstream into problem selection, judgment, business context, distribution, trust, relationships, and delivering an outcome. **Your edge shouldn't be "I can use Claude Code."**
- **His offer ranking:** S = assessments and concierge. A = lead-generation systems, managed agents. B = workshops, knowledge bases. C = voice agents. D = hourly consulting. F = a generic automation agency. The higher an offer ranks, the more it has business outcomes, differentiation, an ongoing relationship and productization.
- **His acquisition ranking (#017):** S = content and referral partners. A = warm outreach, local events, borrowed audiences, door knocking. B = cold email, paid ads, networking. C = cold DMs alone. D = cold calling. F = Fiverr and Upwork.
  - Caveats: content took him **3+ years** to work, and he admits he has little cold email experience. **For cold email, trust Taylor Haren instead.**

## Be skeptical of
- **Selection bias:** he's a beginner at AI but not at business, offers, sales or audiences. His "beginner" case studies had hidden advantages: 670 warm contacts and a serial entrepreneur, or 15 years of domain expertise.
- **His YouTube content sells education to wannabe AI sellers** ("f*ck you money"). His advice for getting B2B clients is different: practical posts like "I built a speed-to-lead system…". Don't confuse the two.
- **His math:** "99.9% margins" and "$1,200/hour" ignore his time, acquisition costs, prep, admin, churn and taxes.
- **The assessment is itself commoditizing,** since he gives away the templates and Claude skills. The durable moat is domain expertise, proprietary data and distribution.
