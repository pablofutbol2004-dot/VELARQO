# John Danes: "I'm a $5M Agency Owner. Here's the Offer I'd Use in 2026" (reviewed 2026-10-11)

Transcript: `data/transcripts/I'm a $5M Agency Owner. Here's the Offer I'd Use in 2026 [john-danes-manual].md` (pasted by Pablo, no video ID or timestamps). Channel had 634 subscribers. The video ends in a pitch for his mastermind (agencyclients.com).

## What he says, in short

- Every agency offer is one of three kinds:
  - **Addition:** adds money (ads, cold email, lead gen, appointment setting).
  - **Subtraction:** saves time (automations, n8n, GoHighLevel setups).
  - **Hybrid:** a chain where each piece fixes the problem the previous one created.
- **Small business owners value money over time** because most haven't solved getting clients. So time-saving offers get a lot of "no", and only already-successful firms buy them.
- **Subtraction offers commoditise first.** They're easy to do and there's little skill gap. Fiverr sells n8n setups for $30-200. They're also usually one-off projects, and they're the first cost cut in a downturn.
- **Addition offers always sell and survive recessions,** but clients often can't work the leads, get overwhelmed, then pause or cancel. You get punished for doing well.
- **Hybrid wins.** His realtor offer grew in three versions, and the price rose with each one:
  - V1: ads.
  - V2: ads plus automated follow-up.
  - V3: ads, follow-up, then phoning every lead 8-12 times to book appointments. He sold V3 at $5-10k.
- **You don't need to be unique.** His agency's offer was ordinary; they won by being better at getting clients. He uses Rogers' diffusion of innovations to argue that most buyers want proven things, not the new thing on YouTube.
- **One productised offer with 3-5 linked deliverables, not an "everything" agency.** They turned down people who wanted only one piece ("just run my ads").

## How much to trust it

- **His results are unproven:** "$2M/yr in 10 months", "771 clients", "$5M collected". They're self-reported, from a 634-subscriber channel, in a video that sells his mastermind. Treat them as marketing.
- **The diffusion percentages are right** (Rogers: 2.5 / 13.5 / 34 / 34 / 16). Using them to argue against new offers is a fair stretch.
- **"99% would double hours for 5x money" is made up,** but the point behind it is widely shared, and our own research says the same. Ledger row 11 found missed-call text-back is a sub-£50 commodity, and the red team called the time-saving parts a "nice to have".
- **It's unclear what the "$5-10k" was:** per month, a setup fee, or with ad spend included.
- **His V3 relies on phoning US consumer leads 8-12 times.** That doesn't map to Pablo: no cold calling, and UK consumer contact has the PECR/TPS limits we hit with installers.
- **About a fifth of the runtime is filler or pitch:** the sushi dinner and the mastermind ad.

## What it means for Velarqo (the pivot, Oct 2026)

1. **It explains why the installer path felt thin.**
   - The *addition* half (old quotes, which bring money) was crippled by the opt-out problem.
   - That left mostly the *subtraction* half: the quote desk writes quotes and saves evenings.
   - By his logic, that's the half small owners don't pay well for. This matches what we found.
2. **Add a check to the pivot red team:** the core of the offer must **add money** (addition or hybrid). An offer that only saves time fails unless the time saved clearly turns into money. Added to `D:\velarqo\docs\IDEA_RED_TEAM.md`.
3. **Our engine is an addition capability.** Cold email to UK companies is lead generation. The obvious hybrid built on what we already have:
   - Find and email the client's B2B prospects (addition).
   - Handle and follow up the replies (subtraction).
   - Book qualified calls into their diary (the outcome).

   That's a candidate for the red team, not a recommendation. It's a crowded market, and it needs proof we can book calls for ourselves first.
4. **Keep it to one offer with 3-5 linked pieces, and don't sell the pieces separately.** We already follow this ("speed-to-lead as a feature, never sold alone").
5. **Don't hunt for a novel idea.** His point, and ours from the red team: an ordinary offer that's well sold beats a clever one. That cuts against swapping Velarqo for whatever sounds newest.
