## Core idea
He doesn't do SDR-style cold email. He runs **a direct-response ad channel where email is the delivery method.**

His flow: TAM → infrastructure → lots of experiments → message-market fit → segmentation → evergreen scale → revenue attribution. The end goal is one winning email sent to about 100% of the addressable market every 30–60 days.

| Conventional cold email | Taylor |
|---|---|
| Predict the best prospects | Start broad and let data reveal the winners |
| 4–8 email sequences | Usually 1 email |
| Heavy personalization | Minimal; personalize *relevance*, not small talk |
| Optimize reply rate | Optimize signups, meetings, customers, revenue |
| Write "the best email" | Test 10–50 genuinely different concepts |

## Best insights
- **Volume buys information.** More sends mean faster experiments, better message-market fit, better unit economics, *then* more leads. "Don't be smart enough to predict the winner. Be systematic enough to discover it."
- **The accordion:** expand to test widely, then compress around what works. After message-market fit: **70%** of volume on proven winners, **20%** on variations, **10%** on completely new ideas. Winners die from audience fatigue and AI spam filters fingerprinting copy.
- **The offer matters more than the copy:** RB2B's free-forever offer converted about 12× better than a 7-day trial for the same product and market.
  - **Priority order:** deliverability → offer → positioning/copy → targeting → small tweaks.
  - **As questions:** Can they see it? Do they want it? Do they understand it? Is it the right audience?
- **Personalized relevance beats personalized small talk.** Fake "saw your LinkedIn post" AI first lines often perform worse.
- **Analytics on the deepest outcome:** emails → human replies → qualified replies → meetings → attended meetings → customers → revenue. A campaign with a better reply rate can still produce fewer meetings.
- **Claude isn't his moat.** His moats are data, experimentation volume, infrastructure and analytics. Claude only amplifies them *once there is labeled data*.
- **His pricing:** he sells *message-market-fit discovery* first, then sells scale or performance once the economics are proven. Tiers are ~$5–6k/month for ~100k emails and ~$25k/month for ~1M.

## Be skeptical of
- **The channel is a sales funnel for his agency:** big number → contrarian mechanism → proof → proprietary complexity → "let us do it" CTA. The "Cold email is officially dead → exclusive Halo" video is textbook problem/agitate/mechanism/scarcity.
- **Attributed revenue isn't incrementality;** there are no holdout groups.
- **"Minimum 1,000 per variant" isn't statistically universal,** and neither is "one email every 30–60 days". He admits a client's 5th follow-up became their best email. **Copy the testing method, not the tactics.**

## Applied to Velarqo
- **Don't copy his scale or infrastructure.** That would be launch anxiety turned into engineering.
- **Copy the method:** pick the market → map the realistic TAM (UK window & door installers) → write several *materially different* offers and angles → send them → record deep outcomes → double down → always keep ~10% for new experiments.
- **For getting your own clients, measure** qualified conversations and clients won per 1,000 prospects, not reply rate.
- **For delivering reactivation, track** contacts → conversations → qualified appointments → attended → quotes → sales → attributed gross profit. That becomes *your* proprietary dataset and future moat. Questions it will answer later: which first message produces sales, which database age converts best, unsold quotes vs untouched leads, which offer gives the highest profit.
- **His sophistication grew out of real volume.** Build the system after you have the business, not before.
