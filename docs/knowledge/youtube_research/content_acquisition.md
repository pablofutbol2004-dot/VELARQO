## The big caveat: three different buckets of evidence
- **A. Real B2B/service evidence (most transferable):** Jake Trinder (YouTube for professionals, e.g. a doctor), Wes McDowell's service clients (a landscaper going national, a dentist-SEO provider), Rob Walling (B2B SaaS content by awareness stage), Lillian Pierson (SaaS), Final Expense Trainer (insurance), Ben B2B, Christian Krause, HubSpot, Clay, ScoreApp, Sell Better.
- **B. The gray zone:** Taki Moore, SooWei Goh, Nik Setting, Matt Gray, Jeremy Haynes, Jeremy Moser. Real businesses, but they sell to coaches, consultants and agencies. Their content and product fit perfectly: the content *is* a product demo for viewers who are their ideal buyer.
- **C. The entrepreneur-education loop (barely transferable):** Patrick Dang, Corey Ganim, Liam Ottley, Made By Malik, Richard Yu… "make money" content sold to wannabe entrepreneurs. In the corpus, 163 of 206 videos mention a course or community, and only 44 mention concrete professional or local business types.
- **Why this matters:** a window-company owner doesn't binge database-reactivation YouTube. These creators have a global market of compulsive viewers, aspirational content, parasocial trust and one-person purchase decisions. B2B services have none of that.
- **Revenue figures are self-reported,** often with hidden prior distribution (existing audience, lists, ads, referrals) and a strong incentive to credit content. The *mechanisms* are more trustworthy than the *numbers*. Unsuccessful content creators never make these videos.

## What survives the filtering
- **You don't need a large audience.** Optimize for buyer density × trust × intent. 100 views from window-company directors beat 100k from AI bros.
- **The funnel matters more than the content:** attention → credibility → proof → capture → repeated exposure → conversion. Across creators the recurring pieces are proof/case studies (82 videos), DMs (74), CTAs (73), paid amplification (66), email nurture (57), lead magnets (50) and retargeting (21).
- **The content that sells B2B:** problem diagnosis, proof with real numbers, teardowns and audits, proprietary data and benchmarks, demos of the mechanism, and buyer education for decisions people are about to make. Not generic "tips".
- **Long-form beats short-form for niche B2B.** Ranking: YouTube long-form > LinkedIn > short-form as support > Instagram as a standalone B2B engine.
- **Faceless:** weak evidence for B2B, since it reduces know/like/trust. The workable middle ground is **founder-backed faceless**: 80–90% screen recordings, charts, CRM demos, narrated case studies and benchmarks, with your real name, voice and profile attached. **The work is the protagonist.**
- **Organic plus paid:** organic finds the winning messages, and paid amplifies them.
- **Track** qualified impressions → ICP visits → proof consumed → opt-ins/replies → sales conversations → revenue. Not followers.

## Applied to Velarqo
- **Content is not the primary acquisition channel from day 1.** You have no audience, no authority, a narrow ICP and need revenue soon, so it's too random.
  - The order: **targeted outbound** (reaches the ICP reliably) → **excellent niche content** (makes cold prospects think "these people know what they're doing") → **case studies, data and demos** (remove risk) → sales → organic inbound slowly → **paid amplification** later.
- **Put content *into* outbound:** e.g. follow-up #2 is "we analyzed 317 old window enquiries, here's a 6-min breakdown". When a prospect googles Velarqo, they should find benchmarks, case studies and demos.
- **Start tiny:** one strong piece a week, each repurposed into a video, a LinkedIn post, clips, an email asset, sales material and retargeting creative. Ideas:
  - "We analyzed 500 old window enquiries. Here's where installers lose the sale."
  - "What £1m of unclosed quotes looks like inside a CRM."
  - An AI reactivation campaign walkthrough.
  - "Old leads vs new Meta leads: which is cheaper revenue?"
  - "I audited 20 UK glazing companies' follow-up; 17 made the same mistake."
  - "How much revenue sits in a 5-year-old window database?"
- **AI's real advantage:** cheap, research-heavy, visual B2B media (scraped data, charts, simulations, calculators, benchmark reports). Think Bloomberg or consulting research, not talking-head tips.
- **Avoid productivity theatre:** no daily reels, 12 platforms or AI posting agents. That's another way to avoid selling.
