## The core model
Pick one local-business niche → find the revenue leaks → install a standardized acquisition and conversion system → charge recurring fees per location → get deeply embedded → expand across the niche or its networks.

The flow: existing traffic/database → convert/nurture → appointment → show → close → review/referral → more traffic.

The modules ("Aristotle system"):
1. Website and lead capture
2. Database reactivation (SMS, qualify, book)
3. Reviews and referrals
4. Speed-to-lead / new-lead nurture
5. Missed calls / AI receptionist
6. Sales coaching
7. Paid ads, added last

**The AI is incidental.** What he actually builds is a revenue operating system for a small business. The durable part is that businesses are bad at following up with customers.

## Key insights
- **The wedge is not the company.** Reactivation gets you in the door; the full system is the business. On live calls he sells the whole system and says "we aren't running ads yet, too much low-hanging fruit in your database."
- **Order of operations:** extract value from assets the client already has (database, existing customers, website traffic, incoming leads and calls) before buying any traffic.
- **How his strategy evolved:**
  - 2025: niche → simple service → scrape 1,000 leads → cold outbound (calling, email, Facebook DMs; his first 3 clients came from Facebook DMs) → proof → **the franchise network explodes through referrals.**
  - Now: niche → system → Meta ads → sales. Be skeptical of this for a beginner: his example partner had JP's offer, system, ad creative and sales process behind him.
- **His moat:** depth in one niche (gyms for years), productized fulfillment, a high density of proof, distribution through networks and franchises, sales skill (lots of live calls), and being embedded in the client's operations. Systems beat agents because a standalone AI receptionist is easy to replace.
- **Weaknesses:** heavy repetition (one core idea behind dozens of hooks). Statistics like SMS rates and missed-call percentages aren't sourced. "Same system works for every local business" is overstated, because funnels differ: a gym vs a £20k windows job.

## Applied to Velarqo (UK windows & doors)
- **V1:** reactivate old quotes and enquiries (quoted but never closed, enquired but never quoted, cancelled or no response, past customers) aimed at **one** conversion event: book a survey, an updated quote or a callback. Get 1–2 companies real revenue.
- **V2:** instant follow-up and qualification of new enquiries, and booking a survey or salesperson. Velarqo becomes the client's lead-conversion layer.
- **V3:** missed calls, after-hours enquiries, unfollowed quotes, no-shows, stale opportunities.
- **V4:** reviews and referrals.
- **V5:** only then, generating new demand (ads, content).
- **Positioning:** "We install the system that turns more of your enquiries and old opportunities into sold jobs." Later: "We run customer-acquisition infrastructure for window & door companies."
- **Acquisition for the first 1–2 clients:** targeted outbound → pilot → case study → *then* test Meta ads. Outbound teaches you the market: database quality, which CRMs they use, average quote value, objections, and the language that gets replies.
