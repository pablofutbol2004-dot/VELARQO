## Core finding
Home-service businesses **already generate valuable demand, then leak it**: slow response → weak booking → quote issued → no follow-up → dormant data → they buy another lead.

**Velarqo's thesis:** every lead the installer paid for should become revenue, be deliberately disqualified, or stay intelligently nurtured. Nothing should vanish because nobody followed up.

## Windows & doors specifics (inferred from the economics; the corpus doesn't validate W&D directly)
- **Strengths:** a considered, quote-driven, competitor-shopped purchase. Lots of "not now" or "too expensive" leads, and the customer still lives in the service area. Years of old quotes pile up, and the leads were already paid for.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - Few repeat purchases, so **unsold quotes are the goldmine, not past buyers** (use past buyers for reviews, referrals and adjacent products).
  - Old leads decay (bought elsewhere, moved).
  - **Attribution disputes** under pay-per-sale.
  - Results depend on the client's own salespeople, capacity and close rate.
- **Speed-to-lead matters less than in HVAC or plumbing,** since nobody's window is an emergency. **Quote follow-up and long-cycle nurture matter more.**

## Lessons for the product
- **Positioning: "revenue recovery" (9/10), not "AI agency" (4/10).** For example: "We recover sales from leads you've already paid for." Never lead with AI, GHL or workflows.
- **Reactivation principles:**
  - Segment first: unsold quote / no quote / lost / not ready / past customer.
  - Open conversationally ("Did you ever get those windows replaced?").
  - Keep sequences short.
  - Reveal an incentive only if needed, so you don't pay people who would have bought anyway.
- **Recoverable value ≈ eligible dormant opportunities × contactability × response × appointment × sale × gross profit.** Database size alone means nothing: 800 hot old quotes beat 10,000 junk records.
- **Product order:** old unsold quotes → old qualified enquiries → quote follow-up for new leads → lead response and missed enquiries → long-term nurture → reviews and referrals (mentioned in 83 of 136 videos) → only then acquisition.
- **Human in the loop:** AI classifies, routes and books, and **a human sells** the £10–30k conversation. Done-for-you beats dashboards (CRM adoption is hard).
- **Capacity-aware campaigns:** run reactivation when the installer's pipeline is light, pause when they're booked solid.
- **The AI receptionist is commoditized:** keep it as a component, not the core.
- **Moat:** a benchmark dataset of how dormant UK W&D demand behaves (lead age, best first message, quote vs enquiry, season, channel, booking and close rates).

## ICP qualification (what makes a good Velarqo client)
- **Good:** already spends on marketing, has years of history, a real pool of unsold quotes, high job value, outcomes that can be tracked or exported, sales and installation capacity, and a named person responsible for sales.
- **Avoid:** sole traders, tiny databases, word-of-mouth-only businesses, no exportable data, bad reviews, booked solid, or unwilling to report sales.

## Don't trust
Revenue-screenshot titles, "works in every niche", vendor surveys, cherry-picked agency case studies, and precise stats like the "400% drop after 5 minutes" (find the original study first).

**Verdict:** mechanism 8.5, home services broadly 8, UK W&D specifically **~7 (provisional)**. Reactivation as the *wedge* into owning the post-lead revenue system: 9. Reactivation as the whole business forever: 5–6.
