## Core finding
**Don't fix a conversion problem by buying more leads.** The order is: recover existing demand → stop new demand leaking → prove the conversion economics → *then* create demand.

Paid acquisition becomes a calculation: **can we buy extra qualified demand below its expected economic value, within the installer's capacity?** If yes, scale; if not, don't.

These are two separate decisions:
- **Meta for Velarqo to win installers:** no, not now (4/10). With an exact ICP and no proof, targeted outbound beats it (9/10). After a strong case study it rises to 8.5/10, e.g. an ad saying "we recovered 17 attended surveys from quotes this installer had written off", plus retargeting people who opened the email or visited the site.
- **Meta for installers to win homeowners:** later, as a standard module, once the core works. The order is: fix the pipeline (10) → Google Search (9, explicit intent) and referrals (9) → Meta (8.5, creating demand and trust) → SEO (8.5).

## Principles
1. **Measure downstream.** CPL → cost per qualified lead → **cost per qualified attended survey** → cost per quote → CAC → **gross profit / spend**. A £40 lead can beat a £12 lead. Cheap leads from Meta's interruption feed often never answer, are outside the area, rent, or want £150 repairs.
2. **Meta and Google are different jobs.** Google captures people already shopping ("double glazing installers Nottingham"). Meta reaches people who weren't searching yet and builds familiarity. **Velarqo stays channel-agnostic: diagnose first, then pick the channel.**
3. **Creative does the targeting.** A concept = persona × angle × offer.
   - **W&D angles:** energy ("still heating the street through 25-year-old windows?"), noise, appearance (before/after), security, maintenance and condensation, home value, trust and process ("what happens during a 10-window installation").
   - **Personas:** older detached family home, retiree, recent buyer, design-conscious buyer, landlord, single-door replacement.
4. **Every creative is a hypothesis,** not volume. "Energy beats aesthetics for 45+ here" or "showing the install process lifts the qualified-survey rate". AI makes testing cheap; it shouldn't replace thinking.
5. **Budget backwards from the conversion event,** not "$50/day because YouTube said so". Example: 20 qualified surveys × ~£120 ≈ £2.4k before drawing conclusions. Tiny budgets can show creative signals (clicks, forms), never commercial viability.
6. **Match the landing experience to how ready the visitor is.** Google traffic gets a direct quote page. Meta traffic needs more: the problem, why now, proof, the process, then a CTA. Never send paid traffic to a generic homepage. For £10k jobs, a little qualifying friction helps (project questions on the form).
7. **W&D has great raw material:** before/after, condensation and thermal images, the fitting process, finished interiors and exteriors, the crew, real local homes. Faces (owner, estimator) build trust for high-ticket work at the home, and Velarqo itself can stay faceless. **No AI-avatar "homeowners" or fake installers.** Trust is the product.
8. **Offers should reduce friction and be true:** free survey, clear finance, a fixed upgrade, an energy assessment, an installation-slot incentive. Not "40% OFF THIS WEEKEND". The tone is competent, local, transparent, low-risk.
9. **Ad Library = market intelligence, not proof.** Scrape UK installer ads, then tag advertiser, geography, product, angle, format, hook, offer, CTA, days active and recurring concepts. Use it to learn conventions *and* to find what everyone does so you can differentiate. Active ≠ profitable, and long-running ads are only a weak signal. **Never score competitor ads as "92/100, copy it".** Never copy creative word for word.
10. **Attribution:** platform-attributed ≠ CRM source ≠ incremental. Test incrementality with geo tests, holdouts and time series, the same philosophy as the dormant-cohort holdout.

## Constraints when ads become a service
- **It's almost another company:** account access, billing, pixel/events, creative, landing pages, tracking, approvals, account restrictions, creative fatigue.
- **Entry checklist:** live lead tracking, healthy follow-up, sales capacity, known close rate and unit economics, clean attribution, client budget, and a creative pipeline.
- **The client funds their own ad spend:** their account, their card. Velarqo manages it and never finances someone else's lead generation.
- **Capacity-aware:** 40 surveys a week for an installer who can do 15 means wasted spend. Fill sales capacity from **the cheapest profitable demand source**: dormant pipeline → quote follow-up → referrals → Google → Meta.
- **Creative capture SOP per job:** before photo, 2–3 process clips, a product close-up, finished exterior and interior, optional customer reaction, job metadata. That feeds ads, case studies and landing pages, and links to `reviews_referrals.md`: sale → proof → creative → acquisition → sale.
- **Use cross-client learning only as anonymised benchmarks.** Never pass one installer's campaign data to a competitor.
- **The risk from #7 applies.** Adding ads too early turns Velarqo into a generic SMMA (Facebook for one, Google for another, TikTok for a third).

## The full Velarqo ladder (#1–#8)
**Entry:** Dormant Pipeline Recovery → **Core recurring:** lead-to-revenue recovery (fresh leads, missed calls, qualification, appointments, quote follow-up, reactivation) → **Post-sale:** customer compounding (reviews, referrals, proof, creative) → **Acquisition:** demand generation (Google/Meta) → **Mature:** allocate effort and spend across all sources by incremental GP and available capacity.

**Moat:** proprietary data on how UK W&D demand turns into revenue (sources, lead traits, messages, timing, creatives, show rates, close rates, reactivation, referrals, the economics of extra acquisition). Not Claude, GHL, Meta or reactivation on their own.
