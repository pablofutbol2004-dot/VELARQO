## 2026-10-06 business batch (20 videos; review: `wl_reviews/2026-10-06_business.md`)
**Meta:** 6 of 20 are worth it: Alex Beck, Aaron Shepherd, Oliver Rasmussen, Sabri (MoV6OAqRBMA), Alex M H Smith (JZVmL99ClfA, plus the group). Revenue claims are self-reported.
- **Back-solve send volume from target pilots, then check the UK Ltd installer TAM** (Shepherd, Rv_Vm7czhlY [1:02]-[4:10]). Pilots ÷ call→pilot ÷ positive→call ÷ positive share ÷ reply rate = sends. His rates are placeholders; replace them after batch 1. If the TAM is small, invest in accuracy and follow-up, not volume.
- **A short touch the evening before every call** (Alex Beck, pJ7m50Ovuw0 [7:23]-[11:36]): confirm the link, read back their answers, screen "who calls the homeowner back and how fast?", send one proof asset. Log show rate and outcome.
- **Dormant-quote messages need a new reason to reply, not "just checking in"** (Beck [15:46]-[17:52]; Sabri MoV6OAqRBMA [7:20]-[9:26]). Test a finance option, a price-hold date, a free slot, "what changed?". ⚠ Never default to discounting for the installer; price is their call.
- **Test a cold-email arm built on public review data** (Rasmussen, 16t-36TIRE0 [15:44]): their Google review count vs a named nearby installer, a soft "would you mind taking a look at an example?", and a prospect-specific demo ready. Run it as an A/B arm. ⚠ His review gating, mass past-customer blasts and $300/mo with refund conflict with `reviews_referrals.md` / `offers_pricing.md`, so don't copy those.
- **Keep a voice-of-customer file:** installers' exact phrases and objections, plus a one-line "why not now?" from each "not now" reply, and write copy from it (Sabri, MoV6OAqRBMA [4:10]-[5:15]). Cap it at an hour a week. ⚠ His "80% research" conflicts with Sell ≥ 50%.
- **Pitch Velarqo as replacing something:** "stop paying for new leads while old quotes sit untouched", not one more tool. Test one high-friction batch (letter or phone call to the 20 best-fit installers with a mini estimate) against email (Alex M H Smith, JZVmL99ClfA [24:18]-[26:24]).
- **An "approved lines" file:** AI only recombines subject lines, openers and objection answers that already earned positive replies. Write new angles yourself in first person (YuOSyRj3sXg [16:48]-[24:17]). ⚠ This narrows `cold_outreach.md` #13 while there's no proof yet.
- Rejected: "work for free" (Winsbury), "don't revisit goals" and "500–1,000 outreaches a day" (T6_rs_Qf_6A). They conflict with the credited deposit, the kill criteria and the 50/day proof step.

## 2026-10-05 batch (8 videos; review: `wl_reviews/2026-10-05.md`)
**Meta:** only 2 of 8 were worth it (Kruse; one section of Conde). The rest is motivation, filler or YouTube/LinkedIn-only content.
- **Count the funnel by step, hold a weekly output you can sustain** (Kruse, o_5Vtn3OH5E [4:08], [5:11]). Log sent / replied / positive / booked / pilot every day. A slow week means find the broken step, not switch channel. Don't judge a step on a tiny sample (Trinder, UVsIqkkPLYU [1:16:50], [1:27:17]: "the data tells you where to look", [1:29:24]).
- **Run 2-3 competitor "prospect" calls** (Kruse [2:03]): sit through the sales call of vendors selling follow-up/CRM/lead-gen to UK installers and write down offer, price, guarantee, process. On your own calls ask "who else are you talking to for this?" (Iles, VLNCU_ZM0Gk [1:13]). Don't claim "best" without proof (Iles admits it was self-defined criteria [5:23]).
- **Security pass before real installer customer data goes in** (Conde, kg_XlSKjwxk [41:12], [43:18], [44:20]): access limited to named users, no secret keys in frontend code, one account can't read another's data. His "don't worry about data security" [40:08] doesn't apply: UK GDPR (my addition, not in the video).
- ⚠ **Kill-criteria number may be too short** (Kruse [0:00]: first $2,000 deal took 50+ "no" calls and ~6 months; n=1, unverified). Conflicts with the "8 calls with 0 pilots" rule in the 2026-10-04 Robson batch. Decide once, in writing, which threshold you use and why.
- ⚠ **Don't import "airtight before you ship"** (Trinder, UVsIqkkPLYU [23:09]-[24:12], [24:12]) to outreach. It fits public content; for 1:1 outreach it feeds the perfectionism the James Lim batch warns about. Ship, count, iterate.
- ⚠ **Conflict with `offers_pricing.md`:** Kruse suggests "keep working for free until you hit the result" [2:03]. That is an open-ended guarantee; keep the small credited deposit and no numeric guarantee in the first pilots. ## 2026-10-04 James Lim batch (13 videos, one framework; review: `wl_reviews/2026-10-04_james_lim.md`)
**Meta:** 13 videos (4.5 h) on "why smart people don't execute" was itself the fake-action trap he describes. No more of this genre until the first Velarqo pilot.
- **Write plans for the stressed version of you who has to execute them** (D5q9ZM78xnE [13:43]). Each bottleneck gets a simple rule of 12 words or fewer, written in advance, e.g. "Before opening YouTube or VS Code, send today's five emails." Test 3–5 rules and keep what sticks.
- **Dose the scary steps at 4–7/10** (YClPdvhGj4s [17:55]). Rate each outreach step (email → follow-up → call → pitch) and repeat a rung until it's boring, then go up. If a step is only 2/10, the blocker is priority, not fear: grey everything else out instead.
- **The first plan will break by week 2, and that's data** (EbohEKgXyGk [2:07], [8:27]). Reset targets to what you actually did +20%. Never answer a miss with a bigger plan (a_K6ewG6eBY [4:15]).
- **New ideas and apathy at the hard point are the pain trap, not signals** (IMFovk18yuo [6:25]). Write one line in parking.md and review it only on the set decision date.
- **Write 3 counters per recurring excuse, away from the edge** (CZFFWgQpEOQ [32:39]). Keep a **lower limit** for exam weeks and deadlines: 15 min of follow-ups (CZFFWgQpEOQ [49:41], [52:49]).
- **Build a racetrack** (wJsZN5c-zbU [4:13]): send a daily output to someone you care about at a fixed time, with a small real stake. Tighten the environment for outreach: phone in another room, 30-min timer, library.
- ⚠ James suggests *free* pilots to make the step feel safe. Velarqo's pricing digest says a small credited deposit instead (`offers_pricing.md`). Keep the deposit.

## 2026-10-04 batch 2: Robson + Harkness (review: `wl_reviews/2026-10-04_robson_harkness.md`)
- **Your expertise doesn't transfer** (Robson, ZrzakMjeX74 [13:52], [21:19]). Earned dogmatism: research and building ≠ knowing installers. Every pain claim stays a hypothesis until 3+ installers say it in their own words.
- **Write kill criteria before the data** (decision hygiene; extends the tactic tracker). Set thresholds per outreach batch or call series in advance (e.g. "150 sends with <2% positive → change ICP or offer; 8 calls with 0 pilots → re-examine the problem"). For big calls (pricing, ICP, dropping ideas) add a 6-month pre-mortem and third-person framing ("should Pablo drop X?").
- **Liking gap** (Robson [55:12]; Boothby 2018, replicated): prospects like you and will help more than you predict. Always follow up and ask directly for the call or pilot.
- **Time audit + standard week** (Harkness, U9x_GUhRs5A [1:02], [2:04]). Log one week in 15-minute blocks tagged Sell / Build / Research / Admin, and keep **Sell ≥ 50% of business hours until the first pilot**. Fixed days for outreach blocks; Sunday slippage review.
- **A task is under 2 hours with a definition of done** (Harkness [7:16]). "Send 30 emails to verified installers in X", never "improve outreach". Messages in set blocks; write intrusions down.

## 2026-10-04 batch (11 videos; review: `wl_reviews/2026-10-04.md`)
**The pattern:** 7 of 11 videos say one thing: **one goal, sell before you build, stop spreading across ideas.**

### Focus & execution
- **One goal with a hard date** (Dan Koe, rAjMOfKSAks [17:50]). Competing goals split focus. Test: "can my current approach get me there?" If not, change the plan (volume, list, offer), not just the effort. Audit the weekly hours and drop anything that doesn't serve the goal.
- **3 paying customers before building anything** (Jay Yang, ZNl8RFqZJEA [35:29]: "test then invest"). No new Velarqo automation until pilots are paying.
- **Don't hop models too early** (roasbrez, 33s1r1cMFM8 [3:10]): he admits he "probably gave up too soon" when switching models. Stay on Velarqo until the first paid pilot before judging it.
- **Commit to the goal, stay flexible on tactics** (Tristan van der Schoot, 0ZOrTQXabGE). Proof lags effort, but still change the list, subject line or offer when the data is bad.
- **Plan tomorrow's 1–3 *output* tasks the night before** ("send 40 emails", not "work on Velarqo"). Hardest first, time-boxed blocks, a hard stop (Dan Zakaria, yRNOX1_LIOs).
- **Sunk-cost test** (Trey Gustafson, OsMCIeHQRl4 [17:50]): continue only if the current costs are still worth it. Apply it to side projects, **not** to the degree, because architecture is your edge.
- **Scale what works instead of adding new things** (Jeremy Haynes, 7Bp8xf5ebvk [4:11]). Once a channel produces, the "risk" to take is more volume in it, not a new idea or market.

### Selling & offers
- **Do the work up front in pitches** (Jay Yang [3:09]): make it frictionless to say yes. For Velarqo, put a mini estimate in the cold email ("~X old quotes → ~£Z recoverable").
- **Tracker per tactic:** tactic / expected result / actual result / decision (Noah Kagan via Jay Yang [5:15]). Use it for email variants, list sources and the Spain test.
- **The four offer levers** (Dan Koe, XqS7kIWMIDE [9:24]): dream outcome, mechanism, speed, guarantee. Velarqo's weak lever is **speed**, so state "first appointments within X days".
- **Performance pricing lands client #1** (roasbrez [8:22]: 10% of a Black Friday drop). Consistent with `offers_pricing.md`.
- **Don't automate what doesn't exist yet** (Dan Koe [34:44]). Run the first reactivations by hand, then turn the process into prompts by feeding the AI real good examples and your actual steps.

### Content (for later)
- **Show the work** (Ronny Mitchell, 6SwBn8QBMLs [5:11]): demonstration and split-screen formats make money; text-over-footage clips get views but not buyers. Velarqo fits a whiteboard ("your old quotes are a hidden pipeline") or an anonymised campaign build. Biz Labs fits simulation vs reality.
- **Script structure BEST:** bait → elaborate → story → takeaway (Ronny Mitchell [1:03]). Topic matters more than format.

### Communication
- **Shyness is practised behaviour** (Vinh Giang, vb8WJgRAKUU [3:00–6:20]). Check three channels: visual (eye contact, posture, hands), vocal (volume, monotone), verbal (rambling). Before the first sales calls, record a mock call in English and in Spanish and score it. Use a fixed opener structure (problem → proof → pilot) so you don't ramble.
