## 1. Eric Nowoslawski: "7 years of cold email advice" (highest value)

**Diagnose in three buckets, in this order:** sending setup, then the list, then the offer.

**Inbox health, five checks:**
1. A warm-up score under 99% is suspect; pull the inbox at around 92%.
2. Seed inbox tests are only a rough guide.
3. **Weekly per-inbox reply rate:** an inbox that has sent 100 or more emails and gets under 1% replies is cancelled.
4. **Auto-reply seed list.** Save every auto-responder address from past replies. Send to them: about 80% should reply back automatically, and under 60% means kill the inbox.
5. **Use the same inboxes for "positive" campaigns** (partnerships, recruiting) that get high reply rates. That keeps the inbox alive and gives a baseline for comparison.

**Other sending rules:**
- About 30 sends per inbox a day.
- Keep a **50% spare pool** of warmed inboxes so sending never stops.
- Never send from the main domain.
- Use **word-level spintax** (synonym variation), including in the signature and the unsubscribe line. One campaign went from 0.3% to 1.2% replies after adding it.
- Alert on sudden drops. The usual cause is a spam trap, so pull those addresses.

**Lists:**
- Work backwards from where the buyers show up (LinkedIn or Google Maps).
- Guess emails from common patterns and verify them before paying a finder. Data should cost no more than about 2p per contact.
- Clean the list with a cheap AI scoring pass over each company's homepage.

**What a cold-traffic offer needs:**
- It **makes money**, or cuts time or cost by 80-90%.
- It's paired with a **signal** that the firm has the problem.
- It has a relevant case study.
- **The next step is easy to say yes to** (a free sample turned a $300 cost per lead into $40).
- It isn't something they could just Google.
- Personalisation means what you'd say after 10 minutes of research. "Loved your website" doesn't count.

**When to judge a campaign:**
- Don't judge it before **1,000 sends**.
- If 1,000 sends get **1 positive reply, run it to 3,000** before deciding.
- Campaigns "work quickly or not at all".
- Email 1 gets almost all the positive replies.
- Test everything, images included.

## 2. John Danes (4 videos): offer, niche, sales, numbers

- **Niche filter:**
  1. The client makes at least **$2,500 profit per deal, up front**. Lifetime value is harder to sell.
  2. The owner is easy to find.
  3. The client sells through **a sit-down meeting or survey**, so results can be tracked.
  4. The market is big enough.

  A crowded niche is a sign of life. In an empty one, "ask what's wrong with it". Pick a niche and stick with it.
- **Trust comes first.** His cold outreach failed until he rebranded as the specialist for one niche: name, website, demo video, proof. Never fake proof. Understanding the client's money maths is itself a trust signal. Outbound validates the offer; ads only come after.
- **Sales call:**
  - Set the frame first.
  - Rapid discovery: where they are now, their goal, why they're not there yet, their money maths.
  - B2B objections are almost never about money. They're about "will this work?"
  - A short pitch with 3 named parts, then the ROI maths.
  - Any guarantee runs over the whole term.
- **Numbers:**
  - Work backwards from the revenue goal, including churn.
  - Track meetings, booking rate, qualified rate, show rate, close rate and churn.
  - Judge marketing on what it costs to win a client, not what it costs to book a call.
  - Use minimum terms, not month-to-month.
- **19 principles that matter for us:**
  - Validate the message before adding volume.
  - Let the market and the data decide.
  - Test channels, then put the effort behind the winner.
  - The offer should change every 6 months.
  - Below $100k a month, getting clients is the only problem.

## 3. Dylan Sigley: the 3 beginner mistakes

- **Quitting before the sample is big enough.** One student quit after 21 emails, then got to $70k a month at volume.
- **Not tracking each step of the funnel.**
- **Weak follow-up.** At least 3 touches. Every follow-up should add something new, never just "bumping this".

## 4. Carly Meyers: paid AI audit funnel

- Content, then a **scorecard quiz** (low-pressure next step that also qualifies people), then a short free call, then a **paid audit** ($5-10k, credited against the work if they go ahead), then the report, then upsells.
- "Build the tension for free, charge to resolve it": don't give the diagnosis away on the free call.
- A scorecard is a possible low-friction call to action later. Not before the first send.

## 5. Eric Siu (via Ali Abdaal)

- Get good at one thing first.
- **Do the work up front in the pitch** (send a finished sample).
- Aim one level below the top.
- Email team members as well as the owner.
- Ask for introductions at kickoff.
- Aim for 50-60% gross margin.
- Focus.

## 6. Charlie Morgan

- Getting clients is a pipe of small stages. Find the one stage that's broken before declaring a whole channel dead.

## 7. Eetu Häkkinen, Callum Mills (low relevance)

- Useful scraps:

  - Build the guarantee from the most common objections on the first calls (Callum).

## What this changes for Velarqo (applied in `D:\velarqo\docs\PIVOT_DECISION_2026-10.md` §7)

1. **Test two hooks.** "Old proposals" has no proven buyers anywhere (an empty niche: ask why). "Booked walk-throughs" has proven buyers: US janitorial appointment-setting is crowded at about $130 per appointment.
2. **Move the target towards bigger jobs** to meet Danes' $2,500 rule. Small office cleaning, at about £1.2-2.5k gross profit a year per contract, fails it. Fire & security and larger or specialist cleaning firms are more likely to pass.
3. **A niche-specific trust page before sending:** real name and face, a demo of the actual work, no fake proof.
4. **Engine upgrades from Nowoslawski:** the per-inbox reply-rate kill, the auto-reply seed test, word-level spintax, and partner campaigns run from the same inboxes.
5. **Judgement rule:** 1,000 sends per arm. If an arm gets 1-4 positive replies, run it to 3,000 before killing it.
