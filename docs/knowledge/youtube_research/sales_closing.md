## Two camps
- **Get great at calls:** Jeremy Miner, Noah Haupt, Cole Gordon, JP Middleton.
- **Make calls unnecessary:** Tom Youngs, Taki Moore, Adam Erhart. These work with warm audiences, cheap offers, or problems the buyer can see for themselves. In practice they move selling upstream into content, proof, DMs and an offer document. They don't eliminate it.

## Main conclusions for Velarqo
1. **Drop "no calls" as a constraint for now.** The first 5–10 clients should come from **short (20–30 min) founder-led calls that double as market research**. Record them and extract the buyers' questions, wording, risks, attribution and legal worries, and why they said yes or no. Rewrite the email, site, offer and FAQ from what you learn. Automating before you understand buyers means "automating ignorance".
2. **The Fazio objection is the biggest risk.** Owners feel "they're my leads" and assume they would have closed them anyway.
   - **The fix is to define an untouched dormant cohort:** previously quoted or enquired, no sale, no activity for 90–180+ days, not in an active pipeline or follow-up. Without Velarqo, that cohort brings in **£0**.
   - **Position it as "recover opportunities you've already written off"**, not "CRM reactivation" or "database optimization".
   - **Later, measure the lift properly:** split the cohort randomly into a contacted group and a **holdout** group you don't contact, and report the *incremental* sales (treatment minus control). Almost no small-business agency does this, which makes it a powerful case study and proprietary data.
   - Fazio's 4 traits of a good cold offer: new money · done for you · de-risked · low commitment. Velarqo is strong on 3 of the 4; the dormant cohort plus incrementality fixes "new money". Long term, the full lead system (new-lead conversion, then acquisition) removes the objection entirely.
3. **The offer outsells the closer.** Priorities in order: ICP > problem > offer > proof > risk reversal > qualification > process > call skill > tonality. Don't spend 30 hours on NEPQ.
4. **Discovery is the skill to learn,** and it should be numerical, not therapy-style. A 30 Minutes to President's Club–style structure works:
   - **Purpose / plan / outcome:** "I'll tell you if there's nothing worth recovering."
   - **Diagnose, then quantify, then show only the relevant mechanism, then set a clear next step.**
   - Key questions:
     - "What happens to a quote that hasn't closed after 30 days?"
     - "Roughly how many unsold quotes from the last 2 years?" and "What's an average job worth?"
     - "Are they being worked by anyone?"
     - "If 20 homeowners replied next week, could your team handle it?"
     - "Besides you, who needs to be comfortable with a pilot?"
     - "What would a pilot need to show to be an obvious success?" Let the client define the win.
5. **Prevent objections in discovery** rather than handling them at the end. "Need to ask my partner" means you didn't map the decision-makers, and "how do I know it works" means you lacked proof or risk reversal. Price objections are usually about **trust**, not money, so the answer is pilots and lower risk, not better tonality.
6. **Qualify commercially and operationally.** Database size isn't qualification. You need age, type (quoted or not), contactability, existing follow-up, consent, job value, capacity to fulfil, data export, and who closes.
7. **Keep the infrastructure invisible.** Sell recovered revenue. Keep "AI" low in the conversation: SMBs aren't anti-AI, they're anti-weird customer experience. AI works behind the scenes and humans take over when someone is ready to buy.
8. **Ask for the sale, with no proposal theatre.** Fit → pilot terms → simple agreement → onboarding.
9. **Pre-sell with assets** (a 60-second explainer, a mini case study, a diagram, an FAQ, the pilot concept) or a **Revenue Leakage Snapshot** ("send unsold-quote count and average job value and I'll estimate your dormant pipeline"). This is content working as sales infrastructure for outbound.
10. **Track the whole system:** contacts → positive replies → qualified conversations → calls → pilots offered → accepted → launched → client result → revenue collected. Close rate alone misleads.

## Path to async selling
1. **Founder-led:** outbound → 20-min call → pilot.
2. **Presold:** outbound → case-study asset → 15-min call.
3. **Productized:** form → personalized dormant-pipeline analysis → offer page → call only when needed.
4. **Mostly async:** brand, case studies, calculator, standard pilot, transparent pricing.

You have to earn phase 4.
