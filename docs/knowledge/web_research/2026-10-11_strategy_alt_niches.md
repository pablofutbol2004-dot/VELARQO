# Is there a better niche for the "old quotes" mechanism? (web research + own counts, 2026-10-11)

Question: same mechanism (re-contact a firm's unconverted quotes and consultations in their name, book appointments, paid per booking, monthly follow-up later) in a niche with bigger tickets, buyers who already value marketing, structured data, and easy legal reach. UK, with Ireland noted.

Method: about 30 web searches (UK, 2023-2026 sources), plus a new count of active Ltd/LLP companies in the Companies House bulk file of 2026-09-01 (5.69m rows, dormant excluded). Settled work was not redone: `PATH_6_MONTHS.md`, `2026-10-10_countries_and_niches.md`, `2026-10-10_monthly_product_renewables.md`, `2026-10-05_revenue_recovery_offers.md`, `NICHE_PROFILE.md`. Where this research contradicts them it is flagged in section 8.

Source tags:
- **VERIFIED** = official statistics, statute or regulator text, or our own Companies House count.
- **VENDOR** = anyone with something to sell: software firms, agencies, lead sellers, consultancies, brokers, and trade-magazine reader surveys (self-reported).
- **ANECDOTAL** = one firm, one forum thread, one testimonial.
- **GUESS** = my reasoning. Nothing sourced.

---

## 1. Bottom line first

1. **Stay.** Nothing found beats windows plus kitchens plus roofing on the combination that matters to a 21-year-old remote founder with free tools: reachable by legal B2B email, no regulator between us and the buyer, no entrenched competitor selling the same thing. The best "better niche" candidates are **adjacent** (same buyer type, same data pain), not a new industry.
2. **Add, in this order, after the windows slice and the kitchens slice:** (a) **extensions, lofts, garden rooms and orangeries** (bigger ticket, same kind of owner); (b) a **commercial recurring-contract services** slice (cleaning, fit-out, signage, security), mainly because B2B-to-B email removes the consent question on the customer side. Both are single 500-company tests, not pivots.
3. **Do not pivot to dental or aesthetics now.** Dental is the best on paper for data quality and willingness to pay, and the worst on competition and legal friction. It becomes interesting only after there is a named case study, and the offer would have to change (see 5.1).
4. **No evidence anywhere that any niche converts old quotes better.** Every conversion figure below is vendor or anecdotal. The only way to learn is the 500-company slices already planned. Treat this file as a queue for slices 3 and 4, not a reason to change slice 1.

## 2. Facts that apply to every candidate

**Legal, in plain terms** (PECR = the UK email-marketing rules):
- **Cold email to the firm** is fine when the firm is a company, LLP or Scottish partnership. Sole traders and ordinary partnerships in England, Wales and Northern Ireland count as individuals, so they need consent. (ICO guidance via legal commentary, VERIFIED in substance; the ICO pages themselves were not opened.)
- **Messages to the firm's own customers** go out in the firm's name. They rely on the firm's soft opt-in: the contact details were collected in "negotiations for a sale", the message is for similar services, and the person was offered an opt-out when the details were taken and in every message. The ICO's own example says an online enquiry about ordering a product can count as negotiations for a sale, and no purchase is needed (VERIFIED, ICO guidance as summarised by Shoosmiths and Sagacity). **The weak point is "opt-out offered at collection".** Many quote forms and WhatsApp quotes have none. That applies to every niche below and is already in the intake audit.
- **If the firm's customers are themselves companies** (commercial niches), PECR does not require consent for emailing corporate addresses. UK GDPR still applies to named people, usually under legitimate interests. (ICO B2B guidance via commentary; commentators disagree on how far it reaches. Get the lawyer's view.)
- **Health data** is special category data under UK GDPR. A message that says "your implant plan" reveals a health condition. Marketing on that basis needs explicit consent, and "healthcare provision" is not a basis for selling. (Commentary from Sprintlaw and Hamilton Fraser; the conclusion is mine, GUESS on the exact line.)
- **PECR fine cap.** Sources conflict on when the £17.5m or 4% cap started: one says 19 June 2025, others say phased. The HSF page would not load. Keep the existing "verify" note.

**Competition test used below:** is anyone selling "chase your old quotes, pay per booking" in this niche? Nobody found for any trade. For clinics the function exists inside practice software and specialist agencies.

**Seasonality:** autumn is good for most home improvement; December is dead; January rebounds (Business Pilot, GGP: leads almost doubled Dec to Jan 2026, VENDOR).

## 3. Candidate evidence, home improvement

### 3.1 Kitchens and bathrooms (the benchmark)
- Firms: our count of active Ltd with "kitchen" in the name is 9,093 (VERIFIED, noisy: includes worktop and appliance sellers). Bathrooms 3,158 by name (VERIFIED, noisy).
- Ticket: £7-25k kitchens; bathrooms about £7k (prior files).
- **New evidence, kbbreview retailer survey 2026 (trading in 2025; VENDOR, trade-magazine readers, self-reported):**
  - Self-reported conversion of enquiries: 21% of retailers under 40%, 31% at 40-60%, 27% at 60-70%, 21% above 70%.
  - Time from enquiry to confirmed order: 13% under a month, 30% one to two months. By subtraction that leaves 57% taking longer than two months (my arithmetic). Only 6% of kitchen retailers confirm within a month, against 25% of bathroom retailers.
  - Enquiries: 38% up, 37% down. Footfall down at about two in five at the end of 2025.
  - One retailer: about 60% of customers who get a design and quote go on to order (ANECDOTAL).
- Reading: a long, slow decision means many quotes are quiet but alive at day 30 to 90, which is better for a chase than a fast-decision trade. Unconverted share is probably 30-60% of quotes (GUESS from the survey bands). Showrooms use design software (20-20, Planit), which means the quote lives in a structured file (NICHE_PROFILE, VENDOR).
- Verdict: the best fit of everything checked. It is already next on the list.

### 3.2 Extensions, lofts, design-and-build
- Ticket: loft conversions about £50k average per Checkatrade; Velux-type £22.5-30k, dormer £40-60k, hip-to-gable £50-70k, mansard £60-85k+ (VENDOR, Wikipedia/Checkatrade/MyJobQuote 2026).
- Firms: our name-match count for loft, extension, home improvement, renovation, refurbishment is 9,717 active Ltd/LLP, of which 4,274 incorporated before 2022 (VERIFIED, very noisy: includes developers). True addressable is probably 2,000-4,000 (GUESS). A large share of the trade are sole traders (off-limits by email).
- Conversion: no UK benchmark found. Generic installation-business blogs say 15-30%, and 10-20% for new prospects (VENDOR, Zigaflow and similar). One Surrey builder rose to 27% after tightening follow-up (ANECDOTAL).
- Decision length: months. The quote often waits on drawings and planning (GUESS).
- Data: email threads, PDFs, architects' drawings. No CRM in small firms (GUESS).
- Owner: builders are as marketing-averse as roofers; design-and-build firms with an architect or designer on staff are more commercial (GUESS).
- Why it still ranks high: one recovered job is a £10-15k gross profit swing (GUESS, 15-25% project-management margin per Trade2Base, VENDOR). A booked "design consultation" can be priced above a window survey. Nobody sells quote recovery to this trade (no sign found).

### 3.3 Garden rooms
- Ticket: latest Garden Room Guide figure £21.6k for a 3x3m room installed; typical spend £20-35k; range £8k-£150k+ (VENDOR, trade guide; the guide's own figures shift between editions).
- Market: £226m in 2024 (ANECDOTAL: one roundup repeated by others, no named source).
- Firms: only 654 active Ltd/LLP with garden room/office/building/studio/cabin in the name (VERIFIED), 334 of them incorporated before 2022. Real total higher (many registered under carpentry or construction SIC codes) but probably 1,500-2,500 (GUESS). Many are young, online-led and sole traders.
- Marketing: they run Facebook and Google ads and buy leads. High willingness to pay, but ad agencies already own them (Meta Ad Library scan would settle it, not done).
- Conversion: nothing found.

### 3.4 Conservatories, pools, bespoke bedrooms (short)
- **Conservatories/orangeries:** £15-40k (VENDOR, unsourced); Palmer 2017 installed value £1.05bn with volume at a record low of 77,700 units (VENDOR, stale). 604 Ltd/LLP by name, 486 older than 2022 (VERIFIED): an old, shrinking pool, mostly the same firms as the window list. Use as a keyword on the windows list.
- **Pools and hot tubs:** in-ground £30-100k+, above-ground installed £5-10k (VENDOR, ranges conflict). SPATA plus BISHTA claim about 200 members (VENDOR). Biggest tickets, tiny pool (a few hundred true builders, GUESS; our 3,343 name match is "spa" noise). Side slice at most.
- **Bespoke bedrooms:** Which? November 2025, nine traders: 3-door £2,900-£3,900, 4-door £4,900-£6,200 (tiny sample). 1,472 by name (VERIFIED, noisy). A free home design visit is a natural booked unit, but ticket is below kitchens. Fold into the kitchens list as a keyword.

### 3.5 Solar, battery, heat pumps
- Prior verdict stays NO-GO as niche #2. New data that partly contradicts "small and lumpy": **2025 was a record, 257,397 certified solar installations, up 32% on 2024** (MCS, via trade press, VENDOR), average price fell from £10,400 (April 2023) to under £7,600 (April 2025). More jobs, lower ticket, and a falling price per job.
- No installer-side conversion data exists. Same MCS list restriction. 0% VAT ends 31 March 2027 (prior file), a real deadline that can justify a Q1 campaign.
- Verdict unchanged: seasonal campaign, hand-built list.

### 3.6 Stairlifts, home lifts, walk-in baths; driveways and landscaping
- **Stairlifts etc.:** stairlift £1.5-5k (VENDOR, weak sources); 1,203 by name (VERIFIED, noisy). A record of pressure-selling complaints (OFT mobility-aids action; Which? 2019 mystery-shop; a trading-standards prosecution) and elderly, sometimes vulnerable buyers. A chase message here is the worst place for an anonymous founder to be accused of pressure. No.
- **Driveways/landscaping:** mostly sole traders (GUESS); 2,864 Ltd/LLP by name (VERIFIED); lower ticket, no records. Ranks below kitchens, extensions and garden rooms (see section 8).

## 4. Candidate evidence, clinics and others

### 4.1 Private dental (implants, Invisalign, cosmetic)
- Firms: 12,223 UK practices; 7,955 independent, 2,065 small/mid groups, 2,203 corporate (Christie & Co 2025, broker report, VENDOR but a count). England alone: 10,160 practices; the number with no NHS contract rose by nearly 1,000 since 2017 (Nuffield Trust via press, VENDOR). 20% of dentists are private-only and a further 14% are over 75% private (GDC 2026 working-patterns data, VERIFIED). SIC 86230 gives 19,199 active Ltd/LLP, but that includes many one-dentist personal companies (VERIFIED count, misleading as a practice count).
- Ticket: single implant £2,000-3,500; Invisalign median about £2,900, range £1,395-4,495; full-arch £10-18k (price-comparison sites, VENDOR).
- Treatment-plan acceptance: no UK national figure. US vendor blogs say 30-50% average. Breathe (a UK practice consultancy) gives a 30-100% range across its own practices and a 70-85% target outside London (VENDOR). So the unconverted share could be 15-55%, a very wide band (GUESS).
- Data: **the best of any niche.** Practice software (Software of Excellence EXACT, Dentally, both Henry Schein One) has recall tools; EXACT's Care Manager lists outstanding treatment plans on one dashboard (VENDOR). No public API found. Consent for marketing is usually a recorded flag per patient (GUESS).
- Marketing literacy and spend: agency guides say 5-10% of revenue for private-focused practices (VENDOR, no method shown). Implant lead cost £100-350 and consult target £150-250 (Dominate Dental, VENDOR, their own numbers contradict each other, and one case study claims £18.74 per lead: treat as sales copy).
- **Competition: the worst.** Dental marketing is crowded, software does recalls and plan follow-up, and a specialist exists whose whole job is patient reactivation (IDMA; one testimonial of £50 per appointment generated and about £153 return: ANECDOTAL). Our price (£75-150 per booked survey) would be above that benchmark.
- Legal: GDC advertising standards (no misleading claims, "specialist" restricted), CAP code via the ASA (a study of 450 north-east practices found 1.8% fully compliant with GDC web guidance: VENDOR academic paper). Health data as above. Marketing consent must be channel-specific. Needs a data processing agreement and a clinical-data security story that a stranger cannot supply in week 1.
- Fit for an unknown remote founder: poor. Gatekeepers are practice managers and treatment coordinators; the owner's mailbox is full of agency pitches.

### 4.2 Aesthetics
- Firms: 8,752 Ltd/LLP by name (VERIFIED, noisy); 3,924 clinics and 7,892 licensed practitioners per Policybee, 19,701 registered practitioners per a trade estimate, sector £3.2-3.6bn (VENDOR, three different numbers).
- **The Botox rule kills the creative:** prescription-only medicines cannot be advertised to the public, including phrases like "anti-wrinkle treatments" (ASA/CAP enforcement notice, VERIFIED via the clinic-marketing pages that quote it; 88% of 233 London clinics sampled were non-compliant, UCL study via press). Our chase copy would sit right on that line.
- Lapsed-client campaign evidence: Aesthetix 5,692 lapsed clients to 722 booked (prior file, VENDOR); an AI vendor claims 15-30% recovery against 3-8% for generic emails (unsourced, VENDOR, hype).
- Licensing for non-surgical procedures is coming but its start date is unconfirmed (GUESS: avoid regulatory change as a variable).
- Ticket per treatment is low (hundreds), so it is a lapsed-customer play, not a quote play. Already parked in the path file.

### 4.3 Hair transplant, cosmetic surgery, hearing aids
- **Hair/cosmetic surgery:** £3-7k hair (clinic guide, VENDOR). 118 hair-named and 244 cosmetic-surgery-named Ltd/LLP (VERIFIED); IBISWorld 4,299 cosmetic surgeon businesses (VENDOR). Patients visit 3-5 clinics (software vendor claim, ANECDOTAL) and 87% of clinics offer free consultations, so unconverted consultations are plentiful. But the pool is tiny, aggregators and medical tourism dominate, and advertising and CQC rules bite.
- **Hearing aids:** pair about £2,850 private (Which?); private 520,818 units in 2025, up 10%, about 22% of units (trade report, VENDOR). Chains hold most of the private market; 869 Ltd/LLP by name (VERIFIED). Elderly patients, no conversion data. No.

### 4.5 Estate agents (valuations not instructed)
- Firms: SIC 68310/68320 gives 133,491 active Ltd/LLP, but that mostly counts lettings and property companies (VERIFIED, misleading). Real agencies with a site: perhaps 10-15k (GUESS).
- Valuation to instruction: 35-55% commonly cited; 53% with a digital proposal versus 41% without (iamproperty, 750 appraisals, VENDOR); proposals sent within six working hours 73% more likely to convert (Acaboom, VENDOR); portal valuation leads 6% (Homehere, ANECDOTAL).
- Ticket about £3-5k commission (GUESS). Data: CRM-structured (Reapit, Alto etc., GUESS). Marketing literate: yes.
- Trap: a lost valuation is gone the day the vendor signs with someone else. Old unconverted valuations have low recovery value unless the property is still unsold (expired-listing logic, GUESS). Property proptech is saturated. Park.

### 4.6 Solicitors and conveyancing
- Ticket about £1-2k (GUESS). Quote-to-instruction about 40% (one industry estimate), a four-office firm moved from 35% to 70% after training (ANECDOTAL from a trainer). 91% of firms do nothing to differentiate in follow-up (survey quoted by a trainer, VENDOR).
- 2,700 Ltd/LLP by name, 383 of them LLPs (VERIFIED). Data is excellent, volume high. But quotes die fast (a house move has a date), tickets are small, and comparison-site quote leads carry their own consent problems. It is a speed-to-lead product, not old-quote recovery. No.

### 4.7 Car dealers (lapsed service and finance)
- 15,630 used-car dealers in the UK in 2025 (IBISWorld, VENDOR). Dealers lose track of about 15% of customers per year (ADS, VENDOR). Recall-based campaigns: Marketing Delivery 21% booked a recall appointment, Sandown (Mercedes agents) 645 appointments from 5,558 lapsed customers, 11% (both VENDOR, case studies tied to a safety recall, which is a much stronger hook than a sales chase).
- Ticket per service £200-500 (GUESS). Franchise groups buy centrally, independents are small. Low ticket, so this is a lapsed-customer volume play. Parked, as the path file says.

### 4.8 Wedding venues
- Ticket: venue hire £6,040 average, whole wedding £21,990 (Bridebook 2026, 7,000 couples, and Hitched, VENDOR). No national venue register (council registers only; VERIFIED rule, GUESS on count). 1,124 Ltd/LLP with "wedding" in the name (VERIFIED, noisy).
- Conversion: Irish survey of 74 venues, 2023: 34% of venues convert over 30% of enquiries to bookings and 27% convert under 10%; 60% convert over 30% of showarounds (VENDOR, Irish, small). UK consultant rule of thumb: about one in five qualified enquiries books (Indigo Digital, VENDOR). So unconverted is 70-80% of enquiries.
- Natural unit (a showround), high willingness to pay, good data. But enquiries are date-specific and couples who say no usually book elsewhere within months, so the recoverable pool is mostly the recent, still-undecided ones. That is a speed-and-chase product, not a dormant-database product. Many venues are farms, partnerships or hotels, and many accept only form enquiries. A possible seasonal slice in January; not a pivot.

### 4.9 Private schools, boats/caravans, funeral plans (short)
- **Schools/nurseries:** 1,395 ISC member schools (2023, VERIFIED); about a quarter of enquiries never logged or followed up (admissions vendor, VENDOR). Bursar gatekeepers, children's data, annual cycle. Poor fit for a remote founder.
- **Boats/caravans/motorhomes:** high tickets, noisy pool (10,240 by name, mostly clubs and suppliers, VERIFIED), dealers often partnerships. No evidence gathered.
- **Funeral plans:** FCA-regulated, elderly buyers. Excluded on regulatory risk.

### 4.10 Commercial: cleaning contracts, fit-out, signage, security
- Pool by SIC and name (VERIFIED, noisy): SIC 81210/81221/81299 gives 39,058 active Ltd/LLP (12,924 incorporated before 2022); "fit-out/interiors/shopfit" 10,703; signage 2,322; CCTV/security/alarm 1,666. Other sources say 26,576 businesses (IBISWorld) to 75,565 (Policybee) for UK cleaning, nine in ten with under 10 staff (VENDOR).
- Margins 15-25% commercial versus 10-15% residential (Whito, VENDOR). Contract values range from low thousands to six figures. Tender win rate: none found.
- Legal ease is the best: the people on the other end are businesses. Quotes in this trade are proposals and walk-round quotes; the chase is B2B-normal. Booked unit: a site walk-through. Recurring contracts make one win worth 12 to 36 months of revenue (GUESS).
- Weakness: price is the deciding factor in many procurements (practitioner source, ANECDOTAL), a lost proposal is often waiting for an incumbent's renewal date, and B2B lead-gen agencies are plentiful. Unknown how much of this trade keeps quote records at all.

## 5. Why the clinics don't win despite the data advantage

**5.1 If dental were tested anyway.** The "send us your old quotes" mechanism does not work as built:
- Patient lists should not leave the practice system, so the work becomes "done-with-you": Velarqo writes the sequences and the practice sends from its own tools. That is a different business (a consultant selling copy), and it competes with software features the practice has already paid for.
- Pricing per booked consult at £75-150 sits against a specialist at about £50 per appointment (ANECDOTAL).
- Time to trust: DPA, security questions, clinical-data handling, ICO registration, indemnity. A remote founder with no clients has none of the proof.

**5.2 Where the clinic logic is right.** Owners spend on marketing, systems hold the plan data, and unaccepted high-ticket treatment is a real pool (ticket and acceptance both sizeable). If a dentist ever asks Pablo unprompted, take the call.

## 7. Ranking against what we already do, with the cheapest test

**Reference point:** kitchens (29) is the benchmark, followed by windows (25) and roofing (22). Roofing is the weakest on the table; its low score is consistent with `NICHE_PROFILE.md` (35% of roofers formed since January 2025, no records).

### Rank 1: Extensions, lofts, garden rooms and orangeries ("premium design-and-build")
- **Verdict: ADD** as the third 500-company slice, after kitchens.
- Why: same buyer type, 3-10x the ticket, no sign of anyone selling quote recovery to them, and it needs no new product.
- Risks: sole traders (off-limits) are a large share; long decision cycle means the first booked "consultation" may take weeks; the offer ("survey") should be reworded to "design visit" or "site visit".
- **Cheapest test:** build a 500-firm slice from Companies House (names or websites with loft conversion, extension, garden room, orangery, design and build), Ltd/LLP only, 3+ years old, 2+ directors or a named owner, working email. Use `cold_offer_v1` with only the noun changed. No new tooling.
- **Pass:** positive-reply rate at least equal to the windows slice, and at least two replies from firms that hold 30+ open quotes. **Fail:** below half the windows rate, or the replies say "we get all our work by recommendation" (then it is a speed problem, not a quote problem).

### Rank 2: Commercial recurring-contract services (cleaning, fit-out, signage, security)
- **Verdict: ADD as an optional fourth slice**, or skip if the first three produce a client.
- Why: the clearest legal ground (B2B both ways), contracts that repeat, and owners who already sell by proposal.
- Risk: the thing quotes depend on is procurement, not persuasion. Chasing may annoy facilities managers, and we do not know if small cleaning or fit-out firms keep quote records.
- **Cheapest test:** 500 firms, split 250 commercial cleaning and 250 fit-out/signage. Ask one qualifying question in the first email ("how many open proposals have you sent this year?") to size the pool before building anything. Count the share of replies with 20+ open proposals.
- **Pass:** at least three firms say they have 20+ open proposals and agree to a call. **Fail:** replies are "we only do tenders" or "price decides".

### Rank 3: Private dental (implants and aligners)
- **Verdict: PARK until a named case study exists, then test one slice.** Not a pivot.
- **Cheapest test (no data, no patients):** 500 private-focused practices (Ltd, CQC-registered, site mentions implants or aligners, a named owner in Companies House). Email the owner a *different* offer: a free one-page "outstanding treatment plan" audit that the practice runs inside its own software and shares anonymised, with Velarqo writing the follow-up sequence. Count positive replies and ask each respondent which software they use and whether they already pay a reactivation firm.
- **Pass:** at least 1% positive and at least half say they have no reactivation provider. **Fail:** replies mention an agency, "our software does that", or a data-protection concern in the first line. Spend no engineering on it before the signal.

### Where the current three sit
- Kitchens: keep as slice 2, promote to run alongside windows if the first slice is slow (the kbbreview evidence supports it).
- Windows/doors: keep as slice 1.
- Roofing: keep running, but treat as the lowest-value cohort and expect the least data. Do not send more roofers than the plan says until windows or kitchens give a reply rate.

### Ireland
Same B2B rule (Reg 13(4)), closest copy of the UK. Ireland has no register of dental practices (HSE written answer, VERIFIED), so a dental or clinic play there needs a hand-built list. SEAI publishes a searchable directory of registered solar PV companies, which is a free list source if renewables are revisited. No Ireland-specific conversion data found.

## 9. Hype flags (do not repeat these in outreach)

- "Reactivation recovers 15-30% against 3-8% for generic emails" (AI outreach vendors): unsourced.
- "$500k-$1m of unscheduled treatment per practice": a US dental vendor blog, in dollars.
