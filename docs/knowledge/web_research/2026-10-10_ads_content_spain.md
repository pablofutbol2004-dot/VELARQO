P primary, S secondary, V vendor, GUESS = the agent's own estimate.

## 1. Paid ads to win installer clients (UK)
- No benchmark exists for "marketing services to tradesmen". Proxies: Meta UK 2025 median CPL ~46 (currency unstated; B2B verticals 2-6x higher) (S, superads); UK agency guide Meta CPC £0.50-1.50, CPL £5-30+ (V); Google Ads US home services CPC $7.85, CPL $90.92; roofing CPC ~$10.70, CPL $228 (S, LocaliQ 2025 via summaries). Those are homeowner-facing costs. GUESS for installer-facing keywords: £3-8/click.
- Meta targeting: detailed exclusions removed Jan 2025 (P); options consolidated 15 Jan 2026 (S). Employer/job-title inclusion unconfirmed. Realistic: Advantage+ audience, lookalikes from best prospects/customers, interests (Window fitting, Glazing, FENSA, Checkatrade), retargeting.
- Ad Library (UK, "window installers leads", ads started 13 Sep-6 Oct 2026): Glasgrowth, OS Group, Koobr, Home Improvement Growth, Lead Pronto, Lazro Growth, WindowCAD. All pay-on-results / no retainer / money-back / named revenue claims. Glasgrowth already uses "start with the old quotes in your phone" as a hook.
- Minimum test: £600-1,000 over 4 weeks (£20-35/day) → GUESS 15-40 form-fills. Enough to judge lead quality, not close rate.
- Verdict (GUESS): CAC per installer client £500-2,000; probably worse than cold email. Not before: 2-3 named case studies with numbers, a booking landing page with a low-risk offer, pricing that pays back £1,000+ CAC, and a differentiator from Glasgrowth.

## 3. Spain
- Email: LSSI art. 21 covers natural and legal persons (P, AEPD a-00081-2015). AEPD E/01853/2015 cites Audiencia Nacional 371/2012: published company addresses don't entitle commercial email; natural vs legal person irrelevant. Cold email to Spanish businesses, including info@, fails art. 21. Only art. 21.2 (prior customers, similar services) exempts. Vendor "legitimate interest covers B2B email" claims conflict with this.
- Enforcement: no verified 2023-26 AEPD fine against B2B email found; blog figures €2k-12k without case numbers (S/V). Scale: up to €30k minor, €30,001-150k serious, up to €600k repeat very serious (S).
- Phone (correction to the "no cold calling" rule as a legal matter): Ley 11/2022 art. 66.1.b allows commercial calls with consent or another RGPD 6.1 basis incl. legitimate interest. AEPD Circular 1/2023 art. 5 presumes contact data of people at companies, sole traders and professionals lawful under art. 19 LOPDGDD if the offer relates to their professional activity (P, draft text; check BOE-A-2023-15071). Needs a documented balancing test, simple opt-out, Lista Robinson check. One fine PS/00135/2025 €5,000 (S). B2B cold calling to reformas/solar/cocinas firms is arguably lawful if documented; consumer calls are not.
- WhatsApp: equivalent electronic medium, art. 21 applies (€5,000 fine reported, S). LinkedIn: unverified; risky under the same logic; LinkedIn bans automation.
- Verdict: email is out. Legal Spanish channels: documented B2B phone calls, inbound content/ads, referrals and partnerships (associations, software vendors), prior customers. No cheap automated route.

## 4. Other English-speaking markets
- **Ireland**: 1 h behind Spain; CRO open data free and bulk, CC BY 4.0 (opendata.cro.ie, since late 2024) (S); mostly Ltd installers; ePrivacy reg. 13 lenient to corporate subscribers incl. generic addresses (S). **Best second market.**
- **Australia**: 9-10 h ahead; ABN bulk extract free weekly XML (entity type, trading name, state, postcode, no emails) (P); many sole-trader tradies; Spam Act inferred consent only if relevant to role. Workable but awkward.
- **New Zealand**: 11-12 h ahead; Companies Office bulk data + free NZBN API (P); small market. Too small, out of sync.
- **US**: 6-9 h behind; CAN-SPAM opt-out, no B2B exemption needed, postal address + unsubscribe (S); no single registry (state contractor licence databases, OpenCorporates paid bulk); many sole props/LLCs; heavily contested by US lead-gen agencies. Legally easiest, most work; try one state later.
- Order: prove the UK case study first, then Ireland. Ads and Spain are later experiments.
