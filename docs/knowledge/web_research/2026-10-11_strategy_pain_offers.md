# Which problem do UK trade owners feel most and actually pay to fix? Is "quotes going quiet" a painkiller or a vitamin?
Research date: 2026-10-11. Web research only (WebSearch + WebFetch, 2023-2026 UK sources; US data labelled US). Builds on `NICHE_PROFILE.md`, `2026-10-11_niche_ethnography_web.md`, `2026-10-10_competitors.md`, `PRICING.md`, `HARD_TO_FAIL_PLAN.md`; not repeated here.

Labels: **VERIFIED** = I opened the page and the method/sample is visible. **VENDOR** = published or commissioned by a firm selling into this market (biased). **ANECDOTAL** = forum, review, single article. **GUESS** = my inference. "(summary only)" = seen in a search summary, page not opened. The search tool is US-only, so UK price pages (Checkatrade, Lead Pronto) were often not readable first-hand.

---

## 1. Bottom line

1. **No source I found, and nothing in our own files, has an owner saying "quotes going quiet is my problem."** Every number on quiet quotes comes from vendors. Owner voice is still zero. The first 10 calls matter more than anything below.
2. **The problem owners clearly feel and pay for is "not enough booked surveys."** 36% of 2,000+ installers say lead volumes are down, 59% say trading is harder, glazing spends about £100m a year on Google Ads, Checkatrade has 50,000+ members.
3. **"Quotes going quiet" is a vitamin for most owners; a painkiller only for the few who already know their numbers.** The loss is silent, some owners think a customer who needs chasing is not worth having, and templated chasing is bundled free or at £0-44 a month. I found no evidence anyone pays a human service for it. Per-result pricing and a sample audit ("we found 41 quotes worth £X") are the right answer to a weak felt pain, but the **ticket is small (£100-300 a month after a one-off batch).**
4. **New candidate, "quote that sells + same-day"** (owner sends notes and price, we send a branded, explained quote that evening and chase it): the best-evidenced *product* for a monthly retainer. Pain is measured (4% quote same day, 55% say a slow quote lost a job, 77% do admin at night), UK homeowner data backs "show why it costs that" (53% would pick the firm that shows it understood them even if dearer), and I found no done-for-you competitor. Weak spots: no independent proof that nicer quotes win more, window firms already have visual quote tools, and willingness to pay for a service is unproven. Score 32/45.
5. **Current offer, blunt:** standalone old-quote recovery scores **25/45 weighted, mid-pack**, below fresh booked appointments (36) and quote-that-sells (32). Glasgrowth now publicly sells "we work your old quotes first" inside a £1,500 retainer, so it is no longer an empty niche. Keep it as the wedge, not the business.
6. **Top 3 (section 5):** (1) booked surveys as the unit, sourced from enquiries and quotes the installer already owns or pays for, ads only after proof; (2) quote that sells + same-day, as the monthly product; (3) old-quote chase as door-opener and shared engine. Recruitment cannot be delivered remotely without calls; finance and AI receptionists are not worth building around.

**Verdict.** A vitamin for most. The `PRICING.md` design (price per result, audit as the closer) is right. The real risks are "they're my leads" and data-sharing fear.

## 3. Candidates

Two read-outs: nobody has shown me that installers buy this, only that it is advertised; and a credit per missed appointment is a softer cousin of the guarantee `PRICING.md` refuses to give.

**Satisfaction and churn.** Loud and specific: 12-month lock-in with no opt-out, leads that "did not justify the spend" [ANECDOTAL, Trustpilot via summary]. One MyBuilder reviewer (April 2026) paid £380 for 12 leads, got scammers and unanswered calls. Bark credits bought after 1 Nov 2025 expire in 3 months (was 12). Lead Pronto: one buyer says "exclusive" leads were already contacted by others and a promised refund never came; others praise window-and-door leads. **Crowded?** The most crowded segment: Lead Pronto, Leads 2 Trade, doubleglazingleads, Glasgrowth and many "pay on results" agencies in Meta Ad Library. **Remote from Spain without calls?** Partly; ads and WhatsApp booking can run remotely, but it needs ad skill, working capital and quality control; rivals use phone setters. **Cold-email fit:** instantly understood, least believable from a stranger. **Recurring:** strongest. **Legal:** consent as worded on the installer's own forms; ad claims under the CAP code.

### 3.2 Quote follow-up / unconverted quotes (current offer)

- **Competitors:** Sternfenster (bundled), Business Pilot (min £240 a month on 24 months [3P]), Tradify £37+, Powered Now £40, Trade2Base, **Glasgrowth (old quotes first, inside a £1,500 retainer)**, ProfitWize, Honeybee (promotional Construction News piece, October 2025, no results).
- **No independent reactivation benchmark exists**; 2-9% booking claims are vendor-only.
- **Win-rate claims conflict:** Tradify 82% of quotes (3,271 firms, only quotes entered in the system, VENDOR) [https://www.tradifyhq.com/uk/trades-report]; Business Pilot 37-44% of leads; forum tradesmen 10-35%. "Quote" and "lead" differ; none is a benchmark.
- **Kitchens, kbbreview 2026:** 21% of retailers convert under 40% of enquiries, 31% 40-60%, 27% 60-70%, 21% over 70%; the quiet pool is smaller than in windows but cycles are slower [VERIFIED page, sample not stated, https://www.kbbreview.com/?p=80543].
- **Remote:** yes. **Cold-email fit:** good. **Recurring:** moderate. **Legal:** most PECR exposure of any offer here (section 4).

### 3.3 Speed-to-lead, missed calls, AI receptionist

- 38% of quote requesters never heard back (Opinium/HomeServe). 55% of trades admit losing a job to a slow quote (NOW Report, n=140 mixed trades, VENDOR). 72% worry about missing calls (Everreach 2024, VENDOR, summary only).
- Generic first-responder figures (78%, 100x, 391%) come from old B2B studies; a 2026 vendor study (Entagl, 32,581 conversations) says replies in 60 seconds convert at 35% against 7% at 1-24 hours [VENDOR, summary only]. **Not home-improvement data. Do not quote to installers.**
- **Price:** Powered Now Receptionist from £29 a month; Fasthosts £39-69; Sayora £49; Softomate from £299 [VENDOR, summaries]. A **commodity** the owner can buy himself for under £50.
- **Where it is worth something:** as a feature of the booked-survey service. A reply within minutes to the enquiries the installer already pays for (shared with 3-5 trades) is a lever he does not have. Replying to an inbound enquiry is not marketing for PECR.

### 3.4 Getting paid: late payment, deposits, chasing

- Ford Pro (1,000+ incl. homeowners): owed £3,942 each, 35 days late on average, 71% had a refusal [VENDOR-authored]. NOW Report: owed £5,901, 67% had a deliberate delay or dispute (n=140). metals4U (Dec 2025): 66% had payment disputed, 57% wrote off over £1,000 (summary only). HeyBRB: invoicing and payment chasing is the #1 task to automate (13% trades).
- Windows and kitchens take deposits and stage payments on made-to-measure orders; roofers and repairs are more often paid on completion (GUESS), so this hits roofers most.
- Tools with reminders (Xero, FreeAgent, Tradify, Powered Now) already exist. I found **no published price for a done-for-you invoice-chasing service for trades**. At a 10-20% success fee (GUESS) it is worth £400-1,000 a year to a firm owed £4-6k.
- **Risk high:** demands to homeowners in the installer's name, mostly workmanship disputes we cannot judge. Debt collecting for a creditor may fall under consumer credit rules (not verified; solicitor first).

### 3.5 Reviews and reputation

Recommendations beat websites (Citizens Advice 36% word of mouth; HomeOwners Alliance 55% in the top three, 9% websites), but nobody lists "no reviews" as a top complaint. A UK managed Google-profile service is £129 a month (aimed at landscapers); review-request tools $29-39 a month, mostly US [VENDOR]. Already inside Tradify/Powered Now-class tools and our Chase plan. Crowded, easy to deliver, weak hook, £30-130 a month. No gated or incentivised reviews (Google policy, CMA). **A feature, not a product.**

### 3.6 Recruiting fitters and subcontractors

- NFRC Spring 2025 (150+ members): 47% found recruitment harder; cost of employment 77% and recruitment 60% were top challenges [VERIFIED, members skew larger/commercial, https://roofingtoday.co.uk/roofing-industry-facing-slowdown-amid-optimism-in-key-sectors-nfrc-spring-2025-report/]. NFRC 2026: **7 in 10 say labour shortages limit the work they can take on** (summary only). FMB H2 2025 (n=493): 72% affected by lack of skilled tradespeople, 30% paused expansion (summary only). kbbreview: 59% of retailers named "finding a good fitter" their biggest headache in 2021; only 29% use their own fitters (summary only).
- Fees: not found. 10-20% of first-year pay is typical in UK recruitment (GUESS), about £3,500-7,000 a hire. Protect My Install (BiKBBI) is free to retailers (2021 sources).
- **Not deliverable remotely without calls**: sourcing and vetting run on phone, WhatsApp and reputation; hires are lumpy, not monthly.

### 3.7 Finance for homeowners

Only lenders claim an uplift (Novuna: 55% would not have bought without finance; Improveasy: 53% spend more) [VENDOR, summary only]; no independent study. Kanda charges installers about 4.25%; Improveasy is **free to FENSA installers** and pays them on completion [https://www.fensa.org.uk/improveasy-finance-packages-fensa-installers], so there is nothing for us to charge. Introducing customers to lenders is credit broking needing FCA permission or appointed-representative status (summary only). **Rank: last.**

### 3.8 NEW: "Quote that sells + same-day"

**The offer as briefed.** After the survey the installer sends notes, price and photos (WhatsApp, voice note). That evening the homeowner gets a clear branded quote showing why it costs that (what is included, materials and brands, guarantee/IBG, photos of past jobs, reviews, good/better/best, a monthly finance figure), then we chase it to yes or no. The owner supplies the price; we supply the presentation, speed and follow-up.

**Do UK trades quote by text, WhatsApp, PDF?** Yes, in a mix, but no independent split exists. Quoting tools send by email or SMS (Powered Now, branded PDF with open tracking), or WhatsApp link to a quote portal with photos and one-tap approval (Trade2Base; its 98% open-rate claim is VENDOR) [https://www.trade2base.com/help/send-quote-whatsapp]. Window firms: only about 23% price live in the home, over half send a supplier request on every job, about a quarter use fabricator software, so many quotes arrive days later [VENDOR, Tommy Trinder via https://glasstimes.co.uk/products/only-1-in-4-installers-quote-on-the-night/]. US survey (ResearchScape for Arcsite, 1,000+ homeowners, late 2021, **US**, VENDOR): 95% get more than one bid, 28% expect a quote on the spot or the same day, 75% prefer a digital quote, top choice factors are price, referrals, professionalism and presentation, and examples of past work [https://www.arcsite.com/blog/2022-homeowner-survey-report].

**Pain evidence (UK).**
- Only 4% quote the day of the visit, 27% within 24 hours, 55% say a slow quote lost a job; 77% do admin in the evening, 5-8 hours a week (NOW Report n=140, HeyBRB n=167, VENDOR).
- Keystone UK Consumer Fenestration Trends Report 2026 (sample not stated; 2025 series drew on 5,300 homeowners; VENDOR-sponsored): only 7% say salespeople addressed what mattered most "very well"; 92% would pick the firm that showed understanding of their needs and style when products are similar; **53% would pick it even if it cost more** [https://glasstimes.co.uk/news/keystone-report-shows-installers-dont-listen/]. This supports "show why", though it is about the in-home visit, not the document.
- Installers' biggest obstacle is competitors selling too cheaply, 77% (Windowlink/Insight Data 2017, old, ethnography file).

**Evidence that better quotes win more: weak, and mostly vendor/US.**
- Tiered pricing "raises average ticket 15-25%" (attributed to HBR, not opened), 18-22% (ServiceTitan, per a third party), 23% (DripJobs). ServiceTitan's own page cites no data; its "customers pick the middle" claim is unsourced [US, VENDOR]. Digital proposals "close 32-48% against 17-22%" is cited without a verifiable source (Pipelineon, US, VENDOR). Some contractors report "analysis paralysis" from too many options.
- BuildOps (US contractor software) says average contractors convert 48.9% of quotes and best-in-class 62.8%, with quote speed the biggest driver; method unverified [US, VENDOR, summary only].
- **No UK independent study found.** Treat all of this as plausible, untested for our trades.

So in windows the "good-looking quote" gap is partly served for about 5% of firms by tools the owner drives himself; in **roofing and kitchens I found no UK-specific equivalent**, and almost nothing does the writing for him.

**Would owners let someone else write and send their quotes?** I found **no survey**. Reasons to hesitate (GUESS): the quote carries his name and price, he worries about wrong scope or promises, and he does not like strangers near the customer. Reasons to say yes: a spouse or office partner often does the paperwork (a quarter of our list's firms are family pairs), 20+ minutes per quote, and the owner keeps control if he supplies the price and approves the PDF in one tap. Ask this directly on calls.

**Pricing benchmarks.** UK VAs £12-18 an hour (trade blog) to £15-35 (VA agency guide), £28-45 London [VENDOR, summaries]. 6-10 quotes a week at 20+ minutes is 2-3 hours, about £130-325 a month of raw typing at those rates (my arithmetic), before design, photos and chasing. Quote-builder apps cost from £23 a month, Tommy Trinder £100+VAT, Business Pilot £240+. No UK per-quote price for outsourced quoting found (SIG Roofing offers estimating tied to its products, no price shown). A sensible band to test is **£250-500 a month, or per quote sent**, against Glasgrowth's £1,500 fee for 10 appointments (my GUESS; owners spending under £50 a month on software will balk).

**Score reasoning.** Pain 4 (measured, but vendor). WTP 3 (installers do pay £100-240+ a month for quote tools; a human service is untested). Ticket 3 (£3-6k a year). Competition 3 (window tools exist, nobody does it for them). Deliverability 3 (voice notes, WhatsApp and design work remotely; windows need the fabricator price from the owner; no calls needed, but depends on owner discipline). Cold-email fit 4 (a sample quote beats a pitch). Recurring 5.

**Legal and honesty constraints.**
- **A monthly finance figure is a financial promotion.** It needs lender-supplied, compliant figures and wording (FCA rules; GUESS, solicitor to confirm); safest is to link to the lender's own calculator or omit it.
- Guarantee/IBG, brand, "includes" and review claims must be true for that installer; photos and reviews need his permission and real source; CMA review rules apply.
- Quotes are customer service, not marketing. **The chase is better placed legally than old-quote chasing**: the opt-out can be offered when the survey details are taken ("we'll send your quote and follow up twice; reply STOP"), which fixes the soft opt-in weak point.
- Made-to-measure and off-premises rules (14-day cancellation, not for made-to-measure) belong in the quote terms; the installer's lawyer or trade body (FENSA/Certass, GGF) should supply the template.

### 3.9 Other loud complaints

- **Wasted surveys, time-wasters.** Powered Now claims 5m+ Britons asked for quotes with no intent to hire (£776m-1.75bn of trade time, extrapolated, VENDOR); IronmongeryDirect 2022 (n=500): 14% name customers not being home. **Sellable as a feature of booking:** qualify by text (budget, timeline, photos), confirm and remind.
- **Costs.** 86% of roofers saw materials rise (NFRC); 88% of kitchen retailers saw operating costs rise (kbbreview). Not addressable.
- **Succession.** 25% plan to retire within five years; 69% have no successor (Screwfix, VENDOR). Too niche.

## 4. Legal frame (PECR/GDPR)

- **Corporate email.** Unsolicited B2B email to companies and LLPs is allowed with sender identity and an opt-out; sole traders and some partnerships count as individuals [VERIFIED, ICO, https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/]. Matches our rule.
- **Emailing or texting homeowners.** Needs consent or the **soft opt-in**: they bought or "negotiated to buy" something similar from the sender, were offered an opt-out **when their details were collected**, and every message carries one [VERIFIED, same page]. The page does not name quotes, so a quote probably counts but I could not confirm. **Weak point: if the installer's original quote form had no opt-out, the soft opt-in may fail for old quotes.** That hits old-quote recovery hardest; speed-to-lead replies and quote-that-sells (opt-out added at intake) much less.
- **Roles.** Installer is controller, we are processor under a written agreement; confirm with the UK lawyer.
- **Post** is outside PECR's electronic rules (UK GDPR still applies). Phone is out for us.
- **Fines.** The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 aligns PECR penalties with UK GDPR (up to £17.5m or 4% of turnover); start date disputed in sources (summary only). Verify on legislation.gov.uk.
- Finance promotions, debt chasing and recruitment each have their own regimes (not researched in depth).

### Ranked top 3

**1. Booked surveys as the product, sourced from what the installer already owns or pays for (row 1 entered through rows 2 and 3).**
The only problem with proven spend (£100-7,000 a year) and a unit the market buys (about £130, Glasgrowth £75-150). Your pay-per-booked-survey model is right; the *source* is too narrow. Old quotes are a one-off pool; live enquiries (web, Checkatrade, MyBuilder, missed calls) and every new quote are a monthly pool he already pays for and under-uses. Test fresh ads at one or two installers only after pilots show survey-to-job rates. Don't lead with ads: most crowded segment, needs budget and skill, and "I'll get you appointments" from a stranger is the least believable claim in the inbox.

**2. Quote that sells + same-day, as the monthly product (row 8).**
The best fit for what `PRICING.md` calls the monthly tier. "Chase + Answer" sells missed-call text-back, which is a commodity under £50. This sells something nobody does for them, attacks the most-measured pain, has a visible proof (a sample quote), a fresh opt-out at intake, and a natural monthly retainer. Doubts: no independent proof that nicer quotes win, owners may not hand over quoting, and willingness to pay is untested. Cheapest test: two free weeks for 2-3 roofers (no UK roofing quote tool found), owner supplies the price, we send within two hours, track won/lost against his last 20 quotes. For window firms using Tommy Trinder, pitch the speed and chase, not the look.

**3. Old-quote chase as door-opener and shared engine (row 2).**
Near-zero extra cost once 1 and 2 exist, risk reversal built in, the audit is a strong opener. Third because standalone it is a vitamin with a small ticket, the most PECR exposure, and now a priced competitor. It should not be what the business is about in month 7.

**Why not the rest?** Recruiting: undeliverable without calls. Payment chasing: likeliest way to damage an owner's name. Finance, AI receptionists, reviews: lender-paid or sub-£50 commodities and features.
