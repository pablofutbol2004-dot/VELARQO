# Ireland as second market: register, activity codes, email law (verified 2026-10-10)

Scope: can we build an Irish Ltd-company list for free, tell companies from
sole traders, and email them under Irish law the way we email UK Ltds under
PECR. Everything below was checked on the web on 10 Oct 2026; tags: (P)
primary source read directly, (D) checked against the downloaded data,
(S) secondary source or search snippet only. Nothing was sent.

## 1. CRO open data (the register)

**Portal and licence (P).** https://opendata.cro.ie/dataset/companies ("Company
Records"). Licence shown on the dataset page: **CC-BY-4.0**, update frequency
**daily**; rights note: "Under the CC-BY Licence, users must acknowledge the
source of the Information in their product or application." The same dataset
is mirrored on data.gov.ie (publisher: Companies Registration Office), which
supplies the attribution line to use when none is given: *"Contains Irish
Public Sector Data licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0
International (CC BY 4.0) licence"*, and flags it as an EU High Value Dataset
(Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/138) (S, data.gov.ie listing via search).
No key, no sign-up, no rate limit for the bulk file. A CKAN datastore API
(`datastore_search`) exists on the same portal (S) but is not needed.

**File (D).** `companies.csv.zip`, 47.8 MB zip, 193.6 MB CSV, one file. The
download URL redirects to a signed object-storage URL that expires in an
hour, so a `HEAD` fails with 403 but a normal `GET` works. Downloaded
2026-10-10 19:01: **825,177 rows** (every company ever registered, live and
dissolved).

**Columns (D, from the file header):**
`company_num, company_name, company_status_code, company_status,
company_type_code, company_type, company_reg_date, last_ar_date,
company_address_1..4, comp_dissolved_date, nard, last_accounts_date,
company_status_date, nace_v2_code, eircode, company_name_eff_date,
company_type_eff_date, princ_object_code`. The portal page carries no data
dictionary; "nard" is the next annual return date (matches values seen).
Values have trailing spaces (`"Normal "`); strip before comparing.

**Status (D).** Live companies are `company_status = Normal` (codes 1151,
1051, 1153, 1141, 1053 all read "Normal"): **326,469** of 825,177. Everything
else is Dissolved (455,929 + 17,653 "Dissolved-20 years"), Strike Off Listed
(9,040), Liquidation (9,000+), Ceased, Struck Off, etc.

**Type (D).** `company_type` is text, e.g. `LTD - Private Company Limited by
Shares` (289,287 live), `CLG - Company Limited by Guarantee` (18,448), `DAC -
Designated Activity Company (limited by shares)` (7,277), `ULC - Private
Unlimited Company` (5,301), `External company` (3,632), `PLC` (374), plus
funds, SEs, EEIGs. The CRO publishes the code list at
services.cro.ie/companytypes.aspx (S: blocked for automated fetch; codes
1153 LTD, 1150 DAC, 1148 CLG, 1157 ULC, 1154 PLC match the file).

**Activity code: NACE exists, with gaps (D).** `nace_v2_code` is a NACE Rev 2
4-digit class stored as a float string ("4332.0"). It is filled for
**208,356 of 326,469 live companies (64%)**; the rest (older incorporations)
have none. Companies must give a NACE code on the incorporation form A1
(cro.ie "activity in the State" page, S: snippet only, page blocks fetch).
A third-party guide claiming the CRO has no activity codes is out of date.
Relevant classes and live counts: 4332 joinery installation 734; 4334
painting *and* glazing 591 (shared class, name must say glazing); 4391
roofing 358; 2512 metal doors/windows 48; 1623 builders' carpentry 208;
2223 plastic builders' ware (uPVC) n/a as separate count; 4752 hardware,
paints and glass retail 122; 3102 kitchen furniture 94; 4399 other
specialised construction 3,502; 4120 building construction 6,294.

**Address (D).** Registered office (often an accountant's), four free-text
lines; `company_address_4` is usually "TOWN, COUNTY, IRELAND" or
"Town,COUNTY". `eircode` is filled for **204,421 live companies (63%)**.
No phone, email or website anywhere in the file.

## 2. Companies vs sole traders

- The CRO company register holds only bodies corporate formed under the
  Companies Act 2014; sole traders are not companies and never appear in
  `companies.csv` (P: file; S: CRO/third-party guides). Every row has a
  `company_type`, so the filter is: `status = Normal` and type in {LTD, DAC
  (shares), ULC, PLC, private-limited-by-shares legacy types}. CLGs
  (clubs, charities, management companies), external companies (foreign
  branches), funds and SEs are excluded.
- Sole traders and partnerships trading under a name other than their own
  register that name on the separate **Register of Business Names**
  (Registration of Business Names Act 1963, form RBN1; companies use RBN1B)
  (S: enterprisenation, irishformations, openforest). A sole trader trading
  under their own name need not register at all (S, sources conflict on
  whether registration is "optional"; irrelevant for us because we never
  use that register).
- Practical consequence: an Irish list built only from `companies.csv`
  contains no sole traders by construction. What it can still contain is a
  one-person Ltd whose only mailbox is `firstname@`; the UK rule applies
  (a Ltd is a corporate subscriber, the person is the contact) but the
  Irish reg. 13(2) wording below makes the business-address point explicit.

## 3. Irish ePrivacy Regulations: S.I. No. 336/2011, Regulation 13 (P)

Read in full at irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2011/si/336/made/en/print on
2026-10-10 (the site blocks scripted fetches; read in the browser).

- **13(1)** Unsolicited direct marketing by email to a subscriber or user
  **who is a natural person** needs prior consent ("unless the person has
  been notified by that subscriber or user that he or she consents").
- **13(2)** Email to a natural person does *not* count as 13(1) marketing
  where the address "reasonably appears to the sender to be an email
  address used mainly by the subscriber or user in the context of their
  commercial or official activity and the unsolicited communication relates
  solely to that commercial or official activity". This is the sole-trader /
  named-employee route; it is "subject to paragraph (4)".
- **13(4)** Email to a subscriber or user **other than a natural person**
  (a company) is prohibited only "where the subscriber or user has notified
  the person that the subscriber or user does not consent". So: companies
  may be emailed until they object. There is no "generic address only"
  rule in the text; `info@company.ie` and `john@company.ie` are both
  addresses of the corporate subscriber, and 13(2) covers the named one
  when the message is about the business.
- **13(10)(c)** Every marketing email must "include a valid address at
  which that person may be contacted".
- **13(12)** It is an offence to send marketing email that "(a) disguises or
  conceals the identity of the sender on whose behalf the communication was
  made" or "(c) does not have a valid address to which the recipient may
  send a request that such communication shall cease" (opt-out address).
  13(12)(b) also bars breaching reg. 8 of the E-Commerce Regulations 2003
  (commercial communications must be clearly identifiable as such and say
  who they are from).
- **13(13)** Each email sent in breach is a **separate offence**.
- **13(14)** Where consent is in issue, **the onus is on the sender** to
  prove the recipient "unambiguously consented".
- **13(15)** Penalty: summary conviction a class A fine (up to €5,000);
  on indictment up to **€250,000 for a body corporate** or €50,000 for a
  natural person. 13(16) lets the court order the data erased.
- **13(18)** An email address or phone number of a subscriber/user is
  deemed personal data, so GDPR (Art. 6(1)(f) legitimate interests, Art. 21
  right to object) applies to the named-contact processing exactly as in
  the UK; the existing `docs/legal` legitimate-interests assessment covers
  the same reasoning.
- Reg. 2 definitions: "user" is "any natural person using a publicly
  available electronic communications service, for private or business
  purposes"; "electronic mail" includes SMS. "Commissioner" is the Data
  Protection Commissioner (now the DPC), who enforces and prosecutes.

**How this compares with UK PECR reg. 22 (P, both read):** same structure
(individual subscribers need consent, corporate subscribers do not; identity
and opt-out mandatory; sole traders are individuals). Differences: Ireland
has the explicit 13(2) business-address carve-out for natural persons
(PECR has none, the ICO treats sole traders as individuals full stop),
each email is a separate criminal offence with the burden of proof on the
sender (PECR is a civil monetary penalty regime), and the DPC prosecutes
in the District Court rather than fining administratively.

## 4. DPC guidance

- **DPC "FAQ on Consent for Electronic Direct Marketing", April 2020 (P,
  PDF read):** restates that the general rule is affirmative consent under
  reg. 13, describes the existing-customer exception in 13(11) (own similar
  products, chance to object at collection and in every message, first
  contact within 12 months), and says "a facility to opt-out must be
  included with each marketing communication" and "It is a criminal
  offence to send direct electronic marketing to a person who objects". It
  is written for retailers emailing consumers and **does not discuss
  corporate subscribers at all**.
- The DPC's web page "Rules on electronic and direct marketing" and its
  e-receipts guidance exist but the site returns "Access denied" to
  scripted and in-app browsers (checked twice 2026-10-10); read it by hand
  before the first Irish send.
- **Secondary confirmation (S):** DLA Piper "Electronic marketing in
  Ireland" (updated 17 Jan 2025): B2B marketing email "can generally be
  sent unless the recipient has informed the sender" it does not consent;
  the address "must reasonably appear to the sender to be an email address
  used mainly by the recipient in the context of their commercial or
  official activity" and "the marketing message must relate solely to that
  commercial or official activity"; "the identity of the sender must not be
  disguised or concealed and the recipient must be offered an opt-out";
  maximum fines €5,000 per message summary, €250,000 per message (company)
  on indictment. Enforcement is real: the DPC prosecuted Stella Novus in
  October 2024 for emailing after an opt-out (S, web60.ie blog; verify on
  dataprotection.ie before quoting).
- UCD/Griffith lecture notes (S) warn that B2B treatment differs across EU
  states; Ireland sits with the UK, not with Germany/Netherlands.

## 5. What this means for Velarqo (decisions, not law)

1. **List: free and clean.** One daily CC-BY file, no key, 326k live
   companies, NACE codes on 64%, Eircodes on 63%, no sole traders. Needs
   our own website/email finding, as in the UK.
2. **Law: same shape as UK PECR, slightly stricter in enforcement.** Email
   Irish Ltds (reg. 13(4)) until they object; include sender name,
   postal address and a working opt-out address in every email (13(10),
   13(12)); treat sole traders as consumers (never on our list anyway);
   keep the suppression list airtight because each extra email after an
   objection is a separate criminal offence.
3. **Attribution:** the CRO licence line must appear wherever the data is
   shown or exported (CSV header comment, docs, any client-facing report).
4. **Open points before any send:** read the DPC marketing page by hand;
   decide the sender's Irish-facing identity (Velarqo is Spain-based, a
   postal address is mandatory); confirm the E-Commerce Regulations reg. 8
   disclosures (clearly identifiable as a commercial communication, name of
   the sender) are satisfied by the current footer.
