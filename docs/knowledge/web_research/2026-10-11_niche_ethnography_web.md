# UK window/door installers, roofers and kitchen fitters: who the owners are and how they sell
Research date: 2026-10-11. Web research only (WebSearch + WebFetch). Purpose: stop being an outsider to the owners Velarqo sells quote follow-up to.

Labels: **VERIFIED** = survey or dataset I opened myself and the method/sample is visible. **VENDOR** = published by a firm that sells into this market (treat as biased). **ANECDOTAL** = forum post, single article, review, trade-press opinion. **GUESS** = my inference, not sourced. Where a figure came only from a search-engine summary and I did not open the page, I say "(summary only)".
Tags look like [publication, year, URL]. Samples are shown where the source gave one.

Related files already in this folder: partners_fabricators (who the system houses and fabricators are), competitors, partners_vendors_crm_roofing_bodies. I do not repeat them; I cross-reference.

## 0. Headline correction to three assumptions in the brief
1. **Windows are not slowest in Jan-Feb.** Business Pilot's monthly data (a CRM used by window installers) shows December as the trough (leads down about 40% on November, sales down about 43%) with January bouncing back (sales +47% in Jan 2022, leads +111% in Jan 2021, +97% in Jan 2020) [Business Pilot Barometer, 2020-2023, https://businesspilot.co.uk/barometer/seasonal-adjustment/ and https://glasstimes.co.uk/featured-articles/busy-but-are-you-profitable/]. VENDOR, own-user data, no sample size shown. Autumn is strong: homeowners want jobs finished before year end [Business Pilot Barometer Oct 2025, summary only].
2. **Checkatrade is not a flat "£100+/month".** Published tiers (per a UK trades-marketing site, checked July 2026): Free £0 (profile and reviews only), Approved £30/month (no search placement, no leads), Growth "from £59 a month" but really quoted per trade and postcode, Sponsored listings from £100/month pay-per-click. Members report all-in spend of £100-£300/month, 12-month minimum, auto-renew, cancellation by phone only in a window about a month before renewal [Whito, 2026, https://whito.co.uk/trades/tools/checkatrade-cost-uk/]. ANECDOTAL/secondary: Checkatrade's own pricing page returned 403 to me, so I could not confirm the tiers first-hand. Leads are reportedly shared with 3-5 trades (member-reported).
3. **The 14-day cooling-off is not automatic for windows.** Off-premises contracts do get 14 days under the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, but Which? says made-to-measure windows and doors carry no legal right to cancel (firms may offer one anyway) [Which?, https://www.which.co.uk/reviews/double-glazing/article/how-to-buy-double-glazing/your-rights-when-buying-double-glazing]. VERIFIED (consumer guide, not legal advice). Cancelling also ends any linked finance.

## 1. Who the owner is

**Age and exit.** The best UK trade-wide data is Screwfix's Voice of the Trade 2025: 34% of respondents aged 55-64 (largest group), only about 24% under 44, 25% plan to retire within five years; 54% of respondents were "just me" businesses; fewer than a quarter of sole traders use an accountant. Sample about 681-700 tradespeople, a retailer's customer panel, not a representative sample [Screwfix Voice of the Trade Manifesto 2025 and trade coverage, https://media.screwfix.com/is/content/ae235/PDF_Download/voice_of_the_trade_manifesto_2025.pdf; https://www.insightdiy.co.uk/articles/screwfix-voice-of-the-trade-2025/1437.htm]. VERIFIED for the sample, skewed to Screwfix shoppers. The 2024 edition (n over 1,000) found over 60% were over 45 and that 69% had no one to pass the business to [Powered Now summary, https://powerednow.com/screwfix-voice-of-the-trade-report-2024/].
Whole construction workforce: 34% over 50, close to the all-industry 33% (June 2024) [Gov.uk Sector Skills Needs Assessment, https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/683d6945b1ba4615aec80e26/Sector_skills_needs_assessments_Construction.pdf (summary only)].

**Gender.** Women are about 15% of construction employment (2024; 14.8% in Q2 2026 per a Mitsubishi Electric industry blog) and far fewer among owners of installation firms [gov SSNA above; https://les.mitsubishielectric.co.uk/the-hub/time-to-face-the-jobs-facts, commentary]. Self-employed overall are 61% male / 39% female (all sectors) [IPSE Self-Employed Landscape 2023, https://www.ipse.co.uk/campaigns/the-self-employed-landscape/self-employed-landscape-2023]. GUESS: owners of window, roofing and kitchen-fitting firms are over 90% men; I found no source for that.

**Team size.** Roofing is the smallest: the average UK roofing business has about 3.4 employees [IBISWorld, https://www.ibisworld.com/united-kingdom/industry/roofing-activities-in-the-uk/2550/, VENDOR, summary only]. Estimates of roofing workforce self-employment run 50-70% (Roofing Today 2026 says about 70% of roughly 39,000-40,000 roofers) [https://roofingtoday.co.uk/roofing-is-sleepwalking-into-a-crisis/, summary only; ANECDOTAL because estimates conflict]. Kitchen retail is bigger and showroom-based: 77% of independent kitchen/bathroom retailers run one site, 17% more than one, 6% none; 44% sell only or mostly kitchens (kbbreview 2024, n over 500, run by Eureka! Research) [https://www.kbbreview.com/59210/research/the-kbbreview-retail-survey-2024-the-biggest-challenges/ and summary of retailer survey 2024]. A 2026 independent kitchen retailer survey covered 250 firms with up to 20 staff and under £10m turnover; 46% reported falling sales [LDD/Censuswide via Interior Daily, 2026, https://www.interiordaily.com/article/9811425/ , VENDOR-commissioned].

**Turnover.** Simply Business, 2025 average pre-tax turnover from insurance quote forms (only trades with over 3,000 quotes): roofers £60,391 (2024: £56,799), glaziers £101,037, bathroom/bedroom/kitchen fitters £85,872, carpenters £66,458, builders £113,462. Window fitters not listed [https://www.simplybusiness.co.uk/knowledge/trades/top-earning-trades/]. VERIFIED method, but skews to sole traders and micro firms who buy insurance online. Tradify (3,271 UK trade businesses on its software, June 2025-May 2026): average invoiced revenue up 14-25% year on year, sole traders over £100,000 in every trade analysed, top sectors near £2m; no roofing/windows line in the text [https://www.tradifyhq.com/uk/trades-report, VENDOR, software users only]. Roofers had the biggest turnover fall in 2024 (-30% from £81,517 in 2023) per the Simply Business series [rcimag summary, https://rcimag.co.uk/news/roofers-hit-hardest-in-2024-turnover-decline-report-finds, summary only].

**Background: tools or business?** I found no survey. Pattern evidence is anecdotal: the roofing profile I found (DPR Roofing) has the MD leaving school at 16 to work for his father's roofing firm, buying a van and ladders at 22 and growing to a few staff [Which? Trusted Traders profile, https://trustedtraders.which.co.uk/businesses/dpr-roofing/, ANECDOTAL, self-reported]. Window firms split between (a) tradesmen who fit and sell themselves, (b) sales-led retailers with showrooms and reps (see section 3), and (c) family fabricator-installer groups. Kitchen showroom owners are more often retail/design people; fitters are usually self-employed subcontractors (GUESS, no survey). Education: **no data found**.

**Attitude to tech.** Mixed and self-reported. Google/Screwfix research (July 2026) claims 86% of construction workers use AI at work but only 16% see significant value; Google-commissioned, so VENDOR [https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/around-the-globe/google-europe/united-kingdom/ , summary only]. ToolTime (a job software seller) says 89% use digital tools for daily operations, while its CEO says many still run on pen and paper, Word and Excel [https://www.electricaltimes.co.uk/the-end-of-do-it-yourself-new-tooltime-research-reveals-younger-generations-call-on-tradespeople-more-than-twice-as-often-as-their-parents/, VENDOR]. Read as: they all have smartphones and use them; few use systems.

## 2. How they get work

- **Word of mouth and repeat custom dominate on the homeowner side.** Citizens Advice/Opinium, 13 Feb-4 Mar 2026, n=5,000 UK adults responsible for home repairs: 36% of people who used a trader in the last 18 months found them through someone they know [https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/about-us/media-centre/press-releases/home-repair-problems-hit-one-in-four-homeowners/]. VERIFIED. A trades-marketing site reports the same survey as also showing 27% used a trader they had hired before, 16% used a recommendation site like Checkatrade, 11% social media, 7% an accredited scheme, and 87% had heard of Checkatrade or MyBuilder [Whito, 2026, https://whito.co.uk/trades/research/uk-trades-marketing-statistics/]. I could only confirm the 36% on the primary page, so treat the other splits as secondary. HomeOwners Alliance/Opinium (Feb 2026, n=834) via Whito: 55% put recommendations in their top three factors, 17% comparison platforms, 9% website/branding.
- **Older cross-check:** Toluna survey of over 1,000 homeowners (undated, pre-2020 judging by references): 12% used Rated People, MyBuilder or Checkatrade in the past year; 70% used someone they knew or a friend recommended [Marketing Donut, https://www.marketingdonut.co.uk/sales/get-trade-business-from-referral-websites]. ANECDOTAL (old).
- **Windows specifically:** Business Pilot's March 2026 white paper (Issue 2, six-plus years of its user data plus Keystone consumer research) says lead volumes are down 15% since 2020, average order value is up over 60%, and word-of-mouth is becoming more important [https://glasstimes.co.uk/news/business-pilot-reveals-major-market-shift/]. VENDOR; no sample size or lead-source percentages published.
- **Share by channel for tradespeople:** no independent survey of firms found. Everyone quoting "x% from Checkatrade" is a vendor.
- **Paid platforms.** MyBuilder shortlist fees about £2-£35 depending on job size by its own pricing; MyBuilder/Rated People "typically £10-£50+" per lead in another Whito estimate; tradespeople reportedly need 4-8 shortlists to win one job; Rated People sells a lead to up to 3 trades [Whito, 2026, https://whito.co.uk/research/trades-lead-generation-cost-uk/; summary only]. Whito says none of 10 UK platforms publishes a win rate. Treat all cost-per-won-job numbers (£80-£200) as GUESS dressed as data.
- **Google Local Services Ads (UK):** reported as available to roofers (about £15-£45 per verified lead), not confirmed for window fitters [Trade2Base, 2026, https://www.trade2base.com/blog/google-local-services-ads-uk, VENDOR blog].
- **Fabricator/supplier referral schemes:** fabricators and system houses route homeowner leads to approved installers (Sternfenster, Emplas, Ultraframe, Rehau, VEKA etc.) - see partners_fabricators.
- **Doorstep/canvassing.** UK councils run recurring warnings about unsolicited roofers (e.g. Bromley, Cornwall) and prosecute rogue roofers regularly; this poisons the whole trade's image (ANECDOTAL, titles seen in search, not read). Door knocking still exists in windows and roofing, mostly in firms with commission reps (see 3).
- **Home shows, leaflets, Facebook groups:** no data found. GUESS: Facebook community groups and Google Business Profile reviews are the real "marketing" for small roofers and kitchen fitters.

## 3. The sales process, step by step

Enquiry (phone, web form, platform lead, fabricator lead) -> callback -> survey/home visit -> quote -> (follow-up or silence) -> decision -> deposit -> fitting -> final payment.

**Speed of the quote is the one measured failure point.** The Powered Now "NOW Report" (Fusion Insight & Strategy for Powered Now and Installer magazine; reported as 140 UK tradespeople, fieldwork April-June 2026 depending on which page): only 4% send the quote the same day as the visit, 27% within 24 hours, over a quarter take more than 3 days, and 55% admit they definitely or probably lost a job purely because the quote went out too slowly. Average admin (quoting, invoicing, chasing payments) 5 hours 20 minutes a week, mostly evenings and weekends; 93% report stress; 52% took no week's holiday in 12 months; owed on average £5,901 [https://www.electricaltimes.co.uk/?p=36067 ; quote-speed figures via https://www.paintinganddecoratingnews.co.uk/sponsored/the-phone-call-i-let-ring-out-from-the-top-of-a-ladder/]. VENDOR-commissioned, small sample, mixed trades (heating, electrical, plumbing, builders), not windows or roofing. Other admin surveys: HeyBRB 2026 (n=167, mixed small businesses): about 8 hours a week, 77% of trades do admin in the evenings [Electrical Times, https://www.electricaltimes.co.uk/uk-tradespeople-losing-ten-working-weeks-a-year-to-admin-they-could-automate-survey-finds/]. VENDOR. Figures across vendors range 5-14 hours/week.

**Windows and doors**
- *Quote on the night vs send off:* about 23% of firms quote live in the home using their own selling prices; over half send a request to suppliers for every job; about a quarter use fabricator/manufacturing software (Tommy Trinder, "hundreds" of companies, Aug 2025) [https://glasstimes.co.uk/products/only-1-in-4-installers-quote-on-the-night/]. VENDOR. This means many window quotes arrive hours or days after the visit.
- *Conversion:* Business Pilot users convert about 40-44% of leads to sales (43.9% Jul 2023, 40.8% Aug 2025, 39.8% Sep 2025, 40.7% Oct 2025); average order value £3,736 in Sep 2025, £4,123 in Jul 2023; around 6 in 10 enquiries do not convert; average user got about 106 leads and 49 sales in Sep 2025 [Business Pilot Barometer, https://businesspilot.co.uk/barometer/ ; https://glasstimes.co.uk/news/mind-the-conversion-gap/ (17 Apr 2026)]. VENDOR, sample not shown, and these are firms that already run a CRM, so likely above the market average. Also: 12-week order books reported in late 2020 (peak COVID demand).
- *Follow-up:* the same Business Pilot MD says most sales are not lost at the first conversation. Sternfenster launched automated follow-up emails at 3, 7 and 14 days after a quote and claims "up to 36%" conversion lift from its own retail arm (Jan 2026) [https://glasstimes.co.uk/products/new-easyadmin-quote-follow-up-tool/]. VENDOR. Note: this is already competition (see competitors).
- *How many quotes homeowners get:* **no UK data found.** A Business Pilot analyst said in 2023 that homeowners are approaching fewer than three window companies, sometimes just one [https://businesspilot.co.uk/barometer/ Aug 2023 piece, ANECDOTAL opinion]. Which? tells buyers to get "a number of quotes" without a number. "Three quotes" is folklore (GUESS).
- *What homeowners say about sellers:* Keystone UK Consumer Fenestration Trends Report (launched 9 Feb 2026, sponsored by Business Pilot and fabricators; sample not stated): only 7% say window/door salespeople addressed what mattered most "very well"; 92% would pick the firm that showed it understood their needs when products were similar [https://glasstimes.co.uk/news/keystone-report-shows-installers-dont-listen/]. VENDOR-sponsored but a big homeowner signal: the pain is listening and chasing, not just price.
- *Biggest sales obstacle named by installers:* competitors selling too cheaply, 77% (Windowlink/Insight Data 2017, sample not stated, old) [https://glasstimes.co.uk/products/pen-and-paper-quotations/].
- *Pressure-selling legacy:* trading-standards cases involve sales staff staying 3+ hours and fake "today only" discounts (Zenith Staybrite prosecuted under the CPRs; Surrey council alerts) [https://news.surreycc.gov.uk/2016/07/22/double-glazing-firm-admits-using-unfair-sales-tactics ; council consumer alerts Dec 2024]. Commission-only self-employed reps are still advertised (e.g. £40,000-£70,000 "on target") and one company adviser says commission-only pay is why hard-sell exists, while independents "broadly reject" pressure selling [https://www.doorandwindowexperts.co.uk/14001/sell-double-glazing/ , ANECDOTAL/VENDOR]. Practical read: small independent installers are owner-led and distance themselves from this; national brands run reps.
- *Finance:* point-of-sale finance is sold to installers by Kanda (fee about 4.25% of order, deposits 10-50%), Improveasy (FENSA partner, up to 12-month payment-free) and Kandoo [https://glasstimes.co.uk/products/win-work-with-discounted-consumer-finance/ ; https://glasstimes.co.uk/news/finance-solutions-strengthened/]. Hitachi/Omni Capital: **no data found** on use or share.
- *Deposits:* no norm published. Examples from supplier quotes seen in council planning files: about 29%, 50%, 60% on bespoke sash orders, i.e. high-ticket made-to-measure orders sit at the high end [Camden planning documents, summary only]. Trading Standards advises avoiding large up-front payments and using staged payments [Leicester, East Dunbartonshire, North Somerset councils, summary only]. Guarantee: Which? recommends a 10-year insurance-backed guarantee (IBG) so the warranty survives the installer failing, and notes card/credit protection for £100-£30,000 purchases [Which?, link above]. GUESS: small installers ask 10-30% on signing for standard PVC-U and more for bespoke.
- *Quote validity:* 30 days is standard in trade advice, 60 for larger jobs needing finance [Trade2Base, VENDOR/ANECDOTAL, https://www.trade2base.com/blog/how-to-write-a-quote-tradesperson-uk].

**Roofing.** The survey step is often a ladder/drone/photo inspection, then a written quote that sometimes never arrives. Customer reviews show the reverse pain: roofers who promise a quote and go quiet, no-shows at surveys [Checkatrade and Which? Trusted Traders reviews, ANECDOTAL]. No UK conversion data. NFRC members (Spring 2025, over 150 members): only 36% had higher workload (down from 48%), 33% more enquiries, 66% paid on time (up from 55%), 80% affected by retentions (retentions are a commercial-work issue, not domestic) [https://roofingtoday.co.uk/roofing-industry-facing-slowdown-amid-optimism-in-key-sectors-nfrc-spring-2025-report/]. VERIFIED, but NFRC members skew commercial and larger than the small domestic roofer. Summer 2025: 43% reported higher workload [Roofing Today, summary only].

**Kitchens.** kbbreview Retailer Survey 2026 (independent retailers, published 8 Jan 2026; sample not stated in article): enquiry-to-sale conversion under 40% for 21% of firms, 40-60% for 31%, 60-70% for 27%, over 70% for 21%; time from enquiry to confirmed order under a month for 13% (only 6% for kitchens versus 25% for bathrooms), 1-2 months for 30%; 38% saw more enquiries, 37% fewer; 70% said average order value rose (mostly inflation); 88% saw operating costs rise [https://www.kbbreview.com/?p=80543]. VERIFIED trade survey. In the 2024 edition (n over 500) "clients taking a long time to reach a final decision" was the top challenge for 14%, and 74% said there are not enough skilled fitters [kbbreview 2024, link above]. This is the best evidence anywhere in this research that kitchen decisions are long, which suits a follow-up product.

**Owner-led vs reps.** Small installers and roofers: the owner surveys, quotes and chases (or doesn't). Showrooms: designers/salespeople on salary or commission. Big brands (Anglian/Safestyle group, Everest): reps and canvassers. Rep structure is ANECDOTAL.

**What tradespeople themselves say about chasing quotes** (forum threads, ANECDOTAL): a paving contractor asked how long to leave it before chasing; replies ranged from 2 days to 7-10 days, phone preferred over email, one said his conversion fell from about 35% to 10%, another never chases and relies on the site visit, and one keeps unwon quotes to re-contact when work is quiet [Paving Expert Brew Cabin, https://ext.pavingexpert.com/brewcabin/viewtopic.php?p=87378]. On a UK business forum (2016) some argue a client who needs chasing isn't worth having, others see silence as "still deciding" [UK Business Forums, https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/what-to-do-when-a-client-doesnt-respond.362750/]. A 2015 thread: many interviewees did not use apps or an assistant to follow up quotes [UK Business Forums, https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/basic-business-challenges-for-tradespeople.342430/]. Win rates quoted by tradesmen themselves: 10-35%. By software users: 40-44% (Business Pilot), 82% (Tradify, see below). The spread is large because "quote" and "lead" are defined differently.

## 4. Communication

- **Phone vs WhatsApp vs email:** **no independent UK survey** gives the split. Trade2Base (VENDOR) asserts WhatsApp has 35m UK users and over 90% open rates, and that "48% of customers book the first tradesperson who calls back" - both unsourced [https://www.trade2base.com/blog/whatsapp-business-for-tradespeople ; /trade-business-customer-service-uk]. Quoting-app marketing treats WhatsApp as the default channel for sending quotes, which is consistent with WhatsApp being the daily tool (GUESS).
- **Answering on the roof:** Everreach 2024 survey of over 250 small businesses incl. electricians, plumbers, builders: 72% worry about losing work if they can't answer the phone; 60% said family have accidentally answered work calls (summary only, VENDOR) [https://www.hvpmag.co.uk/Missed-calls-cost-plumbers-over-amppound25000-a-year-according-to-survey]. Powered Now now sells an AI receptionist on the "missed calls cost jobs" angle; its statistics (33% of small businesses don't answer, 69-80% of voicemail callers hang up) are unsourced or international [https://www.electricaltimes.co.uk/?p=36621, VENDOR]. Don't quote these.
- **Email habits:** no data. Trade2Base says email is where follow-ups "go to die" (opinion, no data). GUESS: owners check email in the evening admin session (supported indirectly by 77% doing admin in the evening) and mobile notifications during the day.
- **When to reach them:** no UK trades-specific study. A call-handling guide says morning and evening are when customers ring but tradespeople are driving or tired; a general B2B study found 8-9am best for office workers [Trade2Base call-handling guide; Decision Marketing]. GUESS for roofers: wet days at home are the best contact days, early morning before the van leaves, and 4-6pm. Do not claim this as fact.

## 5. Software and tools

- **Job/CRM:** Tradify (about £35 per user/month), ServiceM8 (priced by jobs), Powered Now (aimed at sole traders), Jobber, YourTradebase, Fergus, Commusoft, simPRO [Trade2Base comparison 2026, VENDOR]. **Adoption share: no UK survey.** Xero's 2021 "Right tech for tradies" found tradies on pen and paper felt less in control (91% of those who seldom feel in control used pen and paper), global/dated [https://blog.xero.com/data-insights/right-tech-for-tradies-2021/]. The 2015 UKBF thread said many interviewees didn't use apps for quote follow-up. Tradify's own 3,271-business sample claims **82% of quotes convert** (electrical and plumbing above 85%) - VENDOR, and only counts quotes actually entered in the system, so it overstates the market; do not use as a benchmark [https://www.tradifyhq.com/uk/trades-report].
- **Window-specific:** Quoting/pricing apps: Tommy Trinder (Framepoint) - over 500 installers in an older Glass Times item, and later analysis of over 1 million items quoted by over 700 installers; Pricepoint (designer, quoting, CRM); Windowmaker (from £99 per user/month); fabricator portals such as Sternfenster EasyAdmin+; CRM: Business Pilot (with Tommy Trinder integration) [Glass Times 2022-2025; https://businesspilot.co.uk/features/feature-focus-tommy-trinder-integration/]. All VENDOR. How installers quote (Windowlink/Insight Data, 2017, sample not stated, old): 38% independent specialist software, 38% fabricator-supplied software, 24% pen and paper. Note: the brief's "Elev8, Quote2Fit, Evolve" returned nothing relevant in UK searches; treat those names as unconfirmed.
- **Roofing:** UK-specific estimating tools: **not found**. US tools (iRoofing, Roofr, RoofSnap) show up but are North American. GUESS: UK roofers use phone photos, WhatsApp, and a Word/PDF quote or Tradify-style app; some use drone and satellite measure.
- **Kitchens:** 20-20 and Planit design software are the incumbents (2009 forum, UK offices in Ashford and Bolton for 20-20); no current UK adoption data [ANECDOTAL/old].
- **Accounting:** fewer than 25% of sole traders use an accountant (Screwfix 2024-25). Xero, QuickBooks, FreeAgent usage rates for trades: no data.
- **Overall:** no credible "share using any software" figure exists. Safest statement: a meaningful minority of window installers still quote on paper (24% in 2017), most use some quoting software, almost none run a system that reminds them to chase.

- Whito's test of Google AI Mode (60 answers, 20 towns, 22 Aug 2026; electricians, plumbers, roofers): Checkatrade cited in 83%, trade bodies in 62%, median recommended business 96 Google reviews. Small, self-run study, VENDOR.
- **Share of trades with a website:** **no data found.** GUESS: most registered installers (FENSA/Certass) and showrooms have a template site; many small roofers and kitchen fitters run a Facebook page plus Google Business Profile plus a Checkatrade/MyBuilder profile.

## 7. Money

**Job prices (2026 ballparks from price-guide sites; they disagree, so use ranges).**
- Windows: uPVC full house (3-bed) about £3,500-£8,000 in one guide and up to £12,000 in another; £400-£700 per ground-floor casement supply and fit. FMB guide: doors plus windows for a whole house about £6,000-£9,000. Composite front door £725-£2,580 (£800-£1,800 typical). Bifolds: three-panel aluminium £2,500-£4,500; FMB table £1,550-£5,680 [FMB https://www.fmb.org.uk/homepicks/windows/doors-and-windows/ ; Trade2Base; Book a Builder, 2026, summaries only]. Business Pilot's real order-value data: £3,700-£4,100 average in 2025-26 (small jobs pull it down).
- Roofing: re-roof of a 3-bed semi about £5,000-£12,000 (one guide) or £6,500-£14,500 concrete tile, £11,000-£18,500 natural slate; scaffold £600-£1,200; flat roof per m2 felt £50-£100, EPDM £60-£120, GRP £80-£160; typical garage/extension flat roof £1,400-£3,250; repair call-out £150-£350, one or two tiles £80-£200 [Trade2Base, Whito, Pocketwise 2026, summaries only].
- Kitchens: fitter day rate about £250-£350 (Checkatrade guide) with regional sole traders at £150-£250; labour-only fit £1,000-£5,000; supply and fit £4,000-£10,000 mid-range, £12,000-£25,000+ premium [Checkatrade cost guide; Trade2Base 2026, summaries only].

**Margins.** Windows: no credible figure. Commentary says higher-value products earn more because fitting labour is constant, and that the industry was "busy but not necessarily profitable" [Glass Times, https://glasstimes.co.uk/featured-articles/busy-but-are-you-profitable/ ; /have-you-given-away-thousands/, ANECDOTAL]. Roofing: Roofing Today's top-30 contractor tables put average profit at about 6-7%, half below 5% (2018, 2020), large firms only [summary only]; IBISWorld warns reported profits include owners' own wages. Trade2Base rule-of-thumb gross 25-45%, net 8-18% for materials-heavy trades, unsourced. Net margin for a 1-5 person firm: GUESS, high single digit to mid teens once the owner's wage is counted.

**Pricing method:** per-window price books and fabricator quotes (windows), per-m2 or day-rate plus materials (roofing), day rate or % of kitchen price (fitters) - from the pricing guides; no survey.

**Seasonality.** Windows: December trough, January rebound, strong autumn (Business Pilot). Roofing: repair work peaks autumn and winter storms; planned re-roofs in drier months (UK trades-marketing sources, VENDOR, conflicting on winter). Kitchens: GUESS pre-Christmas order rush; no survey data found.

**Cash flow and late payment.** Ford Pro (survey of over 1,000 incl. homeowners): tradespeople owed about £3,942 each (£3.5bn total), invoices paid on average 35 days after receipt, 71% have had a customer refuse to pay, 1 in 5 used payday loans [LBC op-ed by Ford UK, https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/tis-the-season-for-unpaid-invoices-the-growing-crisis-facing-tradespeople-5HjcwbK_2/, VENDOR-authored]. NOW Report: 67% say a customer deliberately delayed or disputed payment (n=140). Powered Now blog quoting IronmongeryDirect 2024: 79% had a customer refuse to pay at least once, losing over £1,000 a year on average (secondhand; sample unstated) [https://powerednow.com/blog/cowboy-customers-featured-on-itv]. FMB/CIOB State of Trade H2 2025 (n=493): net workload +22%, net enquiries +18%, net employment +17% - generally positive for small builders [https://www.ciob.org/industry/research/State-of-Trade-H2-2025]. Citizens Advice: 25% of homeowners reported a problem with the trader on their latest job; 36,903 complaints in 2025 [link above]. The consumer side is also anxious.

**Material price pressure:** NFRC Spring 2025: 86% of roofers saw materials costs rise, 79% labour. kbbreview 2026: 88% of kitchen retailers saw operating costs rise.

## 8. Industry structure

- **Fabricators vs installers:** fabricators make frames from system-house profiles (Veka, Rehau, Deceuninck, Liniar, etc.); installers buy from fabricators and fit. An old BSI case study put it at about 1,500 fabricators serving 11,000+ installers/retailers (c. 2014) [see partners_fabricators]. In Tommy Trinder's quote data, PVC-U is 79% of installed items and 61% of value; London PVC-U share fell to 39% from 48% in 2022 as aluminium grows [https://glasstimes.co.uk/products/pvc-u-market-share-drops-to-39-in-london/, VENDOR].
- **Certification:** six government-authorised competent person schemes for windows/doors: FENSA, Certass, NICEIC (Certsure), NAPIT, Assure, Blue Flame. FENSA says over 6,300 approved installers and notifies over 77% of installations in England and Wales (cited by GGF); last official per-scheme counts were 6,051 FENSA and 2,107 Certass in 2017-18. Nobody publishes a current total. FENSA joining about £267 plus £172 a year; certificate fee £2.05 + VAT per installation [Whito, 2026, https://whito.co.uk/window-door-installers/; GGF]. Roofing: NFRC (roughly 70% of the industry by value per a data aggregator, unverified) runs CompetentRoofer (self-certification plus insurance-backed warranty, 10 years domestic) [NFRC/SIG pages]. Also TrustMark, Which? Trusted Traders (Which? endorses VEKA's installer network), GGF for glazing, KBSA for independent kitchen/bathroom retailers (no total member count published).
- **National players:** Safestyle collapsed into administration in late October 2023; Anglian bought the Safestyle brand and order book and both now trade as the ASHI Group from January 2024 [Wikipedia/Which?/trade press, summary only]. The collapse left customers with warranty and certification problems, which is why IBG now sells as a trust signal. Everest: **no data found**. Local share vs nationals: **not found**.
- **Bigger picture for rival software:** Business Pilot (CRM), Tommy Trinder, Pricepoint and fabricator-owned follow-up tools (Sternfenster) all operate in windows. Roofing and kitchens have far less specialist tooling.

## 9. Culture, values, philosophy

Based on forums, surveys of annoyances and trade-press tone. Mostly ANECDOTAL.
- **Pride and independence.** 84% would recommend a trades career to a young person (NOW Report n=140); 52% take no week's holiday; 93% stressed. The job is a craft that eats the owner's life.
- **Distrust of sellers.** Tradesmen on forums say they refuse to buy from anyone who cold-called them, and describe SEO/web cold callers as buying numbers from directories; marketing sites themselves warn of agencies owning your domain and long contracts [UK Business Forums "SEO Cold Callers", https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/seo-cold-callers.229114/ ; Whito "why tradespeople waste money marketing"]. The homeowner has the same distrust of them: the double-glazing salesman image is a deep scar in the industry, and installers distance themselves from it.
- **Reputation is everything.** Reviews and recommendations beat websites (section 6). Cowboy stigma: Citizens Advice says a single trader register is needed; 25% of homeowners had a problem on their last job; councils prosecute rogue roofers monthly.
- **What annoys them.** IronmongeryDirect 2022 (n=500 tradespeople): clients who hover (41%), constant "how's it going" (34%), "while you're here" extras (28%), asking for a discount (26%), late payment (25%), customers not home (14%) [https://professional-electrician.com/?p=37794, summary]. Powered Now blog cites (secondhand, unnamed study) over 5 million Britons requesting quotes with no intention to hire, about 2.5 million forgotten appointments, and the FMB CEO complaining about "cowboy customers" [link above]. Treat counts as marketing; the feeling (time-wasters and price shoppers cost the day) is real. Also: 32% named difficult clients a top challenge (n=500, 2025) and 31% considered leaving the trade (Arco 2025) via Powered Now.
- **Values.** Being busy, the van and the name on it, family, and independence (GUESS from tone; supported by retirement and "no successor" data: 69% have nobody to hand the business to).
- **Plain speech.** Forum voice is short, practical, sceptical of jargon and "automation". Evidence: threads read as practical advice swaps with no marketing language (qualitative).

## 10. Pain points relevant to Velarqo

1. **Quotes sent late or never chased.** 4% same-day, 27% within 24 hours, 55% admit losing a job to slow quoting (NOW Report, VENDOR, n=140). Roughly 6 in 10 window enquiries do not convert (Business Pilot, VENDOR).
2. **Wasted surveys and no-shows/time-wasters**: real irritant (IronmongeryDirect n=500, 14% name customers not being home; Powered Now claims) but the numbers are soft.
3. **Evening admin**: 5-8 hours a week, evenings and weekends (NOW Report; HeyBRB n=167). They quote after dinner.
4. **Homeowner ghosting and long decisions**: kitchens take 1-2 months or more from enquiry to confirmation for most; 14% of retailers name slow client decisions their biggest challenge (kbbreview 2024).
5. **Lead cost and quality**: Checkatrade 12-month auto-renew contracts, shared leads, reported renewal rises; MyBuilder per-lead fees. All member-reported.
6. **Staff shortages**: 74% of kitchen retailers say not enough fitters; 47% of NFRC roofers found recruitment harder (Spring 2025).
7. **Material and operating cost rises**: 86% of roofers, 88% of kitchen retailers.
8. **Late payment**: owed about £4,000-£5,900 each, 35-day average delay.
9. **Stress**: 93% (n=140).
10. **Competitors already name the problem**: Sternfenster (automated 3/7/14 day emails), Business Pilot (CRM, "most sales aren't lost at the first conversation"), Powered Now and Trade2Base (follow-up reminders). Velarqo needs an angle that a small installer can see is different (e.g. it does it for them, in plain words from the owner, without needing a CRM).

## The 10 things a founder must know about these owners
1. They are small, old and busy: Screwfix's 2025 panel had 34% aged 55-64, 54% "just me", 25% planning to retire inside five years; an average roofing firm has about 3.4 staff. Pitch a person, not a "team".
2. They sell by reputation. Over a third of homeowners find a trader through someone they know (Citizens Advice/Opinium 2026, n=5,000); websites matter to only 9%. Your messages must never put their reputation at risk with an awkward homeowner chase.
3. Late quotes are the measured weakness: only 27% quote within 24 hours; 55% say slow quotes lost them jobs (vendor survey, n=140). That is your cleanest, most believable opening line, but label it as a survey of 140 tradespeople.
4. In windows, roughly 4 in 10 enquiries convert even for firms with a CRM, so 6 in 10 are "quiet" quotes; the money in follow-up is real but someone else (Sternfenster, Business Pilot) has already said so.
5. Kitchens have the longest decisions (most take 1-2+ months; only 6% of kitchen retailers confirm inside a month), which makes kitchens the best structural fit for chase-up. Showroom owners are more office-based than roofers.
6. They do admin at night: 77% in the evening, 5-8 hours a week. They read and reply after about 6pm. During the day the phone is in a van or a pocket (GUESS on the exact hours).
7. They distrust anyone selling to them, especially marketing agencies and cold callers; plain tradesperson language, one small ask, proof with real numbers. Lead-gen platforms have burned them (shared leads, 12-month auto-renew).
8. They hate chasing as an act: some think a quiet customer isn't worth chasing, some never chase, some phone after 7-10 days. Position Velarqo as doing it in their voice and stopping when the customer says no, not as pestering.
9. Money is tight and lumpy: owed £4,000-£5,900 on average, paid on average 35 days late, 67% have had a customer deliberately delay or dispute (vendor surveys). A paid tool must pay back on one recovered job; job values are about £3,700-£4,100 in windows, £1,500-£15,000 in roofing, £4,000-£25,000 in kitchens.
10. The pressure-sales scar is real: windows carry a 'salesman sits until you sign' reputation, enforced by trading-standards prosecutions. Any chasing copy must read the opposite: calm, no fake deadlines, no "today only" pricing, and must respect the 14-day cooling-off and no-cancel-on-made-to-measure rules.
