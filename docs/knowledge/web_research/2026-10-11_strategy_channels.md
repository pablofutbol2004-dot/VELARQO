# First 10 clients, then 50: channel plan for the next 90 days

Written 2026-10-11 for Pablo. Constraints: €0 cash until the first sale, 10-20 h/week (this plan assumes about 15), no cold calling, Spain-based, zero clients, zero case studies. Question: what are the best ways to get 10 clients, then 50, and how should cold email be run?

**Labels.** VERIFIED = I read the primary page this session. VENDOR = a company selling the thing reports it. ANECDOTAL = one forum post, one agency, one case. GUESS = my reasoning. Source tags [S#] are listed at the end. The search tool I had is US-indexed and could not see Facebook, so several UK-specific facts below are marked as not found. Nothing here is an owner's own words except the 2013 forum thread [S10].

## 1. The answer in brief

1. **"First 10" and "50" are different problems.** Ten pilots can come from cold email plus warm introductions. Fifty cannot come from cold email alone (section 2); it needs referrals from clients and at least one partner.
2. **Ranked by payoff per hour:** (1) fixed-up cold email, (2) warm introductions and referral asks from day 1, (3) pilots turned into a case study, (4) a "design partner" ask to small owner-led fabricators and trade bodies, (5) LinkedIn as a trust asset, (6) a data story for trade press, (7) Facebook as listening only. Marketplaces, ads, letters and lead-magnet campaigns wait.
3. **The bottleneck is trust, not reach.** A foreign founder with no surname, UK number or proof asks owners to let him message their customers. Fix name, face and phone route before raising volume (`HARD_TO_FAIL_PLAN.md` section A; I agree it comes first).
4. **Realistic day-90 result (GUESS):** 1-4 pilots, one written case-study permission, one partner conversation that names installers. Ten clients in 90 days at €0 is guru territory.
5. **No independent cold-email benchmark exists for UK trades.** Generic B2B: 3-4.5% total replies [S1][S2], about 14% of them positive in one vendor dataset [S2b]; one roofing-contractor campaign reported 2.4% [S20, ANECDOTAL]. Plan on 1-3% replies, 0.3-1% positive, then measure.
6. **Five cold-email changes** (section 5): name plus one true fact; an easy reply route; provider-aware sending; test offers not words; give first only to people who reply. Keep three emails.
7. **Partner fee only on results.** £100 flat is in range (Tradify pays advisers £150 [S6]); about 20% recurring is the other norm [S7][S17b].
8. **Written terms and testimonial permission on day 1** of every pilot [S16].

## 2. Why cold email alone cannot reach 50 (arithmetic, GUESS)

- Eight inboxes at 20 sends a day is 160 sends. A three-step sequence uses about 2.8 sends per firm, so about 56 new firms start each working day and the 7,512 list lasts roughly 27 working weeks. In 90 days (from about 25 Oct, ramping) you reach perhaps 2,000-2,500 firms.
- `HARD_TO_FAIL_PLAN.md` assumes about 1.5 pilots per 1,000 firms (middle case, itself a guess). That gives **3-4 pilots by day 90** from cold email at best, 1-2 at the low end.
- Fifty pilots at that rate means about 33,000 firms; the UK pool is about 12,000 window/door and 12,000 roofing businesses [HARD_TO_FAIL_PLAN sources], not all Ltds with emails. Cold email would burn the market before reaching 50.
- So the route to 50 is: pilots → owners introduce non-competing owners (word of mouth is how this trade works: 36% of homeowners found their last trader through someone they know [NICHE_PROFILE, Citizens Advice 2026]) → one or two partners who each reach hundreds.

## 4. Channel notes and evidence

### 4.1 Cold email (changes in section 5)

- **Reply rates, VENDOR:** Hunter 4.1% average (11M emails, 2024), follow-up campaigns 4.9% against 3.0% without [S1]. Instantly 3.43% average, top quartile 5.5%+ [S2]. Definitions differ; one 7.5M-email dataset found 0.45% [S3b].
- **Small and targeted wins, VENDOR:** campaigns of 50 or fewer recipients averaged 5.8% against 2.1% for 1,000+ [S1]. Confounded (small campaigns are hand-built), but it supports a hand-written batch for your best-fit firms.
- **Follow-ups:** Instantly: 58% of replies arrive on step 1 [S2]. Hunter: unsubscribes rise after the third follow-up [S1]. Belkins (secondary summary): 4+ emails more than tripled unsubscribes and complaints [S3]. A roofing agency says the first follow-up brings 40%+ of replies [S20, ANECDOTAL], which conflicts with Instantly. Keep three.
- **Subject lines and first lines:** Hunter has no subject finding [S1]; Belkins reports personalised subjects at 7% against 3% replies [S3, VENDOR]; Aaron Shepherd says subjects "really don't matter" (KB, ANECDOTAL). Lavender's 231,818 graded emails: top grade 4.3% against 3.4% [S19, VENDOR]. Consistent signal: a named person plus one true fact beats a template. Do not spend a test cell on subject wording.
- **Personalisation candidates:** years trading (Companies House, accurate for every firm), town (the course advises against [S21]), FENSA or Checkatrade badge (about 8% of window firms [NICHE_PROFILE]). Nothing independent ranks them; test years-trading first.
- **Deliverability to Microsoft-hosted recipients:** placement figures disagree by 20+ points: Validity via Warmforge 87.2% Gmail against 75.6% Outlook; Smartlead Q3 81.98% against 76.86%; GlockApps Q4 2025 57% against 45% [S13, all VENDOR]. None covers a new Workspace domain sending to Microsoft 365; one practitioner reports Gmail inbox but Microsoft spam on clean authentication [S13, ANECDOTAL]. Microsoft's May 2025 rules cover Outlook.com/Hotmail/Live mail from senders above 5,000 emails a day [S14], so they do not bind you and say nothing about Microsoft 365 business filtering. Your list: 76% own domain (host unknown), Gmail 13%, Hotmail/Outlook 6% [kb_digest niche]. You will not know your Microsoft share until you look up MX records (free).
- **Give-first, evidence:** free audit of 20 old quotes is the "reverse lead magnet" (claimed 2-3% replies on a hard offer [S21, VENDOR, US]); a rewritten quote needs their quote, so only after a reply; personalised video: the 4x claim is Loom's own, one 53-recipient test cannot support a rule, and one source says video at scale to 800 small-business owners can lose to a tight email [S19]. Use video only after a positive reply.
- **Legal:** Ltds and LLPs can be emailed without consent given identity and an opt-out; sole traders and some partnerships count as individuals [S4]. The ICO says the guidance is under review (Data (Use and Access) Act): recheck before each new slice. LSSI risk already accepted by Pablo (memory, 2026-10-01).

### 4.2 Warm intros and referral asks

- **Evidence:** Hunter found referral requests were the most effective follow-up type [S1, VENDOR]; your follow-up 3 ("who's best to speak to?") already does this.
- **Who to ask (GUESS; Pablo to fill in):** classmates and lecturers whose families own a UK trades firm; Spanish friends with UK-based relatives who had windows, a roof or a kitchen done; tutoring students' parents; anyone in UK construction, estimating or surveying. Message 30 people: "Do you know anyone who runs a window, roofing or kitchen firm in the UK? I'm testing a service that chases their old quotes, free for the first one." Expect 3-6 leads, 1-2 conversations.
- **After the first pilot:** ask every client for two non-competing owners. Owners in neighbouring regions know each other through fabricators, merchants and trade bodies.
- Cost €0; 3 h in weeks 1-3, then 1 h a week.

### 4.3 Partners and white-label

`PARTNERS.md` lists 31 targets; this is what the web check changed.

- **Fee norms.** Tradify UK pays £150 cash per referral who becomes a paying customer, aimed at coaches, accountants and bookkeepers, paid a month after upgrade [S6, VERIFIED]. Trade2Base pays accountants and bookkeepers 20% recurring [S7]. SaaS programmes commonly pay 15-30% recurring (median about 20%, often capped at 12 months); flat $100-$500 bounties are normal for enterprise B2B [S17b, VENDOR]. Your £100 flat fee, or 10-20% for 12 months for lead vendors, is in range.
- **Not confirmed.** Business Pilot's partner page names no referral terms; it is a directory with an enquiry form [S8, VERIFIED]. FENSA's homepage lists no benefits or affinity partners [S9, VERIFIED], so `PARTNERS.md` row 28 rests on earlier research. Verify before writing to FENSA.
- **Existing B2B2B.** Fabricator networks already bundle leads, CRM and marketing (Sternfenster's EasyAdmin, Emplas, Bison's Genesis network [S12]). Sternfenster's quote chaser emails at 3, 7 and 14 days and claims up to 36% conversion gain from "early results" in its own retail arm [S12, VENDOR, no method]. Fabricators are partly competitors; they build tools in-house.
- **Accountants and bookkeepers for trades** are a real referral channel (Tradify, Trade2Base), but UK accountants taking fees must disclose them to clients and may face ICAEW/ACCA rules [S7b]. Slow to recruit one by one; park until you have a result.
- **Pitching as a nobody.** Do not ask for a deal. Ask for 15 minutes of advice ("tell me what's wrong with this") and two friendly installers for a free pilot. The £100 fee is the sweetener, not the pitch. The owner-led doors in `PARTNERS.md` (CORC, AdminBase, Quickslide, Masterframe, Windowlink, Leads 2 Trade) fit; VEKA, Rehau, Eurocell, Business Pilot and FENSA wait for a result.
- **Bring the start forward.** `PARTNERS.md` starts 10 Nov. Partner replies take weeks and this is the only route to 50, so start the owner-led doors in week 3 (26 Oct), once the legal name and address are on velarqo.com.
- **Legal.** Named role at a Ltd, identity and opt-out [S4]; not from the outreach mailboxes (existing rule).

### 4.4 LinkedIn

- **Do UK trade owners use it?** The one UK survey found (Direct Line business insurance, 2023, sample size not stated): 93% of tradespeople use social media; Facebook rated effective for leads by 58% of users, Instagram 39%, YouTube 33%, TikTok 31%, LinkedIn and X 25% [S5, VENDOR]. That measures tradespeople reaching homeowners, not owners finding suppliers. No LinkedIn usage figure for glazing or roofing owners found.
- **Use it for people who are on it:** fabricator, merchant, CRM-vendor and trade-body staff, plus bigger installers' sales managers. 20 connection requests a week, a sentence each, no pitch.
- **The profile is a trust asset.** Cold-email recipients will Google "Velarqo" and "Pablo": real photo, surname, "based in Spain, working UK hours", one line on what you do (`HARD_TO_FAIL_PLAN.md` A1-A3).
- 1.5 h in week 1, then 1 h a week. Kill: no reply from any partner-side contact after 40 requests.

### 4.5 Facebook groups

- **Names, sizes and rules: not found.** The search tool could not see Facebook; only generic advice surfaced (join 5-10 groups, spamming gets you banned) and one WhatsApp group of 171 builders [search]. Pablo must search inside Facebook for "window fitters UK", "double glazing installers", "roofers UK", "roofing contractors UK", "kitchen fitters UK" and check each About section and last-post date.
- **On The Tools** is a large UK construction community (1.5 million Facebook following quoted; Jewson sponsors its awards) [search, VENDOR]. Trade-wide, not glazing; listen only.
- **Skip the US playbook's shortcut.** Adam Erhart's "first client from Facebook groups" (answer questions, wait a week, be helpful) is sound, but the same video suggests telling admins you work in the industry to pass entry questions [S21]. Do not. UK trade spaces mostly ban vendors (NICHE_PROFILE).
- **Rule:** join 3 groups honestly, read, log repeated questions, comment only from real knowledge, no links or DMs. 30 min/week. Kill: no useful insight after 4 weeks.

### 4.6 Content with a niche angle

- The KB says content is not a first-client channel without an audience, long-form beats short-form for niche B2B, and most evidence comes from gurus selling to would-be entrepreneurs [KB content_acquisition.md, ANECDOTAL]. Tradespeople do use social, but to reach homeowners [S5].
- **Worth 1 h/week from week 4:** one data story from your Companies House counts (for example 35% of UK roofing Ltds formed since Jan 2025 [NICHE_PROFILE, DATA]). Business Pilot's monthly Barometer (conversion about 40%, so about 6 in 10 enquiries do not convert [S11, VENDOR]) shows the trade press reprints supplier data. Pitch Glass Times or Roofing Today a short sourced piece, after the website and legal details are fixed.
- "I rewrote a roofer's quote" videos and build-in-public: no evidence they reach owners. Skip until a client agrees to appear.

### 4.7 Lead magnets and 4.8 Upwork/Fiverr

A quote follow-up text pack is a GUESS as distribution: competitors already sell spreadsheets (A$19) and software with built-in follow-ups [search]; use only as reply bait and judge by calls booked. For marketplaces, no evidence UK owners hire quote-chasing help there; the only supply found is a US VA vendor [S18b]; fees are Upwork 0-15% (about 10% typical) and Fiverr 20% [S18], and they sell hours and stars, not outcomes. Skip both.

### 4.9 Doing the first jobs free, structured

Arm B already offers "first 50 free". To turn it into a case study:

1. **In writing before work starts:** what counts as a booked survey; the exact homeowner text; a weekly cap; permission to publish firm name, area, trade, numbers and a quote. The advertising rules require documentary evidence a testimonial is genuine, contact details for the giver and permission to use it [S16]; hold a signed permission, not a casual "ok".
2. **Clean cohort** (quoted, not bought, 3-24 months old, no active follow-up) answers "they'd have won those anyway". A holdout group gives an honest incremental figure; do it on pilot 2 or 3.
3. **One-line case study:** X old quotes, Y replies, Z surveys booked in N days, trade, area, method.
4. **End of week 2, ask for** two names, one sentence in their words, a 60-second phone video if they agree.
5. **Charge something.** If an owner will not agree a per-booked-survey term in writing, treat the lead as weaker.
6. Homeowner messages: no finance figures, no deadlines, stop at first "no" (LEARNINGS 4, 21).

### 4.10 Letters later

The oldest UK figure found (Royal Mail-commissioned Quadrangle study of 600+ campaigns, undated) puts the most common B2B response at 1% [S17]. Recent 2-4.4% figures are US vendor blogs with an unconfirmed DMA attribution [S17]; plan on 1-3%. A first-class standard stamp is £1.80 from 7 April 2026 [S15], so the 50-letter test in `LETTERS.md` costs about £90 plus print. That breaks the €0 rule: wait for the first sale, and send with a named result.

## 5. The five highest-leverage changes to the cold email campaign

Current state (`COLD_EMAIL_V2.md`): subject `last year's quotes`, greeting "Hi,", the homeowner text shown in email 1, "Worth a go?", sizing question in follow-up 2, "who's best to speak to?" in follow-up 3, no links, three steps.

**1. Name the person, add one true fact.** 68% of emails go to info@-type inboxes and 64% of firms have one director [LEARNINGS 2]; "Hi," reads as a template. Use the Companies House director first name where reliable and one accurate fact (years trading, from incorporation date). Hand-write the opener for the best-fit 150 (5+ years, filed accounts, 2+ directors or FENSA/Certass listing). Evidence, all VENDOR: the course advises first name on every email [S21]; Belkins ties personalised subjects to 7% against 3% replies [S3]; Hunter finds merge fields plus manual edits do best [S1]. Caveat: strip "Ltd/Limited" and fix ALL CAPS in `{company}` [S21]. Test: half of the first 500 get the fact line.

**2. Make replying easy the way owners reply.** Admin happens in the evening, phone is the default for chasing quotes [NICHE_PROFILE; S10, ANECDOTAL, 2013]. Put a UK phone or WhatsApp route in the signature and promise a reply within the hour, 08:30-17:00 UK. Make email 1's ask a one-word question ("tens or hundreds?") instead of "Worth a go?" (the course's "triple tap" ends on a one-word reply [S21, VENDOR]). The £5/month UK number is the one spend I would reconsider: a +34 number on a UK trade site costs trust, and your own rule says agree the cost first. Pablo's call; the free fallback is WhatsApp on the Spanish number with "based in Spain, answering UK hours" on the site. Test: email-only against email plus phone.

**3. Send provider-aware.** Look up MX records for all 7,512 domains (free; I can script it) and tag Google, Microsoft 365, Outlook.com or other. Then run seed tests per provider, read replies and bounces per provider each Friday, and if Microsoft-hosted placement is poor, send those firms fewer and later from the best-warmed inboxes. Why: vendor placement figures differ by 20+ points [S13] and none covers a new Workspace domain sending to Microsoft 365, so only your own data counts. Passing SPF, DKIM and DMARC is required by Microsoft only above 5,000 a day to consumer mail [S14] but is worth doing anyway.

**4. Test the offer, not the words.** The course, the KB and your own audit agree the offer outweighs copy [S21]. Send times and subjects show no robust effect (Hunter: none for day or time [S1]; Belkins favours Wed/Thu mornings [S3b]; they disagree, so skip). Use the 500-firm slices for offers: pay-per-booked-survey (arm A) against first 50 free (arm B), then roofers on "quote that sells + same day" against old quotes. Your pain research scores standalone old-quote chasing 25/45 ("vitamin") and same-day quote 32/45, and 55% of tradespeople in one vendor survey (n=140) admit losing a job to a slow quote [LEARNINGS 6-7]. No verdict before 500 firms per arm (`HARD_TO_FAIL_PLAN.md`).

**5. Give first, only to people who reply.** Mass free audits and videos spend your time before you know who is real. After a positive reply or a sizing answer, send (a) the exact homeowner text with their firm name filled in, (b) an offer to read five old quotes on a 10-minute call, no export, (c) a 60-second video recap after the call. This is the "reverse lead magnet" (perceived value rises with effort [S21, VENDOR]) aimed at warm people; video evidence is vendor-only [S19].

**Leave alone for now:** sequence length (keep 3), subject wording, send time, spintax (the course's "mandatory" claim is unverified [S21]), links in follow-up 3. **Reply handling** (from the course, VENDOR [S21]): same thread, same inbox, within the hour; courtesy reply to soft nos; nudge a week after your reply; match replies from other addresses.

**Honest scenarios for day 90 (GUESS).** Low: 0-1 pilot (cold email quiet, warm intros weak); fix offer and trade before 15 Dec per `PATH_6_MONTHS.md`. Middle: 2-4 pilots signed, 1 case-study permission, 1 partner conversation advancing. High: 5-6 pilots, with a fabricator or trade body naming installers. **Ten clients by day 90 is not in the realistic range.** Ten by about month 6-8 is plausible if the offer holds, since pilots and referrals compound.

### From 10 to 50 (months 4-12, GUESS)

Every pilot yields two names, so warm referrals can double the base before cold email is needed. One fabricator or trade-body yes beats a thousand cold emails but moves in months and wants proof. Add kitchens and Ireland rather than re-mailing the same 7,500 firms; re-touch non-repliers after 90 days with the case study as the new angle. Letters, ads and paid listings come after the first invoices.

## 7. Guru hype flagged

- **"Cold email is dead / 96% opens" (Taylor Haren's "Halo"):** a product pitch with his own numbers.
- **LeadGenJay's 7-hour course:** says "not sponsored" yet carries affiliate links and a paid community; US-centric; advises dropping the opt-out line, which is bad advice in the UK [S21]. Use the deliverability and reply-handling ideas only.
- **"Reply in 5 minutes = 60% higher conversion":** unverified.
- **Loom "4x replies":** Loom's own claim; tiny-sample brags are vendor marketing [S19].
- **Roofing agency "7-14% reply":** self-report by a seller of the service [S20].
- **Open rates:** unreliable (Apple preloading; tracking lowers replies [S1][S2]).
- **"2-5% of old leads book":** vendor figures in your own files, not independently supported.
- **"First 10 clients in days":** works for people with a network to sell to. Warm intros are your version.
