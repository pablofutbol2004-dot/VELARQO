## 1. Missed-call text-back / AI receptionist

- **Price.** Erhart treats MCTB as a "high-profit" add-on, a button you push inside GHL, not a standalone product [AE @3:01:38, 14:17:04]. Every price point in the corpus is a bundle: reputation management $197-297/mo [AE2]; Clay's review product $1,500 setup + $429/mo, later ~$100-300/mo tiers by volume [CL @13:41, 16:49]. No operator gives a clean standalone MCTB or AI-receptionist price.
- **How sold.** Audit-as-pitch: send a free report, point out the single biggest hole (missed calls, unclaimed reviews), offer to fix just that [Nick Saraev Daily 6Q10E6JMYcU @8:23]. Erhart's script lists MCTB as one line in a menu [AE @54:41]. Hype flag: "lazy, passive, runs itself" framing [AE2].
- **Churn / where it broke.** Erhart: 76% of clients cancel traditional marketing within 90 days; adding software "drastically lowers" it [AE @14:12:55]. Unsourced, and he sells GHL. Clay's cheap review clients stayed because they "don't expect you to move mountains", but his model worked face to face and struggled once he used ads and cold outreach [CL @14:42, 17:52]. His later postmortem: pay-per-review made revenue unpredictable and attracted bad customers [OP @FObpNztnuug].
- **Bundled with.** Reviews, reactivation, web chat, follow-up, booking, website; SaaS as a down-sell so cancelled $2.5-4k clients park at a low-ticket plan [AE @5:04:28, 15:41:44].
- **Dissent worth keeping.** Zack Kirk: "you simply cannot start with AI receptionists", owners don't trust the AI [S2L]. Middleton: a standalone receptionist is easy to replace; systems beat agents [JP]. Serge: a voice agent for receptionist work "is not going to give you $15,000", unlike one tied to sales conversion [SG 58CoEov @2:25:10]. Internal rating: MCTB is a feature, not a company (7/10) [S2L].

## 2. Conversion infrastructure (speed-to-lead, follow-up, booking) as monthly retainer

- **Prices.** Middleton: $499 reactivations growing to $1.5-2.5k/mo, some over $10k; live call $1,500 reactivation plus reviews/referrals at $250/mo per club on a 12-month term; preferred vendor to a gym network [JP]. Erhart package ladder $4k / $2.5k / $1.5k with a ~$500 low tier [AE @8:30:02]. Ganim: free 15-min assessment, $999 paid assessment, $1-2k/mo "concierge", $1-5k upsells [CG]. Noah Haupt (home services): $150 per attended estimate + $1k setup, or $2k for 15 appointments [OP @nyx7OidDjmQ].
- **One-off to monthly.** Middleton: reactivation opens the door, then he sells the whole system and says "no ads yet, too much low-hanging fruit in your database" [JP]. Ganim: the paid diagnosis becomes the concierge subscription, with a Notion hub showing accumulated value [CG]. Serge: "people stop buying when you have nothing else to sell them", so space modules out [SG 58CoEov churn passage]. Pure DB reactivation is front-loaded; the pool shrinks (8,000 to 500 records) and does not create MRR alone [OP].
- **What the client must do.** Provide data and CRM access, keep calendar capacity, take over hot leads, report outcomes [agency_ops digest]. Clay keeps support low because clients "aren't logging in" [ghl digest, CHxt2VDkBEw].
- **Why they left.** Serge's 2021 appointment-setting agency (~$30k/mo): clients churned at 30-60 days, he says not for delivery but because their offers, sales process and scripts were bad [SG _yuyaiTgaWI @47:32]. Noah Haupt churned most of his first ~10 performance/free-trial clients [NH]. Erhart: deliver visible ROI in the first 90 days [AE @14:33:01].
- **Hype flags.** Middleton's "Harvard 400%" speed stat is wrong; never use it [S2L]. Serge's "$600k/mo", "$7 cost per lead" and "one-tenth cost per call" are unaudited and sell his program.

## 3. Own the demand (ads to appointments / leads)

- **Pricing patterns.** Noah Haupt: beginner version $300-500 upfront + ~$100 per appointment [NH @34:14]; his ladder runs pay-per-lead, per-lead + setup, guaranteed leads in a window, per-appointment, with per-client/job the strongest; $2-3k+ retainers once results exist. Another variant: $500 upfront + $100/appointment vs $2k/mo [OP @ZInH_RIDXQA]. Tim Schucker (selling leads, high-ticket buyers incl. home remodelers): pay-per-X spectrum, the closer to "paid per client" the more pricing power [TS @4:12].
- **Capital.** Schucker: never generate a lead before it is sold; the buyer prepays and funds ad spend, you keep the difference [TS @6:16-8:23]. Haupt: the client needs roughly 4-5x ROI on ad spend or cannot afford a fee; with $300 jobs a fee over ~$300-400/mo makes no sense, with $10k jobs $3-4k/mo does [NH @1:27:59]. Velarqo's rule: client funds spend, Velarqo never finances [meta_ads digest].
- **Entry conditions named.** High-ticket jobs, not $200 ones [NH, TS]; follow-up already working, because cheap leads that don't show waste $300-500 per call [SG _yuyaiTgaWI @4:58:10] (Serge's fix: a human contacts every opt-in within 5-10 min); Middleton puts ads last [JP].
- **How often it fails.** No operator gives a failure rate. Evidence: Haupt churned most early performance clients [NH]; Clay's $15-16k/mo ad agency was "awful... clients not getting results" [CL @5:15]; Serge's clients churned in 1-2 months [SG]; per-sale billing invites attribution disputes at five-figure ticket sizes [home_services digest]. "10 jobs in 30 days or $20,000" reads as a scam to experienced owners [OP @VjtFDC0VevA].

## What Velarqo should copy / ignore

1. **Copy: audit-as-pitch** (show one hole, fix one thing) [Saraev]. Velarqo version: the free quote-leak scan.
2. **Copy: bill on the attended survey, deposit credited against fees**, max two charge mechanisms [OP]; matches Haupt's $150 per attended estimate.
3. **Copy: enter with one module, then expand through the lifecycle** (Middleton): quote follow-up first, speed-to-lead and missed-call text-back as bundled safety nets.
4. **Copy: qualify the client's data, CRM and calendar capacity before pricing**; Haupt's fee-vs-job-value rule is the ICP filter.
5. **Ignore: MCTB or AI receptionist as a headline offer.** No operator charges for it alone; Kirk and Middleton warn against leading with it.
6. **Ignore: owning demand (ads) for now.** Every operator who ran ads for small clients either churned them fast or relied on prior proof; revisit after a case study, client-funded.
7. **Ignore US price anchors** ($100/appointment, $197 review plans, "$10k in X months" titles); derive from UK installer gross profit per attended survey. The honest quote is Clay's: it takes longer than anyone tells you.
8. **Copy the churn lesson:** clients leave when their own offer or sales process is weak (Serge), so screen out bad-fit installers and keep a next-module roadmap to sell. Never repeat the "Harvard 400%" stat.
