# KB digest: landing page for sceptical UK tradespeople with no proof yet (2026-10-11)

**Sources:** `D:\knowledge_base` (`digests/`, `chat_notes/`, `topics/`, `chatgpt/` batch files, `data/transcripts/`). Tags are `[file]` or `[video id, channel]`. **Coverage is thin:** the KB has no transcript about landing pages for UK trades, and only 375 transcripts sit on disk (the 18 database-reactivation videos exist only as ChatGPT batch text, if at all). Most page advice comes from info-product or agency sellers, so it is adapted by judgment, and labelled as such. Revenue and conversion figures are self-reported unless noted.

## What this means for velarqo.com

1. **One page, one job, one button.** Headline, plain-English explanation, who it is for, three-step "how it works", then a single CTA. Skip the multi-page site [OKKI0RbOo6M, Adam Erhart; -9ngF81_NjM, Adam Erhart].
2. **Lead with the outcome in the tradesperson's own words**: old quotes that went quiet, brought back as booked surveys. No "AI", GHL or "database reactivation" in the hero [home_services; sales_closing].
3. **Replace missing testimonials with specificity and honesty**: real name and face, what exactly happens, how payment works, and a plain statement that you are new. No invented proof, no avatars [meta_ads_local; 6Q10E6JMYcU, Nick Saraev].
4. **Treat "they're my leads" and "how do I know it works" as the two main objections** and answer them on the page, not in a call [sales_closing].
5. **Use the pricing model as the risk reversal**: you are paid per booked survey, a small first batch is shown before any commitment, and no revenue promises [offers_pricing; dbr_research_passes_2-3].
6. **Publish the money mechanics** (fee per booked survey, what counts as booked, who owns the data). Hiding price breeds mistrust [_XmzVYmVfJM, Wes McDowell, adapted]. **Overridden by house rule:** no price figure on the site (PRODUCT.md); the page states the mechanism ("a fee per booked survey, agreed on a call") and the booked-survey definition instead.
7. **Show the real thing**: one sample text thread beats adjectives; a worked "what 200 old quotes could mean" example needs a conversion rate we can't back, so it stays on the call, with the installer's own numbers [dbr_research_passes_2-3; batch3_instant_lead_response].
8. **Design for a thumb on a phone**: key message and button without scrolling, short blocks, little video [-9ngF81_NjM; 0t93nIBhQ-g].
9. **Match the cold email.** The visitor googled the name, so the page must answer "who is this, is it legit, what do you want" in seconds [OKKI0RbOo6M; chatgpt cold-outreach batches].
10. **Say what you do not do**: no cold calling, honest about no case studies, can stop any time, anything sensitive (complaints, disputes) passed to the installer the same day.

## Headline and above the fold

- Heading makes the big statement and a subheading explains it in plain English; one to three promises at most [0t93nIBhQ-g, Shan Hanif].
- A business owner who Googles you wants two answers: what you do, and will it help me. The headline should name the outcome with no jargon [OKKI0RbOo6M].
- Above the fold needs a headline, a subheadline and either a CTA or a trust cue; an empty hero graphic wastes it [XCrDp5KLsIU, Sweat Equity, a DTC store review; applies in principle].
- Specific beats generic: name the trade and the pain (agency examples add a niche term such as "closings" for real estate) [-9ngF81_NjM].
- Local-trade lead pages that work are described as headline, call-out, form, nothing else [MliVWaMuvGw, Full AI SMMA Course]. That page serves homeowner leads, not a vendor, so borrow only its brevity.

## Page order

A workable sequence, merged from the sources: hero (headline, subheadline, CTA) → one line of "who it is for" → the problem in a few sentences → how it works in three steps → what you get and what it costs → who is behind it → objections/FAQ → repeat CTA [0t93nIBhQ-g; OKKI0RbOo6M].

- Do not dwell on the problem. Sceptical adults see through hammering the pain; move to the solution quickly [0t93nIBhQ-g].
- Be upfront about exactly what is bought; do not hold detail back for a call [0t93nIBhQ-g].
- Case studies and descriptions below the fold matter "significantly less" than people expect [-9ngF81_NjM].
- **Plain tone model:** a home-service recovery vendor explains it as making sure follow-up is not forgotten, not replacing the owner; says it avoids blasting the whole database [7cv2z0ixi2s, Relevategen].

## Trust when you have no proof

- Prospects look you up first, and an empty web presence reads as bot or scam; a real name, real profile and wins and losses make you look human [chatgpt cold-outreach batches, several channels].
- Use your real founder name; trust is the number-one hurdle in cold outreach [chatgpt cold-outreach batches].
- Faces build trust for high-ticket work at the home; Velarqo itself can stay faceless, but this audience is buying a stranger's service, so a real face and name on the page is the safer call (judgment) [meta_ads_local].
- "Founder proof" (why trust this person) is a standard section even in pages with testimonials [0t93nIBhQ-g].
- Without case studies, one operator advises a result-based guarantee and a statement that you run the system on your own business, accepting a lower close rate [6Q10E6JMYcU, Nick Saraev]. Another suggests deposit-plus-milestone structures [58CoEov_sxk, Serge Gatari]. **[HYPE/UNVERIFIED]** for the "guarantee revenue" part; it conflicts with the house rule below.
- Over-stuffed testimonial walls look like showing off; people are not naive [0t93nIBhQ-g].
- **Do not** use fake scarcity, fake reviews or AI "homeowners" [0t93nIBhQ-g; meta_ads_local].

## Objection handling and risk reversal

- Biggest objection: owners feel the leads are theirs and would have closed anyway. Answer: define the untouched cohort (quoted or enquired, no sale, quiet for 90 to 180+ days, not being worked); without Velarqo it brings £0 [sales_closing].
- Price objections are usually trust objections; answer with a pilot and lower risk, not smoother copy [sales_closing].
- "Who is this brand, are they real" is the top silent objection of cold visitors [aaOYHwg_RzQ, e-commerce email course; analogy only].
- Risk reversal that works in 2026 is casual and plain ("if it's not a fit, no hard feelings"), not a stack of money-back clauses [yX5uiphK7NM, Aaron Shepherd]. Another seller warns that heavy guarantees make buyers numb [-9ngF81_NjM].
- **House stance:** no numerical guarantee in first pilots; pay-on-result is the reversal; "30 sales in 30 days" reads as a scam to an 18-year owner; guarantees only later with causal conditions [offers_pricing].
- Contract points worth mirroring in an FAQ: what counts as a qualified or booked survey, no-shows, attribution window, client reports won/lost [dbr_research_passes_2-3].
- Pre-empt capacity worries: roll out slowly so the installer's team can cope with the bookings [XWbzdHSRtcQ, Damian Qualter].

## CTA and video

- Exactly one action; competing options confuse [0t93nIBhQ-g; OKKI0RbOo6M]. Link it to a low-commitment step (a short chat, "show me on a small batch"), not checkout [0t93nIBhQ-g].
- Avoid "does that make sense?"; invite "what questions do you have?" [MliVWaMuvGw].
- Video: one source says always include one [0t93nIBhQ-g]; another says a photo of yourself is acceptable, video just converts higher [-9ngF81_NjM]; a third claims almost nobody real watches VSLs [xFncbLb3U1A, Serge Gatari, opinion]. **My read:** a short founder clip (under about 60 seconds, captioned, not autoplaying) is optional, never required; the page must work with sound off. "VSL went from 18% to 43%" [rbJRkAtfs5s, Lorenzo Gemma] is **[HYPE/UNVERIFIED]** and coaching-specific.
- Ongoing offer: a demo or "I'll run it on a small batch at our cost" outsells a pretty site [dbr_research_passes_2-3].

## Mobile and readability

- One source claims around 92% of visitors are on mobile **[UNVERIFIED]**; design phone-first regardless [0t93nIBhQ-g]. Another store review reports about 90% mobile engagement [XCrDp5KLsIU].
- Tools: free session recordings and heatmaps (Microsoft Clarity) to see real behaviour [0t93nIBhQ-g]. Page-structure tests come after traffic exists.
- Use "you" and first person [0t93nIBhQ-g].

## What to avoid

- Selling features or tools (GHL, workflows, AI) instead of the result [-9ngF81_NjM; home_services].
- Several offers or categories on one page [0t93nIBhQ-g].
- Fake urgency or scarcity [0t93nIBhQ-g].
- Revenue promises or guarantees the installer's own sales team controls [offers_pricing].
- Hidden pricing and vague "book a call" gates [_XmzVYmVfJM].
- Slick agency language; the house rule already says copy should be tradesperson-plain [project memory; sales_closing: "keep AI low"].
- Overbuilding before testing: a live one-page site beats a perfect funnel [OKKI0RbOo6M; cold_outreach #14].
