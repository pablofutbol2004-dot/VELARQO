# Red team: what kills Velarqo, and what I'd bet on instead (11 Oct 2026)

Written as an operator and investor deciding whether to back the next 6 months. Built on the 13 files in the brief, `web_research/2026-10-11_strategy_channels.md` (read last; see 6.3), and about 25 web checks done today.

Labels: **VERIFIED** = primary or official source opened today, or our own DB count. **VENDOR** = published by someone selling into this market. **ANECDOTAL** = one case or article. **GUESS** = my judgement. "(secondary)" = seen in a law-firm or press summary, not the primary text. Not legal advice. **SOLICITOR** = needs a UK lawyer.

---

## 0. The verdict in five lines

1. **The core delivery, as written today, is mostly unlawful.** Texting or emailing old quotes needs an opt-out offered *when the details were collected*. Our own scan finds one on about 2% of installer sites, and phone enquiries almost never get one. The ICO says that without it, further marketing texts "would breach PECR". Cold email v2 *shows the installer the SMS we'd send*: we are pitching them an illegal act.
2. **The pain is unproven, and two headline numbers don't say what our files say.** The 38% (HomeServe) is homeowners who never *got* a quote. Business Pilot's "6 in 10" is leads, not quotes.
3. **Founder hours are the real ceiling.** At 10-20 h a week, half of it selling, he can deliver for 2-3 monthly clients in term time. A realistic month-12 income is **£1k-4.5k a month**. That's real money in Spain, but it is not "rich".
4. **No pivot needed. Change the delivery channel and the centre of gravity.** Old quotes go by **post** (outside PECR) with a "text us back" line, so every message after that answers the homeowner. The thing we sell monthly is the **forward-looking product**: opt-out added at intake, every new quote followed up, same-evening quote desk.
5. **Ship on 25 Oct.** The first 1,000 sends are the only research that matters now. Research freeze until 15 Dec, this file included.

## 3. Legal reality check on the core delivery

### 3.1 The rules (homeowners are individuals)

- **Email and SMS marketing** need consent or the soft opt-in: details collected by the installer in negotiations, similar products, **an opt-out at collection**, and an opt-out in every message (VERIFIED, ICO). Lead-site contacts fail.
- **A chase is marketing** if it encourages a purchase. A "can we keep in touch?" text is marketing too, so the consent-request workaround is closed (VERIFIED, ICO).
- **Sender and instigator are both liable** (VERIFIED, ICO PECR guide). The installer instigates and Velarqo sends.
- **Live calls** by the installer: allowed to numbers that aren't TPS-registered and haven't objected. TPS numbers need specific consent "even if they are an existing customer" (VERIFIED, ICO telephone marketing). TPS covers mobiles too (GUESS-strong; check tpsonline.org.uk).
- **Post** is outside PECR. UK GDPR legitimate interests applies, with a written assessment (LIA), transparency, and honouring objections. The ICO has said postal marketing doesn't need consent where it's proportionate and expected (secondary, 2018; **SOLICITOR** to confirm after the DUAA).

### 3.2 What Velarqo can lawfully do

| Situation | SMS / email | Post | Installer's own calls |
|---|---|---|---|
| Old quote, opt-out offered at collection | Yes, with STOP in every message | Yes | Yes, TPS-screened |
| **Old quote, no opt-out (about 9 in 10, GUESS)** | **No**, not even a consent request | **Yes**, with an LIA and an easy objection route | Yes, non-TPS numbers only |
| Lead-site contact (Checkatrade, Bark...) | No | Probably (**SOLICITOR**: platform terms) | TPS rule |
| Homeowner replies to our letter by text or WhatsApp | **Yes**: answering their own message | — | Yes |
| New enquiry: first reply, sending the quote, "did it arrive?" | Yes: service, not marketing (GUESS on "did it arrive", **SOLICITOR**) | — | Yes |
| Chasing a **new** quote to a yes or no | Yes, **if the intake offered an opt-out** ("we'll send your quote and follow up a couple of times; reply STOP any time") | — | Yes |

Ledger #24: 2% of 14,384 trade homepages mention opt-out (VERIFIED, own DB; homepage text understates form small print a little). Many enquiries arrive by phone, the Papa John's pattern. **Planning assumption: under 10% of any installer's old quotes can lawfully get SMS or email.**

### 3.3 A lawful, scalable model: "letter + text-back + intake fix"

1. **Old quotes get a personalised letter** in the installer's name: "You asked us to quote for your windows in March. If you're still thinking about it, text YES to 07xxx and we'll find a time. If not, there's nothing to do and we won't write again." It is calm, the opposite of the double-glazing scar.
2. **Replies go to a number Velarqo runs for the installer.** Every message after that answers the homeowner's request. Booking confirmations and reminders are service messages (VERIFIED, ICO examples).
3. **Opt-outs** go to the installer's suppression list.
4. **Day 1 of the pilot:** opt-out wording on the web form, one line in the office phone script, and a quote-email footer. Every **new** quote can then be chased lawfully. This is the bridge to monthly.
5. Optional: **installer-made calls** to high-value non-TPS old quotes, from a list Velarqo sorts by value.

**Cost.** All-in print and post costs 64-73p a letter at government bulk rates (VERIFIED, GOV.UK Notify pricing, not open to us). Royal Mail second class is 91p from April 2026 (VERIFIED, press). Commercial pay-as-you-go hybrid mail is probably £0.80-1.30 (GUESS; Stannp's 2026 price not found). 300 letters ≈ **£250-400, paid by the installer at cost**. That doesn't touch the €0 rule, and it filters out tyre-kickers the way PRICING's deposit idea intended.

**Response.** US industry benchmarks for house-list mail are cited at 5-9% (ANA/DMA via vendor pages; secondary, disputed). A Royal Mail-commissioned UK study puts *cold B2B* letters at about 1% (channels file, S17). A letter from a known firm to someone who asked it for a quote sits nearer the house-list end (GUESS). **Plan on 1-3% booked, measured against a holdout.**

**The pitch this unlocks:** "Quote follow-up done properly: a letter from you, not spam texts. Anyone interested texts back."

### 3.4 Exposure

- **Installer:** instigator under PECR and controller under GDPR. Under the current SMS design, the risk covers almost every record. The practical cost is local reputation (GUESS).
- **Pablo:** liable as sender. If he decides the timing, content and targeting he may be a **joint controller**, not a processor (**SOLICITOR**). As an EU-established business he may need a **UK GDPR Art. 27 representative** and may carry EU GDPR processor duties (**SOLICITOR** + Spanish lawyer). Homeowner SMS sent from Spain may bring **LSSI art. 21** into play, separate from the cold-email LSSI risk he has already accepted (**abogado**).
- With letters, most of the tail risk disappears.

**Get a fixed-fee quote now (pay after the first sale) for these questions:** (1) the letter + text-back model and an LIA template; (2) whether one "here's your quote / any questions?" message is a service message; (3) intake opt-out wording that creates a valid soft opt-in; (4) processor vs joint controller; (5) Art. 27; (6) lead-site contacts by post; (7) the liability cap and indemnity in the client agreement.

## 4. Offer stress test

**Shared assumptions (GUESS):** 8 inboxes × 20/day = 160 sends ≈ 53-56 new firms a day (matches the channels file). The 4,335 established firms are used up by about mid-February at full speed. Cold, no proof: positive replies **0.3% / 0.8% / 1.5%**. Positive → call 50%; call → pilot 25-40%; pilot → paying 50-70%. That's roughly **0.3 / 1 / 2.5 paying clients per 1,000 firms**. Six months ≈ 4,000 firms. Delivery capacity in term is **5-8 h a week**.

**(a) Old-quote recovery, per booked survey (letter version).**
- *Who buys:* established window firms and kitchen showrooms with 200+ old quotes and no CRM.
- *Close in 6 months:* **1 / 3 / 8** paying pilots.
- *Per client:* 300 quotes × 1% / 2.5% / 4% = 3 / 7.5 / 12 surveys × £100 = **£300 / £750 / £1,200, once**.
- *Revenue:* month 6 ≈ **£0 / £600 / £2,500** that month, lumpy; month 12 ≈ **£0 / £800 / £3,000**. It doesn't compound.
- *Hours:* 6-10 h setup per client, then 1-2 h a week for 4-6 weeks.
- *Must be true:* quotes carry postal addresses (near-certain, since the survey is at the house), 1%+ book, the client pays postage.
- *Verdict:* **door-opener and proof engine.**

**(b) Monthly conversion layer: intake opt-out + every new quote chased + same-evening quote desk, £300-500 a month.**
- *Who buys:* owner-led window firms and showrooms who quote at night.
- *Close:* mostly converted from pilots: **1 / 3 / 6 by month 6; 2 / 6 / 12 by month 12** with 6-8% monthly churn.
- *MRR:* month 6 **£350 / £1,200 / £2,700**; month 12 **£750 / £2,400 / £5,400**.
- *Hours:* ~20 quotes a month × 20-30 min, plus replies and a report ≈ **2.5-4 h a week per client** (maybe 1.5-2 h with AI-drafted quotes approved in one tap). Capacity is **2-3 clients in term, 6-8 in summer**; the high case needs a VA by summer 2027.
- *Must be true:* owners send notes and prices the same evening (no survey says they will; ask on every call), and PRICING's "human within 1 working hour, 9-7" is dropped in favour of an instant template reply plus an evening human reply.
- *Verdict:* **this is the business.**

**(c) White-label to agencies and lead vendors.**
- *Who buys:* about 15-30 targets. Glasgrowth already does old quotes in-house. The "£60k in 60 days" agencies will want the SMS version, which moves *their* risk onto Pablo as sender.
- *Close:* **0 / 1 / 2**.
- *Revenue:* month 6 **£0 / £400 / £1,500**; month 12 **£0 / £800 / £3,000**.
- *Hours:* heavy.
- *Verdict:* answer if one asks; don't spend January on it.

**(d) What I'd add.**
- **Plan B for the 15 Dec trigger: B2B outbound for UK B2B service firms** (commercial cleaning, fit-out, signage, security). The engine he built *is* the product: booked meetings with other companies. B2B email to corporate subscribers is allowed (VERIFIED, ICO), so there's no homeowner-consent problem. It's remote, with no calls (the client takes the meeting). Against: crowded, churny, and more LSSI exposure from sending for clients out of Spain (**abogado**). At £750-1,500 a month, 2 clients ≈ £2-3k by month 12. His own campaign numbers become its first case study.
- **Skip:** missed-call text-back (a sub-£50 commodity), finance introducing (FCA), recruitment (needs calls), review tools.

**Month 12, mid case:** (a) ~£800 lumpy, (b) ~£2,400 MRR, (c) ~£800, Plan B ~£2,000. Only (b), fed by (a), compounds.

## 5. What nobody has raised (or raised and didn't act on)

1. **The cold email advertises the unlawful act.** v2 email 1 shows the SMS, and the channels file says to send "the exact homeowner text" after a reply. Show the letter instead.
2. **Two headline stats are misread** (2.4, 2.7) and sit in the copy bank. Take them out of the old-quote arguments.
3. **Value per booked survey is now estimable:** £3,700-7,400 job × ~30% close (Glasgrowth data) × ~30% gross margin (GUESS) ≈ **£330-670 gross profit**. £100-150 per survey is an easy yes on the numbers. The obstacle is trust, not price.
4. **Holdout on pilot 1, not pilot 2-3** (as the channels file suggests). Hold back 20% of old quotes at random and send them nothing. It kills "they're my leads", makes invoices hard to dispute, and makes the case study credible. It costs nothing.
5. **The 1-hour human SLA can't be met by a student** (PRICING, channels file). Promise an instant reply plus a human the same evening. Spain is UK +1, so evenings work.
6. **January is both the window-trade rebound and probably exams.** Size pilots to fit.
7. **Money from Spain.**
   - No Spanish IVA on B2B services to UK businesses (GUESS from the IVA place-of-supply rule, art. 69 LIVA; **gestor** for invoice wording).
   - A non-VAT-registered UK client must count our fees towards its own VAT threshold (VERIFIED, HMRC VATPOSS14700, via search summary).
   - Velarqo shouldn't need UK VAT registration (GUESS-strong).
   - The day he registers, €0 ends: cuota ~€80-88/month, gestor ~€40-80/month (GUESS), quarterly 303/130 filings even at zero. Register the week the first invoice is certain.
8. **Insurance:** installers or their insurers may ask for PI or cyber cover, which is awkward for a Spanish autónomo to buy (GUESS). Have the honest answer ready: processor under a DPA, deletion after 30 days, no card or health data.
9. **Homeowner data security:** GHL (USA) plus a laptop means transfer questions (UK→EU→US) and breach duties. Minimum: full-disk encryption, no attachments, written deletion dates.
10. **The 2% proxy is also a product.** Almost every installer needs the intake fix: form wording, a phone line and a quote footer. It's concrete, cheap and useful before any survey books, and it's the legal foundation of the monthly product.
11. **The list is enough for offer tests, not for six months of full-speed sending.** 4,335 established firms ≈ 8 slices of 500. Young roofers (about 2,100 under 5 years) have no backlog: keep them out of the first 1,500. List growth (45k window companies in the DB without email, plus kitchens and extensions) runs weekly from November.
12. **How clients measure value:** they count jobs and pounds, not surveys. Add a 60-day "did it become a job?" check. Report pounds of quotes reopened.
13. **Churn:** assume 6-8% a month; trade marketing services churn fast (ANECDOTAL: Checkatrade complaints, Glasgrowth's 2-month minimum).
14. **The video asset:** installers go on camera for Glasgrowth. Ask the first happy client on day 1.

- **Letters.** It parks letters as a costly *acquisition* channel to installers (first class, £1.80). I use them as the *delivery* channel to homeowners: second class or bulk, paid by the client, so the €0 rule isn't touched.
