## 1. Landing the first 1-3 clients with no proof

- **Make the offer carry the proof.** Clarence Nap (zero experience, no testimonials) says a promised outcome, delivered fast and done-for-you, stands in for proof. His line: "10 qualified meetings in 30 days, you only pay if they show up" [2MziP5pcuDo@1:54, @12:57]. Noah Haupt agrees: with no results, "perceived likelihood of achievement" is low, so remove about 99% of the risk and keep it to 30 days or less [8ba9cCMt22g@6:17].
- **Free pilots versus pay-on-result (the KB disagrees).**
  - Charlie Morgan: "never do free", because owners distrust something they receive without giving. Pay-on-result gets "10 times more clients than free" [IFLM1LrPNtc@24:34]. He also says to cold-outreach with no testimonials, since it's chicken-and-egg [@12:44]. Treat the first clients as proof of concept, not income [@0:00].
  - Noah Haupt, two beginner offers [8ba9cCMt22g@8:22-10:28]:
    - Free trial plus a small deposit. The deposit buys commitment because free clients ghost. He suggests about $50, refundable.
    - Deposit (about $300) plus about $100 per qualified appointment, then a retainer after 30 days. Show the maths: 5 appointments, 2 closed, £3k each.
  - Austin Automates: "you don't pay a dime until I book your first 10 appointments", then a build fee and monthly fee, month-to-month, asking for 90 days if it works [8LDHDR5FzR8@6:20-10:34]. **Warning:** that is an open-ended guarantee, and `offers_pricing.md` already rejects it. His "$110k" is unverified.
  - Fits the existing decision (credited deposit plus fee per attended survey).
- **Proof by demo or work up front.**
  - Jay Yang: reduce the friction of saying yes by doing the strategy first [`wl_reviews/2026-10-04.md`, 3:09]. Mapped to Velarqo, that is a mini-estimate in the email: "~X old quotes, ~£Z recoverable".
  - Rasmussen: opener built on public data (their Google review count against a named local rival), a soft CTA, and a prospect-specific demo built before the reply [16t-36TIRE0@15:44]. His results are unquantified and his review-gating conflicts with `reviews_referrals.md`.
  - Counter-view: Charlie Morgan wants a single path (book a call), with no audit or Loom in the first email [1rRo1xYacGo@48:31]. `cold_outreach.md` says to send a Loom only after interest.
- **Self-run test as proof.** JP Middleton started with $499 reactivations and a "14-day trial" on a former-member list, then used the review as proof [TovKT7FEsF8@15:22; iTOoJp4kz4M@33:35]. He got his first customer from a week of DMs [iTOoJp4kz4M@12:29]. His example is a US gym.
- **Free reactivation as a closer for a bigger sale:** run it first to get quick wins [MasQp-qoJFY@17:05]. Treat it as a tactic, not proof.
- **Fazio's warning applies to the cold email itself.** "We'll call your old leads" reads as scalping money you already have, so a cold audience won't buy it [R1ZHAUSKSSo@7:11-9:44]. Frame it as *new* survey bookings from quotes the installer has written off. `sales_closing.md` agrees.

## 2. Why DBR agencies fail or churn, and how they became recurring

- **The list runs out.** Wardrope: "it's a finite list", and clients are slow to call back and then blame lead quality [llUqU2E6xR4@1:04-2:08]. DBR is the "foot in the door". Speed-to-lead is "where the passive money is" [@3:12]. His other follow-ons are customer-support bots and document collection [@4:15-8:27]. The two-step "hand-raise" and "300 replies / 30 bookings per 1,000" are vendor claims (`dbr_research_passes_2-3.md`).
- **Zero-risk pricing attracts bad clients.** Clay Lawrence: pay-per-review gave unpredictable revenue, worse customers and more complexity, and cost over $10k of MRR [FObpNztnuug@0:23-0:43].
- **Client can't absorb the leads.** Qualter: his team test 100 names, then 500. If the sales team can't cope there is "no availability for 3 weeks", and conversions die [XWbzdHSRtcQ@5:22]. A message must say who you are, where from, and the relationship [@0:01].
- **Channel.** Qualter and Shemar Ellis say email is ignored for reactivation and SMS or WhatsApp works better [XWbzdHSRtcQ@1:06; jHETpVoOggs@2:05]. That's for the homeowner side, not your cold email to installers.
- **Compliance.** GHL Wizard shows the import consent check ("not from a third party") [MasQp-qoJFY@4:16]. It's US-oriented. The UK points (soft opt-in, Checkatrade/Bark leads quarantined, sender and instigator both liable) are **ChatGPT's, unverified** (`dbr_research_passes_2-3.md`).
- **Data friction and blame at invoice time.** `agency_ops.md`: late callbacks, missed surveys, reps marking leads lost. Fix with a scope table and a readiness gate before launch.
- **Recurring revenue routes:**
  - JP: $499 reactivation grew into $1.5-2.5k/month, some above $10k [iTOoJp4kz4M@33:35].
  - Austin: $2.5k setup plus $300-400/month, with a reviews add-on at $1.5k plus $500/month [8LDHDR5FzR8@6:20-7:24].
  - Noah: deposit and fee per appointment, then a $1.5k/month retainer [8ba9cCMt22g@10:28].
  - `agency_ops.md`: use the pilot postmortem as the renewal meeting, then diagnose live leakage.
  - Recurring fees need recurring value (speed-to-lead and quote chasing).

## 3. Not burning a small TAM

- **Your arithmetic (inf):** 16 mailboxes x 20/day = 320/day, so about 4,800 companies take roughly 15 sending days for one touch each. Test on slices first, not the whole list.
- Fazio's agency plateaued with a "super niche" market and a mediocre offer. A small TAM needs a good offer [TMkEDChOhyk@15:40-16:43].
- Leon Matschke, small TAM: personalised emails, add other channels, and **message every decision-maker** [cR1CgO2NHC0@7:23-11:38; UO07-RCClKM@13:38]. His claim that cold email works for 500-person markets is unquantified, and cold calling is out for you. His "new angle every 30 days to everyone" [UO07-RCClKM@12:34] is promotional and aggressive.
- Re-contact intervals: Shepherd says 3 emails max, then re-engage in 2-3 months [XuqivscJjTQ@1:02]. A 90-day cool-off with fresh messaging and a new angle appears in [RcTjoLcUtgw@21:58]. Taylor Haren suggests one email to the TAM every 60 days, starting with small slices [ES89-zWNgoY@5:14]. His scale claims are his own.
- Test material changes only, with a minimum send count written first (`watch_later_lessons.md`, Hormozi). Rerun a winner 3+ times.
- **Multiple contacts per company:** the KB says yes for B2B tech. For a Ltd installer, usually one or two directors (inf). PECR note from `cold_outreach.md`: Ltd/LLP only, sole traders need consent.

## 4. Partnership and referral channels

## 5. Speed and quitting

- Clarence Nap's claim: 250 emails/day for 10 days, 2% reply, 10-15 interested, so one client. "Most people send 100 emails, get discouraged and quit" [2MziP5pcuDo@24:01]. His numbers are illustrative.
- Noah Haupt: first clients in days, but through cold calling. He says it took him 5-6 months to leave the "struggle phase", and 90%+ quit in the first 100 calls [QKZ60Sv7xig@2:06-3:09]. Calling is excluded for you.
- Patrick Dang: don't judge cold email until thousands of sends [Z8oxIM1InTM@21:58]. Kai Stone: most quit before client one [uUZGrlrYCnw@3:08].
- Counter-evidence: JP took 9 months to his first $10k month [TovKT7FEsF8@0:22]. A first $2,000 deal took 50+ "no" calls over about 6 months (n=1, `watch_later_lessons.md`).
- Write the kill criteria before the data, so a miss triggers a change and not a bigger plan.

## Top 8 actionable moves

1. **Lead with the new-money frame:** "bookings from quotes you've written off", not "we'll message your old leads" [Fazio R1ZHAUSKSSo@7:11].
2. **Pilot:** small credited deposit plus a fee per attended survey, 30 days, 250 records, no numeric guarantee [Noah 8ba9cCMt22g@8:22; `offers_pricing.md`].
3. **Build a prospect-specific mini-estimate for the best 20-50 firms** and A/B it against the plain email [Jay Yang; Rasmussen].
4. **Send in slices:** about 400-500 companies per angle, a written minimum send count, then rerun winners 3+ times.
5. **Hold at least 60-90 days between touches,** each with a new angle. No monthly blasting [RcTjoLcUtgw@21:58; Shepherd XuqivscJjTQ@1:02].
6. **Contract the definitions:** qualified/attended, no-shows, attribution, exclusions, a cap on weekly leads the client can handle [Qualter @5:22; `agency_ops.md`].
7. **Pre-plan the second product** (quote chasing / speed-to-lead) so DBR isn't one-off [Wardrope @3:12].
8. **Start a partner list now** (UK trade bodies, buying groups, software vendors, Facebook groups) with a 30-minute-a-day cap [Erhart; JP]. Then judge cold email on thousands of sends, not hundreds.
