# Homeowner reply handling v0

## YES / interested
1. acknowledge context;
2. ask only missing qualification fields;
3. propose available appointment slots;
4. confirm date/time/address/contact;
5. write booking + source attribution;
6. send client notification.

Example:
> Great — happy to pick it back up. Is it still the replacement windows at {{address/postcode}}? If so, we have {{slot1}} or {{slot2}} for a survey. Which works better?

## “How much?”
Do not invent price.
> The old quote may need updating, so I don't want to give you the wrong figure here. I can arrange a quick re-check/survey and the team can price it properly. Would {{slot1}} or {{slot2}} work?

## “Maybe later”
> No problem. Roughly when would be more useful — {{month/window}}? I can leave it alone until then.

Only schedule future contact if the client's legal basis/process allows it and the homeowner's response supports it.

## “We already bought elsewhere”
> Thanks for letting me know — I’ll close this one off. All the best with it.

Mark closed. Do not keep selling.

## STOP / unsubscribe / don't contact
- no persuasion;
- confirm only if appropriate;
- suppress immediately across campaign;
- retain minimal suppression evidence needed to ensure they are not contacted again.

## Complaint / “how did you get my number?”
Human escalation.
Provide factual provenance from client data; do not improvise legal explanations.

## Wrong person / moved / deceased / sensitive context
Suppress and escalate where appropriate. Never continue automated persuasion.

## AI guardrails
AI may classify routine replies and draft responses.
AI must **not autonomously handle**:
- complaint/legal/privacy requests;
- threats;
- vulnerability/sensitive personal circumstances;
- ambiguous opt-out;
- pricing promises outside approved rules;
- discounts not explicitly authorised;
- materially unusual jobs.
