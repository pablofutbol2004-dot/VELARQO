# Cold email experiment v0 — installer acquisition

Goal: get **qualified conversations**, not maximise opens.

## Cohort design
Start with 4 cells of roughly equal quality. Avoid changing more than one major variable per cell.

### Opener A — problem question
Subject: `old quotes`

Hi {{first_name}},

Do you ever go back through window/door quotes that went quiet a few months ago?

I’m working with installers on a simple model: we follow those old enquiries up in your name and only charge when a qualified survey gets booked.

Worth seeing how it would work with your quote book?

Pablo
Velarqo

If you'd rather I didn't email again, just reply and I won't.

### Opener B — sunk acquisition cost
Subject: `quick question`

Hi {{first_name}},

You’ve probably got homeowners you already paid to generate, survey and quote — then they just went quiet.

Velarqo re-contacts those old quotes, handles the replies and puts the interested ones back into the diary. No setup fee for the pilot; we get paid on qualified bookings.

Useful if I send over the pilot structure?

Pablo
Velarqo

If you'd rather I didn't email again, just reply and I won't.

## CTA test
Within either opener, compare:
- **CTA 1 — conversation:** “Worth seeing how it would work with your quote book?”
- **CTA 2 — permission:** “Useful if I send over the pilot structure?”

Do not jump straight to a calendar link in email 1 during the first test. The first experiment is whether the mechanism earns a reply.

## Follow-up sequence

### +3 business days
Hi {{first_name}} — not sure if this sits with you.

Roughly, are we talking tens, hundreds or thousands of old quotes/enquiries sitting around?

If there isn't a meaningful backlog, I'll leave it there.

### +5 business days
One reason I'm testing this specifically with window/door firms: fresh booked appointments are already sold in the market, while old quotes are people you paid to acquire once already.

The pilot is deliberately simple: eligible old quotes → approved follow-up → interested homeowner → booked survey → pay per valid booking.

Worth a look?

### +7 business days — final
I'll close this out after this.

If you ever want to see whether there’s usable work sitting in your old quotes, send me a rough list size and I can tell you what I’d check before touching the data.

## Personalisation test
Do **not** generate fake compliments.

Test only useful factual context:
- town/service area;
- years trading when confidently sourced;
- products genuinely listed on site;
- FENSA/Certass where verified.

Example:
> Saw you cover {{town}} for replacement windows/doors — quick question about the quotes that don't go ahead.

Compare against no-personalisation control. If personalisation does not raise **positive reply rate**, remove the cost/complexity.

## Metrics per variant
- attempted
- delivered
- bounced
- total replies
- positive replies
- negative replies
- opt-outs
- qualified calls booked
- calls held
- pilots won

Ignore open rate as a primary decision metric.

## Stop / revise conditions
- unusual complaint/angry-response cluster;
- bounce spike;
- one variant produces materially more opt-outs with no positive lift;
- replies reveal offer confusion (“what are you selling?” repeatedly);
- prospects consistently lack an old-quote database.
