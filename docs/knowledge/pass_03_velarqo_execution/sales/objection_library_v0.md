# Sales objection library v0

Use these as **reasoning structures**, not scripts to recite.

## “How do I know it works?”
No case-study theatre.
> You don't yet — that's why I’m not asking for a long retainer. We inspect the list first, run a bounded pilot, and the billing unit is a qualified booking. The pilot is designed to answer whether it works on *your* data.

## “Why wouldn't we just call them ourselves?”
> You can. If your team already systematically works every old quote, this is probably redundant. The service is valuable when good enquiries are sitting untouched because nobody owns the follow-up consistently.

## “I don't want to annoy old customers.”
> Neither do I. We only want records that pass the data/compliance gate, use a short contextual message from your business, stop when someone says no, and suppress opt-outs. A campaign that creates complaints is a failed campaign even if it books work.

## “Our data is a mess.”
> That's one of the first things the pilot tests. Send a small redacted export. We can see whether the problem is just cleanup or whether the list is genuinely unusable before anything sends.

## “We don't have a CRM.”
> That's not automatically a problem. Spreadsheet or export from the quoting/job system can be enough. If everything is paper/inbox-only, we first see whether the expected recovered value justifies extraction.

## “£75 per booking is too much.”
Do not defend price abstractly.
> What does a booked survey normally cost you today, and roughly what percentage turn into a sale? If £75 doesn't leave you strong economics, we shouldn't force the model.

## “Can you guarantee sales?”
> No. I control the reactivation and booking process; I don't control your survey, pricing or close. The guarantee is around the result I can actually verify — the qualified booking and the no-show credit.

## “Do you use AI?”
> Some parts can be AI-assisted, but that isn't what you're buying. You're buying the recovered qualified survey and the system around it.
