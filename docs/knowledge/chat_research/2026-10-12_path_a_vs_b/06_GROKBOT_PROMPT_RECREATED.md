--- BEGIN COPYABLE PROMPT ---

You are a skeptical 2026 B2B market analyst. Your task is to decide whether a solo, technically capable founder based in Spain with 8 cold-email inboxes warming should test a US roofing **quote recovery / no-ads lead conversion** service, or reject it for a commercially better narrow business.

CONTEXT
- No existing clients, case studies, team, or proven sales operation. Limited weekly hours. Able to take short video sales calls; does not want in-person walk-ins, huge cold-calling campaigns, or building a faceless content audience before first revenue.
- Previous Path A: help B2B firms (fire protection, security, commercial HVAC/cleaning) win $20k+/year contracts with prospect lists, cold email, response handling, appointments and proposal follow-up. Rejected as too operationally complex.
- Path B lite: no paid ads; follow up existing inbound enquiries / missed calls / unsold quotes and book interested prospects. Candidate has been narrowed to a roofing quote follow-up service, not a full marketing agency.
- Proposed paid pilot $400–800 for 30 days; possible recurring $500–1,000/mo, entirely unvalidated.
- Strongest fears: inability to close, highly competitive US, seller saturation, no money, software already offering workflows, not enough inbound lead volume.

DECISION TO RESEARCH
Can this founder realistically get the first paying customer and earn profitably with this service, or would another narrow B2B offer make more sense?

REQUIRED EVIDENCE
1. Market demand: US roofing lead/quote volumes by business size; conversion rates, sales cycle, common loss reasons; credible data on actual late or absent follow-up; distinguish residential vs commercial roofing; show sample sizes and dates.
2. Customer economics: value and gross profit of incremental jobs, not just gross revenue; quote counts required to make a $500–1,000/mo service financially viable; estimate plausible uplift with transparent assumptions, pessimistic/base/optimistic scenarios.
3. Competitive substitutes: What features exist in Jobber, AccuLynx, ServiceTitan, JobNimbus, Housecall Pro, HighLevel, etc.? At what plan/tier? Is functionality actually used by SMB operators? Are failures technical, behavioral or commercial? Do agencies manage it already? Price actual competitors with live links and dates.
4. Buyer willingness to pay: Find dated, direct US roofing owner discussions, interviews, forum messages, job postings, purchase signals, product reviews and actual offers. Separate statements like "I need more leads" from willingness to pay for quote follow-up; avoid inferring from vendors' advertising.
5. Buyer accessibility: Are roofing decision makers reachable on LinkedIn, email, Facebook groups, trade suppliers, associations? Do not infer active LinkedIn prevalence from a handful of profiles. Look for observable distribution channels. How often are they solicited by marketers and agency owners? Use evidence rather than claiming everyone gets X pitches.
6. Delivery: Design a minimal 30-day pilot using the existing CRM, requiring appropriate authorization; access, opt-out, consent, human handoff, baseline and incremental outcome attribution, limits on making sales promises, privacy/security, and owner time. Estimate hours/week and hard costs.
7. Outreach: Research current and applicable legal limits on cross-border cold email from Spain to US prospects, as well as client SMS/email contact to old roofing leads. Give cautious, sourced advice; don't assume US law alone governs a Spanish sender.
8. Direct comparisons: Evaluate (A) roofing quote recovery, (B) local speed-to-lead/booking (no ads), (C) B2B outsourced appointment setting, (D) specialized CRM/process cleanup, (E) niche ads lead generation, (F) another underserved vertical you discover. Rank only after researching acquisition feasibility, trust threshold, delivery burden, retention, and ROI.
9. External validation: Audit 20–40 real identifiable roofing businesses of several sizes across 2–3 US regions, using only permitted public signals. Flag what is observable vs what must be asked. Never claim to know internal conversion or CRM usage from a public website.
10. Build a concrete *test-before-build* plan: 14-day user-interview/discovery plan; 100–250 target selection approach; examples of legitimate compliant outreach; what counts as positive willingness-to-pay; clear pivot/continue/stop criteria; what evidence cannot be learned from the web alone.

SOURCE RULES
- Use primary sources, official pricing/docs, survey methods, current customer complaints, owner communities and actual agency offers. Date each figure; cite direct URLs for every consequential claim. No fake numbers or invented customer quotes. For uncertain statistics, say "unknown" rather than extrapolating from unrelated trades.
- Challenge the premise; do not sell me the idea. If another model is better, pick it and explain why. Identify market demand separately from ability of a newcomer to acquire and close customers.
- No generic statements like "there's huge demand for AI" or "the market is worth billions." Focus on paid customer outcomes and conversion constraints.

OUTPUT
A. One-paragraph answer: pursue, pivot or reject; confidence and evidence gaps.
B. 5–8 model comparison matrix with supported demand, price, complexity, acquisition path, defensibility, and risks.
C. Customer economics with pessimistic/base/optimistic scenarios and assumptions.
D. Software capability and actual-use gap audit with URL evidence.
E. 20+ real-business mini-audits or a documented sampling limitation.
F. Target ICP, minimum qualifying lead/quote volumes, pilot scope and test price.
G. 14-day validation sprint and what evidence would change the recommendation.
H. Full annotated source list by quality and publication date.
