# Who we sell to: UK window, roofing and kitchen firms

Written 11 Oct 2026. One-page answer to "know the niche like an insider". Built from:
- **Our own list** (7,512 companies, Companies House data, counted 11 Oct) → `knowledge/kb_digest_2026-10-11_niche_owners.md`
- **UK web research** (surveys, trade press, forums) → `knowledge/web_research/2026-10-11_niche_ethnography_web.md`
- **Reddit pastes** → `knowledge/chat_research/2026-10-11_reddit_voice_notes.md`

How sure: **[DATA]** = counted or a real survey. **[VENDOR]** = survey by a company selling to them (biased). **[STORY]** = forum or single case. **[GUESS]** = reasoning, not proven. Nothing here came from an owner's own mouth yet. The first 10 calls fix that.

---

## 1. The three owners in one paragraph each

**Window and door installer.** A man (80%) of about **51**, running a firm that's **11 years old**. Half are one-director firms. In a quarter of all firms a husband/wife or father/son pair runs it [DATA, our list]. He surveys and sells himself, or has one rep [GUESS]. He works with a local fabricator who makes the frames, and he fits them. He's FENSA or Certass registered. He knows the "double-glazing salesman who won't leave" image and markets against it: 12% of window sites say "no pressure" outright [DATA].

**Roofer.** A man (87%) of about **41**. He's the only director in 3 out of 4 firms, and the company is often brand new: **35% were formed since Jan 2025** [DATA]. He's an ex-employee, often from a family roofing firm, who bought a van and ladders and went on his own [STORY]. Average roofing firm: about 3 staff [VENDOR]. Roofers are the least likely of the three to have their own email domain (67%, vs 18% on Gmail) [DATA].

**Kitchen firm.** About **49**, firm 8 years old, more often a showroom with a designer than a man in a van. Most likely of the three to have a woman director (22%) [DATA]. Fitters are often self-employed subcontractors [GUESS]. Kitchen buyers decide the slowest of all: only **6% of kitchen retailers get an order confirmed within a month** of the enquiry [DATA, kbbreview 2026].

**Businessmen or handymen?** Our list is already the "business" end: limited companies with a website. The real handyman end (sole traders, about half of all trades) isn't on it and is legally off-limits to us anyway. Inside the list:
- ~2,100 established firms (5+ years) with 2+ directors → most business-like
- ~2,200 established one-director firms → owner does everything
- ~1,900 under two years old → mostly new roofers with little history

They are tradesmen who became owners, not salesmen who hired tradesmen [GUESS, consistent with everything found].

## 2. How they get work

- **Word of mouth first.** 36% of homeowners found their last trader through someone they know; 27% re-used one they'd hired before [DATA, Citizens Advice 2026, 5,000 people]. Only 9% rank a trader's website in their top 3 reasons [DATA, smaller survey].
- **Lead sites second.** Checkatrade (£30-£300/month, 12-month auto-renew), MyBuilder and Rated People (pay per lead, leads shared with 3-5 trades). Owners resent them [STORY]. Only about 8% of window firms on our list show a Checkatrade badge, and 8% run Google Ads [DATA].
- **Fabricators pass leads** to approved window installers [VENDOR].
- Door-knocking still exists, mostly at big firms with commission reps [STORY].

## 3. How a job happens

Enquiry (phone, web form, lead site) → call back → **home visit / survey** → **quote** → silence or decision → deposit → fit → final payment.

- **The quote is slow.** Only 4% quote the same day and 27% within 24 hours, and 55% admit they've lost a job because the quote was late [VENDOR, 140 tradespeople, mixed trades]. In windows, only ~1 in 4 quote on the spot; most send prices off to the fabricator first [VENDOR].
- **Most quotes don't convert.** Window firms using a CRM win about 40-44% of enquiries, so ~6 in 10 go quiet. Firms without a CRM probably do worse [VENDOR, Business Pilot].
- **Chasing is patchy and personal.** Some phone after 2 days, some after 7-10, some never ("if they need chasing they're not worth it") [STORY].
- **Homeowners say sellers don't listen.** Only 7% say window salespeople dealt "very well" with what mattered to them [VENDOR, Keystone 2026].
- **Rules:** quotes usually valid 30 days. Off-premises contracts get a 14-day cooling-off, but made-to-measure windows don't [DATA, Which?]. Deposits run 10-50%, higher for bespoke [STORY/GUESS]. Finance (Kanda, Improveasy) is offered by some installers.

## 4. Phone, WhatsApp, email, software

- **Phone in the van, admin at night.** 77% do admin in the evening, 5-8 hours a week [VENDOR, two surveys]. They quote after dinner.
- **WhatsApp:** treated as the normal way to send quotes and photos, but **no real survey exists** [GUESS].
- **Email:** 68% of our list uses a shared inbox (info@, office@), read by the owner or the spouse in a 1-2 person firm [DATA + GUESS].
- **Software:**
  - Windows has its own tools: Tommy Trinder (~700 firms), Business Pilot (CRM), fabricator portals. In 2017 about a quarter still quoted on paper [VENDOR].
  - Roofers have no UK-specific tool. Phone photos, WhatsApp, and a Word/PDF quote or an app like Tradify [GUESS].
  - Kitchens use design software (20-20, Planit).
  - Almost nobody runs anything that reminds them to chase [GUESS].
- **They won't learn a new dashboard.** CRM adoption is hard; "do it for them" works better than "give them a tool" [STORY, US].

## 5. Money

| | Typical job | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Windows/doors | £3,700-£4,100 average order; whole house £3.5k-£12k; composite door ~£800-£1,800 | Busy autumn, **December dead, January rebounds** [VENDOR] |
| Roofing | Repair £150-£350; flat roof £1.4k-£3.3k; re-roof £5k-£18k | Storm repairs autumn/winter, re-roofs in dry months |
| Kitchens | Supply and fit £4k-£25k+; fitter £150-£350/day | Long decisions, pre-Christmas rush [GUESS] |

- **Margins:** no reliable number. Roughly 25-45% gross, high single digits to mid-teens net once the owner pays himself [GUESS].
- **Average small-firm turnover** (insurance quote data): roofers ~£60k, glaziers ~£101k, kitchen fitters ~£86k. This skews to the smallest firms [DATA].
- **Cash is tight.** Each is owed ~£4-6k by customers on average, typically paid ~35 days late [VENDOR].
- **Our price has to pay back on one recovered job.**

## 6. What they're like

- **Proud, independent, overworked.** 52% took no week off last year; 93% say they're stressed [VENDOR]. Being busy, the van with their name on it, and family matter to them [GUESS].
- **Burned by sellers.** Lead sites with shared leads and auto-renew, SEO cold callers, "pay on results" agencies. Many refuse to buy from anyone who cold-called them [STORY]. Trades forums ban vendors outright (Reddit pastes).
- **Allergic to hype.** An 18-year owner reads "30 sales in 30 days or £20k back" as a scam [STORY, US]. They mock guru talk and "learn a trade" dream-selling (Reddit pastes).
- **"They're my leads."** Their biggest objection to anyone helping with sales is that they'd have won those jobs anyway [STORY, US].
- **Price-shopping is squeezing them.** Cheap start-ups anchor homeowners low ("get more quotes") (Reddit pastes). A quiet quote is often a customer comparing prices.
- **Reputation is everything.** An awkward chase that annoys a homeowner costs them more than one lost job [GUESS, follows from the word-of-mouth data].
- **They talk plainly.** Short, practical, sceptical of jargon.

## 7. What this changes for us

1. **Send to the established firms first.** Old quotes only exist in firms that have been quoting for years. Put the 4,335 firms with 5+ years and filed accounts first. Push roofers under 2 years old (about a third of roofers) to the back.
2. **Use the owner's first name.** Companies House gives us every director's name. "Hi Dave" to info@ reads like a person; "Hi there" reads like a scraper.
3. **Lead with the pain they admit to:** quotes that went quiet, and quoting at night. Never "more leads". If we quote a number, say where it's from ("a survey of 140 tradespeople").
4. **Sell "done for you", not software.** They won't log in to anything. We do the chasing, in their name and their words. The customer stays theirs. That answers "they're my leads" and sets us apart from Sternfenster/Business Pilot, which need their system.
5. **Make the follow-up we send for them calm.** No fake deadlines, no "today only", stop the moment the homeowner says no. That's the opposite of the double-glazing reputation, and it protects their name.
6. **Small, believable promises.** No revenue guarantees. One small ask.
7. **Timing (to test, not fact):** they read email in the evening, so test sends landing ~5-7pm against morning. Roofers may be easiest to reach on rainy days.
8. **Kitchens may be the best fit of all** (slowest decisions, biggest jobs). It's worth moving them up once the first windows/roofing results are in.
9. **Seasons:** for windows, autumn is good and December is the worst month to start a pilot. Plan around January.

## 8. What only real conversations can tell us

Ask these on every call and log the answers. The call script already covers most:
- Do you quote on the night or send it later? How long after?
- WhatsApp, text or email for quotes? Where do old quotes live?
- Who chases, how many times, and when do you give up?
- How many quotes a month, and roughly how many win?
- What do you pay for leads today?

Also, Pablo: 20 minutes on r/uktrades and r/DIYUK reading "never heard back" / "chasing quotes" threads, pasted here, fills the voice gap our tools can't reach.
