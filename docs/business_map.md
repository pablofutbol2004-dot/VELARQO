# Velarqo business map: idea to £100k/month, solo

Every area we need to be good at, as the questions we must answer. Mark
each one as we answer it, with where the answer lives (usually `truth/`).
Two sides throughout (see CLAUDE.md): **V** = Velarqo winning clients,
**C** = delivering for clients.

## The target, as maths (assumptions to test, not facts)

£100k/month solo means high value per client and low hours per client:
- 20 clients × £5k/month, or
- 40 clients × £2.5k/month, or
- performance: ~650 booked surveys/month × £150.

Each client needs roughly 1-2 hours/week of your time at that scale, so
delivery must be mostly automated. Unknowns that decide which path works:
recovery rate on old quotes, client lifetime, churn, price per result.

## 1. Market and positioning (V)
- Which trades have lots of quotes, low close rates, high job values and old quotes worth chasing? Windows/doors first; then which next (kitchens, roofing, solar, conservatories, driveways, bathrooms, HVAC)?
- How many UK firms per trade are the right size (enough quote volume, owner still decides)?
- Who else sells "old quote reactivation" or appointment-setting to UK trades, what do they charge, and why would a firm pick us?
- What one sentence makes a window firm owner say "yes, that's my problem"?
- Niche down or stay "trades in general"? When to add the second niche?

## 2. The customer: UK installers (V)
- Their real sales process: enquiry → survey → quote → follow-up → close. Typical close rate, deal size, sales cycle.
- Where old quotes live (paper, Excel, CRM, quoting software like Pro-Quote, Business Ecosystem, Easy Job) and how we get the data out.
- What they already spend on leads (Checkatrade, MyBuilder, Bark, Facebook ads, Google), and what a booked survey is worth to them.
- Objections and past burns: "tried agencies", "leads were rubbish", "my data's a mess", "customers will be annoyed".
- Seasonality: busy and quiet months; when they're most open to buying.
- Who decides: owner, office manager, sales manager?

## 3. The offer (V)
- What exactly do we deliver, and what's the result we're paid for (booked survey, attended survey, sale, revenue share)?
- Pricing model: performance only, setup + performance, retainer, retainer + bonus, revenue share. Which gets to £100k fastest with least risk?
- Guarantee and risk reversal that doesn't bankrupt us.
- Free/low-cost first step that proves value (audit of their old quotes, a pilot on 100 quotes).
- How to avoid bad-fit clients (too small, no data, won't follow up leads fast).
- Upsells and ongoing value after the old quotes are used up (new-quote follow-up, reviews, referrals, a second list).

## 4. Getting clients: outbound (V)
- Cold email: copy, sequences, personalisation, subject lines, reply handling.
- Deliverability: domains, inboxes, warm-up, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, volume per inbox, bounce/complaint control, tools (Instantly, Smartlead, etc.).
- Lead data: accuracy, decision-maker names, verification, refresh cadence.
- Other channels: cold calling (UK rules, TPS/CTPS), LinkedIn, letters/direct mail, Loom videos, trade shows, Facebook groups for installers.
- Benchmarks: reply %, positive %, call booked %, close %; what "good" looks like.

## 5. Getting clients: inbound and trust (V)
- Case studies: how to get the first one and how to present it.
- Referrals from clients, suppliers (fabricators, window manufacturers), trade bodies (FENSA, Certass, GGF).
- Partnerships: fabricators/suppliers who sell to hundreds of installers.
- Content / personal brand: is it worth it for a solo operator, where (LinkedIn, YouTube, trade forums)?
- Website and landing page: what an installer needs to see to trust us.

## 6. Sales (V)
- Discovery call structure, qualifying questions, demo/proof, closing.
- Handling price, trust and GDPR objections.
- Proposal, contract and onboarding in one flow; time from "yes" to live campaign.
- Selling in English as a non-UK-based operator: how to handle "where are you based?".

## 7. Delivery: reactivation campaigns (C)
- Campaign design: email vs SMS vs both, message sequences, offers that bring homeowners back, timing.
- Realistic recovery rates by quote age and segment (lost on price, went quiet, no-show).
- Speed to lead: making sure the client responds to replies within minutes; do we handle replies and book surveys ourselves?
- Calendar booking and reminders to cut no-shows.
- What happens when the old list is exhausted: ongoing follow-up of new quotes.

## 8. Delivery: tools and data (C)
- GoHighLevel: sub-accounts, snapshots, workflows, SMS (UK sender IDs, costs), email domains per client.
- Client data import: messy exports, dedupe, cleaning, segmenting (our pipeline).
- Consent/soft opt-in check per client list.
- AI for reply handling and booking; when a human must step in.

## 9. Results, attribution and billing (V + C)
- Proving a booked survey came from us (tracking, calendar source, CRM stage).
- Reporting clients actually read (weekly one-pager: replies, surveys booked, revenue won).
- Billing and collections for performance deals; preventing disputes.
- Metrics we track per client: cost to deliver, margin, time spent.

## 10. Retention and expansion (V + C)
- Why clients churn in this model and how to prevent it.
- Turning a one-off reactivation into a monthly product.
- Referrals between installers; case study from each client.

## 11. Legal and compliance (V + C)
- Spain: autónomo vs SL, tax (IRPF, IVA art 69 for UK clients), VeriFactu, social security.
- UK: PECR/UK GDPR for our outreach; client campaigns to homeowners (consent, soft opt-in); UK representative; ICO fee.
- Contracts: service agreement, performance terms, data processing agreement, liability limits.
- Insurance: professional indemnity / cyber.

## 12. Money (V)
- Unit economics: cost per client acquired, cost to deliver, gross margin, payback time.
- Cash flow on performance deals (we pay costs before we get paid).
- Pricing increases over time; when to raise prices.
- Personal: taxes, how much to reinvest, runway.

## 13. Systems and automation (V)
- What to automate first to stay solo at 20-40 clients: lead sourcing, outreach, onboarding, campaign setup, reporting, billing.
- One dashboard for the whole business: pipeline, clients, results, cash.
- Documentation (SOPs) so a VA or contractor can take pieces later.

## 14. Scaling solo vs hiring (V)
- Which tasks go to software, which to freelancers/VAs, what stays with you (sales, key decisions).
- Productising: same offer, same setup, same report for every client.
- When to add a second niche vs go deeper in windows.

## 15. You (V)
- Weekly operating rhythm: outreach, calls, delivery, review.
- The few numbers you look at every week.
- Focus: what not to do at each stage.
