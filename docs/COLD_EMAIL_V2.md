# Cold email v2: proof by showing, not asking (proposal, 2026-10-10)

Nothing changes in `config/experiments/` until Pablo approves. All four
emails pass `compose.problems()` (73 / 71 / 72 / 38 words with signature
and opt-out).

## Why change email 1

The current email says "I chase them in your name". A stranger with no case
study asking to message your customers is the scariest sentence in the
sequence, and the thing that answers it (the exact text the homeowner gets)
only arrives in follow-up 2, which most people never read. v2 moves the
demonstration into email 1. The A/B stays clean: same opener, same text,
only the offer line differs.

## The sequence (subject for all: `last year's quotes`)

**Email 1, arm A (pay per booked survey)**
```
Hi,

Ever had a homeowner ring back months after a quote, ready to go?

Most don't. So I'd text the ones {company} quoted last year, from your name:

"Hi, it's {company}. We quoted for your windows a while back. Did you ever get that sorted?"

Anyone still keen goes in your diary. You only pay for surveys that get booked.

Worth a go?
```

**Email 1, arm B (first 50 free)**: same, with the offer line
`I'll chase your first 50 free. Anyone still keen goes in your diary.`

**Follow-up 2 (+3 business days), both arms: what you get back + no-data start**
```
Hi,

What you'd get back: a short list of homeowners who replied "yes, still interested", each with a survey time in your diary. Anyone who says no is left alone for good.

No spreadsheet needed to start. Read me five old quotes on a ten-minute call and I'll tell you if they're worth chasing.

Roughly how many have you got: tens, hundreds?
```

**Follow-up 3 (+5 business days)**: unchanged ("Last one from me... who's best to speak to?").

Roofing: swap "your windows" for "your roof". Kitchens later: "your kitchen".

## What a no-case-study first batch can honestly say

- What exactly happens (the text, shown). Done above.
- What they get back (names + diary slots). Done in FU2.
- What it costs them to start: nothing, no spreadsheet, ten minutes. Done.
- **Not** expected numbers. "Typically 2-5% book" would be a vendor claim
  we can't back; one installer asking "says who?" ends the trust. The
  honest version of "expected numbers" is the sizing question
  ("tens, hundreds?") answered by *them*, then on the call: "if one in
  twenty is still interested, that's X surveys from your list."
- Signature: `Pablo <Surname>` once the surname is agreed, then
  `Velarqo, velarqo.com`. The website carries the face, the Spain base
  and the UK number.

## Using the first case study (the moment it exists)

1. Written permission from the installer on day 1 of the pilot, not after.
2. Numbers in one line: "X old quotes, Y replied, Z surveys booked, in N days,
   [trade], [area]". Then it goes, the same week, into: follow-up 2 (replacing
   the first paragraph), the website trade page, the partner emails, your
   LinkedIn, and the reply templates.
3. Ask that installer for two names and one sentence in their own words.
4. Record a 60-second phone video with them if they'll do it.
5. Re-contact everyone who replied "not now" in the previous 90 days with
   the result, as a new angle (the engine's 90-day rule allows it).
6. Only then: the first ad test and the first content post.

## The money, in installer units

| Monthly income | Needs, roughly (guess) |
|---|---|
| €5k (~£4.3k) | 4 pilots a month at ~£1k each, **or** 8 "Chase + Answer" clients at £349 + bookings, **or** a mix: 3 pilots + 4 monthly |
| €10k (~£8.6k) | ~20 monthly clients, **or** 10 monthly + 4 pilots a month |

At 1-2 pilots per 1,000 companies emailed (guess), €5k/month means
converting roughly a third of pilots to monthly and keeping 3-4 new pilots
a month coming: about 2,000-3,000 new companies emailed a month, which is
what 16 inboxes give. So the 16 inboxes and the list growth are sized
right for €5k; €10k needs partners or Ireland on top.
