# Making Velarqo hard to fail

Written 2026-10-10. Built on the repo, the live lead list, a web check of the UK
market (sources at the bottom) and the knowledge-base digest
(`docs/knowledge/kb_digest_2026-10-10_hard_to_fail.md`).
**Guesses are marked (guess).** Replace them with real numbers as soon as the
first batches give us some.

## The honest answer first

Nothing makes the first email batch certain. Nobody can promise that.

What you *can* make close to certain:

1. **You find out fast and cheaply** whether the offer works.
2. **You never run out of shots.** You keep enough market, money and
   morale to try again with a better offer.
3. **If it works, it can grow** into something worth being rich from.

First businesses rarely die because the idea was wrong. They die because the
founder runs out of shots: the list gets burned, the money runs out, or they
quit after the first quiet batch. Everything below protects those three things.

## The numbers, plainly

**The list (live database, 10 Oct):** 6,450 emailable Ltd companies:
2,804 window/door and 3,646 roofing. Most are small: 2,184 file micro-entity
accounts and 1,597 haven't filed accounts yet.

**The wider market:** about 12,000 UK window/door installation firms (Insight
Data via Glass Times), about 12,000 roofing businesses (IBISWorld), and 5,000+
MCS solar/heat-pump installers. The database already holds 45,633 window/door
companies. We just don't have emails for most of them yet.

**From emails to clients, per 1,000 companies that get the full sequence (guess):**

| | Low | Middle | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positive replies | 5 | 15 | 30 |
| Calls | 2-3 | 7-8 | 15 |
| Samples of old quotes sent | 1 | 3 | 6 |
| Pilots | 0-1 | 1-2 | 3 |
| **Pilots from the whole 6,450 list** | **~3** | **~10** | **~20** |

**What one client is worth on today's offer (guess):** a firm that sends 40
quotes a month has roughly 300-450 usable old quotes from the last two years.
At 2-5% booked and £75-150 per survey, that's **about £500-3,000, once**.
After that, the new quotes going quiet each month earn only £100-300 a month.

**What that means:** the first campaign buys you **proof and your first
money, not wealth**. Wealth needs three more things:

1. **A monthly product.** Every new quote gets chased automatically from day 3,
   not month 6, plus fast replies to new enquiries and review requests. Charge
   a monthly fee plus a fee per booking (to test: roughly £300-750 a month).
   Competitors already do this: doubleglazingleads.co.uk sells "quote chasing"
   as an add-on. The knowledge base says the same: database reactivation
   (DBR) is the foot in the door, and the money comes from the ongoing
   service (Wardrope, JP Middleton).
2. **A bigger market:** all 12k window/door firms, plus solar/heat pumps,
   kitchens, bathrooms and conservatories.
3. **A second way to find clients:** partners who already sell to hundreds of
   installers.

**Rough path (guess):** 10 pilots, of which 6 stay on monthly at £500, gives
£3k a month. Getting to £10k a month needs about 20 monthly clients. That's
**6-12 months of steady work**, not 6 weeks. It's doable, but only if you're
still going in month 7.

## How it fails, and the counter to each

| # | Failure | Likely? | Counter (in your hands) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **You stop** after a quiet first batch, or switch ideas | High (it's the most common way) | Rules written now (see below). Judge on thousands of sends, not hundreds |
| 2 | **Installers don't trust a stranger** ("Pablo", no surname, no face, no phone) | High | A trust pack before 25 Oct (A1-A3) |
| 3 | **The list gets burned** with a weak first offer | Medium-high: 16 mailboxes contact all 6,450 in about 10 weeks | Send in slices (C1), use the extra mailboxes for new trades (C2) |
| 4 | **A yes stalls** in paperwork, slow replies or slow setup | Medium | Prepare it now (E1-E4) |
| 5 | **The pilot books little:** bad data, Checkatrade/Bark leads they can't legally message, or the installer can't take the surveys | Medium | Sample audit first, a weekly booking cap in the contract, the right cohort (quoted, not bought, 3-24 months old) |
| 6 | **Each client is worth too little** | High on the current offer | The monthly product, designed now (D1) |
| 7 | **Emails land in spam** | Medium | Already handled: warm-up, plain text, bounce/complaint kill switch. Add seed tests (B3) |
| 8 | **Money runs out** before it works | Depends on you | A runway check (G2) |

## Everything you can do, in order

### A. Trust: before the first send (25 Oct)
1. **Full name, a real photo and a UK phone number** (incoming calls only) on
   the website and in every email signature. A sceptical fitter googles you
   first. A UK virtual number costs about £5 a month; agree the cost first.
2. **Say plainly that you're based in Spain and work UK hours.** If they find
   out later, it looks like you were hiding it.
3. **A 60-second phone video of you** explaining the offer, on the website
   only (links in cold emails hurt delivery). A matching LinkedIn profile.

### B. Offer and copy
1. Keep the frame **"surveys back in the diary from quotes you'd written
   off"**, never "we'll message your old leads". The second reads as taking
   money they already have (Fazio).
2. Add a **speed promise:** "first homeowners contacted within 7 days of
   getting the list". Speed is the weakest of the four offer levers right now.
3. **Seed test before every new mailbox goes live:** check that a test email
   lands in Gmail and Outlook inboxes, not spam.
4. Later (week 3+): a **mini-estimate arm** for the 50 best-fit firms, e.g.
   "firms your size usually have 300-500 quotes that went quiet". This means
   doing the work for them up front.

### C. Protect the market
1. **Slices of about 500 companies per offer/angle.** No verdict before that
   number, and no new touch on the same company within 90 days. When you do
   touch them again, use a different angle.
2. **The 12 new mailboxes open new trades; they don't finish windows faster.**
   Start list-building now:
   - more emails for the 45k window/door companies already in the database
     (the cheapest win);
   - solar/heat pumps (the MCS register is public);
   - kitchens, bathrooms and conservatories.
   Target: 15,000+ emailable Ltds by January.
3. Track the size of the untouched list every week. If it drops below
   8 weeks of sending, list-building jumps to the top of the to-do list.

### D. Make each client worth more
1. **Design the monthly product before the first pilot,** and agree the
   continuation in the pilot terms. "Free first 50" already requires this.
2. Bill per **attended** survey once no-show data exists. It's fairer, and
   it's harder to dispute.
3. Later upsells, sold only to happy clients: missed-call text-back, review
   requests, fast reply to new enquiries.

### E. Be ready for the first yes (do it before it comes)
1. **Full dry run with made-up data:** sample audit → messages → booking →
   weekly report → invoice. The first client should hear "we start in 7
   days", not "give me 3 weeks".
2. **Paperwork prepared, not done:**
   - Book a gestor consultation now, so the autónomo registration happens the
     week the first pilot is agreed.
   - Open a free Wise GBP account now.
   - Get a fixed-price quote now for a UK lawyer to review the pilot agreement
     and data processing agreement (DPA).
3. **Pilot contract** sets out:
   - what counts as a booked or attended survey;
   - no-shows;
   - 90-day attribution;
   - quotes already active are excluded;
   - **a weekly cap on bookings the installer can handle**.
4. **Positive replies ping your phone.** Answer within an hour, 08:30-17:00
   UK time. Set up the free booking page (already on your to-do list).
   Record 3 mock calls before the first real one.

### F. A second channel (30 minutes a day, max)
1. **Partners who already sell to installers:** trade window fabricators
   (Eurocell alone claims 40,000+ trade accounts), glazing CRM/quoting
   software, roofing merchants. Offer: "your installers win more jobs from
   quotes they already sent; I pay £100 for each one who signs." Email 20
   partners. One yes is worth months of cold email.
2. **UK installer Facebook groups:** be a useful member, don't pitch.
3. Later: a letter to the 20 best-fit firms (Alex M H Smith's idea), and
   trade press (Glass Times). The next big trade show is May 2027 (FIT Show).

### G. You (the biggest lever, said kindly)
1. **The rules below, written now while you're calm.**
2. **Runway:** know your monthly burn (about €170 in tools, plus about
   €88/month once you register as autónomo). Make sure you can carry it for
   **9 months with zero income.** That single fact turns "another failure"
   into "a bet I can afford to repeat".
3. **Sell ≥ 50% of your hours until the first pilot.** No new research
   videos or new systems until then. The knowledge base is already big
   enough; more reading now is the comfortable kind of busy.
4. **The first goal isn't money. It's "a stranger sends me their old
   quotes" by 30 Nov.** Money follows that step. Measure the step.
5. A daily scorecard: sent / replies / positive / calls / samples / pilots.

## Rules, decided in advance

- **No verdict on an offer or angle before 500 companies** have had the full
  sequence, *and* the seed test shows we land in the inbox.
- **0-1 positive replies after 500:** change the offer or the trade, not the
  commas. **2+:** keep it and run it again on a new slice.
- **No verdict on the business before 2,000 companies and 2 different
  offers.** Even then, a bad result means changing the offer, the trade or
  the channel, not quitting.
- **Replies but no calls:** the email over-promises or the reply is too slow.
  Fix the reply, not the email.
- **Calls but no samples:** trust or data fear. Lead with the "names blanked
  out" sample and the face/phone.
- **A pilot that books little:** check the cohort and data before blaming
  the offer. One pilot is one data point.
- **Review on Fridays only.** Mid-week panic gets written in `parking.md`, not
  acted on.
